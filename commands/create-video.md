# /create-video — STATIC ONLY · YOUTUBE LONG-FORM

Input: `$ARGUMENTS`. This is a **normal Claude Code command**, not a Claude workflow, automation, or multiple-mode selector. It has exactly one production pipeline: reference research → choose a niche → invent and research an original long-form story → fully narrated image-led video → CapCut project/export. Never ask which mode to run. Never load or invoke legacy phases, generic video-editing flows, browser inspection services, or video-to-video generation services. No external monitoring app is used.

Verify real files rather than trusting conversation memory. Continue the earliest incomplete stage; **never re-ask a choice already present in the run**. This works across `/compact` or a new chat.
2. If it starts with `more`, use the existing `RUN` and its locked niche. Each new video gets its own creative direction and narrator choice; keeping the run's look and voice for a series identity is a choice made and recorded there (`voice-pick` with a reason), not an automatic carry-over. Ask only for a count when absent, then use new video IDs, new original topics and new duration locks. First `scan` for newly added references; prepare, measure and analyse only the new ones (`analysis-plan` plans only references without a finished analysis), then bring the niche map up to date (`niche-evidence`, the map, `niche-check`) and `niche-lock --refresh --why "..."` when the locked niche gains members. Never create a new run for more videos.
3. If it starts with `clone`, run `static_core.py clone --from-run "RUN"`; keep evidence/cards and the niche map (the clone picks and locks its own niche), start an independent new niche and new production. The old run remains untouched.
4. Otherwise, the **first requirement is a path to `references/`**. If `$ARGUMENTS` contains a path, use it; if not, ask exactly for that folder path. `static_core.py doctor`, then `static_core.py init --references "PATH" --slug "youtube"`, then `scan`. A GPU failure stops with its cause; it never falls back to the CPU, which runs only on the user's explicit yes (`--device cpu --cpu-approved "<their words>"`). If files are corrupt/missing, report and skip those files explicitly; never silently treat missing speech as silence.

## Core execution contract

**A. Watch, listen, classify, understand.** Analyze each unique video as a cohesive whole: original audio/Whisper word-timestamps, 2 fps sampled visual coverage (a frame every half second, each at its own timestamp on the transcript's clock; a run keeps the rate it was analysed at), closer looks at a higher rate for brief or ambiguous stretches (`inspect-window`), transcript, story architecture, scene progression, emotional movement, narrator character, pacing, music, retention hypotheses, title/thumbnail promise. Run `av-profile --run "RUN" --refs ...` and read each reference's **watch sheets** (one image per minute: frames every 5 s with the words spoken under them) beside its measurements (pace and its swings, how the voice moves, the bed under it, cuts, holds, motion): it is how an agent watches a film. Reason about what makes each one *enjoyable*, not only what it contains (10 §1). **Each reference is analysed on its own, and the work is split by reading work, never by subject** (01, "Splitting the analysis work"): after `av-profile` and `content-review --ref V### --shot-sheets` for every reference, `analysis-plan --run "RUN"` makes the batches from sheets, words and minutes alone (`--agents N` when you have a reason) and writes one brief and one to-do file per agent; spawn one analysis agent per batch, all at once, each given only its `spawn_prompt`. The number of analysis agents follows the workload: it never anticipates, limits or shapes how many niches there will be, and a batch is not a group, a cohort or a niche. Never group references by title, channel or first impression before they are analysed. Each agent creates `20-analysis/cards/V###.json` (with `moments_that_matter`: timed, what the reference does to make a development matter, observed apart from your interpretation; topic discovery reads them; each dimension in its own field: `niches` the subject, `story_form` the format, `viewer_reward`, `visual_medium`, the narration; no `cohort_id`) and `20-analysis/V###-notes.md`, and the reference's **content review** (01, `content-review --ref V### --shot-sheets`, then `--file`: every shot seen once, every decision proportionate and grounded in its frames, words and times): its graphic or harmful segments and, kept apart from those, its religious, superstitious or holiday material, each with its timestamps, role in the story, what depends on it and what is done with it. A segment is excluded selectively; the rest of the reference stays useful. **Then discover the niches from the finished analyses** (01, "Discovering the niches"): `niche-evidence --run "RUN"` lays every reference out with each dimension apart (subject, format, viewer reward, look, narration, runtime and pace, content), in reference order, with no batch or earlier grouping shown; read it whole, open the cards you need, and write `20-analysis/niche-map.json`: as many niches as the evidence holds (one, a few or many), each defined by what its audience comes for, with its members (core, or overlap where a reference belongs to two), the formats inside it, its comparable cohorts for a length and timed evidence for what makes it engaging; an outlier stays an outlier, and a format, look or voice several niches share is recorded as shared, never made a niche by itself. Then `20-analysis/niche-synthesis.md` from the map, a `20-analysis/creative-profiles/[niche-slug].md` per niche (editorial implications for making a new video in that direction; see §02), and `niche-check --run "RUN"` (form and traceability only; it also reports how the niches fall across the analysis batches: re-read any niche that is exactly one batch). Explicitly distinguish verified observation, plausible explanation and unknown metrics; never average unrelated formats. When a card is weak, inspect the actual reference again.

**B. Ask one intentional choice: niche.** Present the niches of the checked map, each with what it is about, one short indication of what *makes it engaging* (from its timed evidence), the formats inside it and its Standard lengths (`niche-check` → `facts.cohorts`: each comparable cohort's IDs, durations and median, and whether `length` would accept it). The user picks a niche (or explicitly delegates that choice), and may go further: a subject inside it, an exact topic, or an exact story or concept. Lock the pick before anything else rests on it: `niche-lock --run "RUN" --niche <id> --user-words "<their words>"` (a delegated pick: `--decided-by director --why "..."`); one niche per run, another niche is a clone. Their own words are the scope (`subject.scope`), which you restate (`understood_as`, `boundary`) and keep. Inside a niche or a subject, **you find the subject, premise, angle, chapters and execution autonomously**, by research (D); an exact topic or story is developed, never replaced, and a limit it has (too little material for the length) is reported through the planning, never solved by switching subject or niche. Do not ask users to choose one of your titles or scripts by default. Prompt only when their actual choice is necessary: custom vs comparable-reference Standard length, count (default one), language, narrator, a material cost authorization, and the **captions**, asked once in the same start dialog with no default: **Luckiest Guy — Word Box** (white capitals, the spoken word on a rounded colour box), **Montserrat Bold — White Text / Yellow Active Word** (the spoken word yellow, the rest white, black outline, no box) or **No Captions**; record it with `captions-choice --run "RUN" --captions on --style luckiest-guy|montserrat-highlight` (or `--captions off`). Persist those choices; no repeating. A run made before this question existed is asked once, when its edit begins. No image engine is asked about: the stills are made with FLUX 3 Image Klein int8 on this PC unless the user explicitly chooses another (E).

**C. Lock long-form duration BEFORE script, speech, images or CapCut.** Standard means median of **at least two user-visible, comparable, long-form reference IDs of one comparable cohort of the locked niche** (same niche, comparable story form and runtime family: the niche map's `why_comparable` says why their lengths compare); use `length --mode standard --cohort-id <cohort> --refs ...` after showing IDs/durations. A Short cannot set the length for a history narration. For custom length use `--mode own --minutes ...` (its references from the locked niche). Defaults: YouTube **16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps**, with a user-adjustable long-form minimum; no vertical templates. The lock is immutable without an explicitly authorized length revision (`length --revise "why" --user-approved "<their own words>"`, the old lock kept in its history); don't silently shrink it to match generated audio or sparse visuals, and never pad a script to reach it: a script whose honest length misses the lock goes to the user with the evidence (03).

**D. Make a genuine new story, not a reference remix.** Read stages 02–03. **Find the subject first** (02, "Finding the subject"): within the user's scope, research candidate subjects or angles from the references' `moments_that_matter` and independent sources (survey the scope before committing to the idea you started with, and read each finalist's strongest source before comparing), read before anything rests on them (the ledger `30-research/video-NN/sources.md`, each source's `Access` recorded; claims.json, each claim's confidence), and record in `direction.json` → `subject` what each offers this audience (what happens, why it matters, its reward, its strongest material with how sure the film may be of it), whether its material can carry the locked length (a progression of what the viewer newly gets and your verdict, never a share of minutes), which one you choose, on which material, and why not each of the others (in words, never a score), and the value the film must carry (`value_to_carry`). `direction-lock` refuses a direction without it, a choice resting on unread, disputed or unconfirmed evidence, reasons two candidates share, an exact topic or story replaced, another niche than the locked one, or material judged short for the length without re-selection or the user's decision. Then, for the chosen subject, explore the range of ways it could be told and write as many *different editorial approaches* as it genuinely allows, at least two, with no fixed number (between them they differ on at least three of perspective, scale, structure, voice and visualization, not only in subject, and one challenges the reference formula; five battles told the same way are one approach; `chosen_approach.exploration` says what range was considered and why these approaches cover it); select on substance, credible evidence, distinctive viewpoint, human relevance, an earned ending and compatibility with runtime. Study source documents for factual topics independent of reference videos and keep the claim/source ledger (each claim tied to the script lines that say it, `line_refs`; a contested, speculative or assumed value named where the script says so, the beat's `hedges`); separate fictional hypothesis from fact.

**Creative direction comes first** (read 10; it is the director's manual). In `40-plans/video-NN/direction.json`: the measured reference evidence, the approaches and the chosen one with its runner-up, the episode strategy, and **two to four audiovisual treatments** (narrator, writing voice, pacing, visual language, sound, how picture and voice relate) each written out in three sample moments (the opening, a quiet middle, and where its value lands hardest: `turning_point`, or `strongest_moment` when the video has no turn to invent) and in its `movements` (how the whole video unfolds: what the viewer gets in each, roughly how long, and where it delivers what the opening promises; the order in which the viewer should learn, not automatically the order of events; 10 §2). Then one blind read of the treatments (`review-packet --kind treatment`: the treatments, how each unfolds, the video's promise and the standing house rules, never your promise marks, the chosen approach's pitch or the reference scenes the content reviews cut; one fresh agent, `review-record`, every finding answered), your decision with reasons (with `promise_and_delivery`, and `length_fit`: how its telling compares with the format, density and narration of the references the length and pace came from; a cohort shares the niche, not necessarily the format, and a mismatch is explained or taken to the user as a length decision, never solved by silently changing the lock or by filler; and `pacing`: the reference narrators this narration follows, each weighed with its reason (narrator and delivery, format, density and terminology, sentences, emotional course, variation, pace while speaking against overall, pauses), where in their range it belongs, and its uncertainty when only one matches: never one convenient reference, an average or an earlier production's voice; 03, 10 §3), and `direction-lock`, **before any script exists**. A direction locked before 2026-11-24 (its lock has no `planning_rules` stamp) keeps its plan: the planning rules added that day reach it only as advisory warnings, and a lock stamped 2 sees the storytelling rules of rules 3 the same way, while safety and content, money, asset integrity and the export checks hold for every run (10 §3). A later change re-locks with `--revise`; its answer lists what the change touched (`outdated_now`), and each piece is updated or kept on record with `direction-reapprove` before the stage that uses it continues (10 §3). Each change reaches only what it changes: another subject, everything; a new angle on it, the beat map, script and shot plan; a researched value, only the beats that carry it, their lines and their shots; a corrected source or claim, a re-check where it is used (`direction-lock --research-rechecked`), nothing rebuilt; a beat changed after the shot plan's check, only its own shots (`shot-check` names them; `--keep "why"` when they still fit). Nothing here is a niche formula: two videos in one niche may need opposite treatments.

Then, from the locked treatment: `blueprint.json` (an `ending_payoff` where the video has one, or `reward_shape`: how its value builds up), `beat-map.json` (each beat with its `experience`, `narration_direction`, `visual_intent`, `sound_direction` and `pace`; each `key_moment` with its `significance`: why it matters to this viewer, its reward, and what they must know first, given earlier through `depends_on` or `self_contained`; each researched value in `carries` of the beat that delivers it, after what it `needs_first`, or dropped in `subject.dropped` with the reason; key moments where the video turns or pays off, and none invented where its value builds step by step; manual 10 §4), `script.json`, `editorial-review.md` (your own self-audit, with `writing_signals`), plus human-readable outline and title/thumbnail options. Each beat must move the story forward and be mapped to narration lines and a planned visual purpose. Do not pad or speed-read. **How the narration sounds and breathes is decided here, not at the audio stage.** It flows from the treatment and each beat's direction, read against the references' measured delivery.
- Each beat's `narration_direction` decides the performance for the writer: the narrator's attitude, the rhythm, whether the words alone carry it (they usually should). More expressive is not better (10 §4).
- The narration is planned as a performance before anything is paid for (05, "Narration as a performance"): at each important moment, how the line lands (`pause_landing`), whether a silence is needed and what it gives, what is seen and heard during it, and how the next line starts again (`pause_resume`). A delivery or pause cue is used wherever it gets a moment directly, with its scope stated (`cue_scope`: a cue colours the rest of its own line); the beat map marks `chapter_start`, so recording parts break only where the story starts a new chapter and a technical boundary never makes the narrator conclude or restart. Cue effects and the length forecast are bands from evidence (this voice's own `cue_observations` first), never one number per tag.
- Inline audio tags come from the whole catalogue (`STUDIO/data/elevenlabs-audio-tags.json`, read through 05). Every narration uses purposeful delivery and/or pause cues where the story calls for them (no quota, never decoration, never a fix for unclear writing); a script with none needs `tags_exception` with the user's approval (plan-check). Each kept tag gets its line under "Delivery and tags" in `editorial-review.md` (plan-check refuses one without).
- Silence is a tool, not an ingredient (03, 10 §5): the narrator's own rhythm comes first, and a held silence is planned only where the viewer needs time the voice does not give. A planned pause names the WHOLE silence wanted (`pause_total_s`, the voice's own gap included), how it is realised (`pause_via`: a tag or an edit hold, never both) and what it is for, what is seen and what is heard while it lasts (`pause_purpose`, `pause_picture`, `pause_sound`). Its length is a proposal: no quota, no formula, no length copied from a reference. Silence never makes the length (plan-check refuses it); the cold reader is shown every silence as the viewer would hear it.
- `text` holds only the spoken words and their tags: names the voice may say wrong go in `pronunciation.json`, team notes in a line's `note` (05).
- Each `content_flags` hit gets its line in `editorial-review.md`.

Then the **cold read**: `review-packet --kind script` (title, words and silences only, in a folder outside the run), then `review-read --kind script` (the reader as its own process confined to that folder; or ONE fresh agent of type `static-cold-reader` whose whole prompt is to read only that packet and follow it: Read and Write only and its packet outside the run, but not sandboxed, so `review-record` records the route, `reader_route`, and warns on it), `review-record`, and an answer to every finding (revised with its lines, kept if minor with why, disputed with why). The reader also names the developments that matter and, for each, what happened, why it matters from the words alone, what would be different without it and when the words gave its reason; `review-record` turns a gap into a finding (manual 06), so a reader who supplies the reason from their own knowledge, or gets it only afterwards on a key moment, still flags it. The script reader also says what the opening promised, where it first got it and which waits built anticipation or only delayed (a delay is a finding, a promise never delivered a major one; no minute makes a wait right or wrong). A major finding means revising and a fresh round-2 read by a new agent; a change to a line the reader's verdict rested on (a key moment, its setup or hedge, a value, a claim, a comprehension gap) needs a fresh read too, however few its words, unless `revision_notes` in the answers file says its meaning is unchanged; after round 2 you decide, on record. A major problem an earlier reader also found was not fixed by the earlier revision: its answer names the root cause (`recurrence`: wording, order, structure, treatment, material, duration or a dispute; 06), rewording is refused, and a structural or treatment cause is answered by revising the direction (`direction-lock --revise`) and rebuilding the script from it, a duration cause by the user's length decision, followed by a fresh read blind to the earlier findings and versions; a higher score never closes a major. Find the cause of a dull or confusing passage before changing it (10 §4, "Boredom at its source"): never effects, speed or more narration by default, never a picture to rescue a relation the words never make, never a cause, a prediction or a certainty the evidence does not give, never a number the research does not hold. A harmless format slip in a reader's file (two ranges in one string, two treatments in one finding) is put right by `review-record` with the reader's words untouched and the original kept; it is never a reason for a new read. Never write or soften a reviewer's findings. Run `plan-check --run "RUN" --video NN`; **do not buy TTS or pictures if it fails**. Fix the *story*, not just the numbers.

**E. Narration and production.** Read stages 04–05. Choose this video's narrator for its locked treatment, on quality (personality, charisma, naturalness, emotional range, clarity), never to fit the locked length or a planning pace: its real pace is measured on real parts, and a real mismatch with the length is diagnosed (voice, density, delivery or length) and taken to the user, never re-locked automatically (03) (`voice-candidates`: five candidates described by character, register, age and accent; each preview's delivery is measured, because no agent here can listen; a preview's pace does not predict the real pace; Voice Design is priced first and billed only with `--execute`; the shortlist goes to the user with its listening files, and the narrator is locked on their listening choice, never on previews, pitch numbers or a pace alone). `voice-pick --settings '{"stability":S,"similarity_boost":M}' --reason "..."` locks it for the video: voice, model and both settings, chosen for the narrator (stability is never a pace control: Eleven v4 has no speed setting). Videos of a series may share a voice; a change for a video is `voice-pick --replace` with a reason. Only `eleven_v4` / `eleven_v4_turbo` are used. When the voice, a setting, the tags or a name are in doubt, propose a short `narration-trial` (each question changes one thing; priced and shown to the user, recorded only after their approval, one take at a time inside the credit cap they authorised; the user gets the blind listening set, and no production setting changes until they have listened). ElevenLabs generations are paid, so the order is:
1. `tts ...` without `--execute`: the parts (they follow the beats), the characters and the credit forecast (`--read-rates` reads the model's credit rate for it, a free call). The voice, model and settings come from the video's voice lock; a different value on the command line is refused.
2. The first paid recording is a representative calibration, the smallest that shows the whole performance the script asks for (an explanatory and an expressive passage, the hardest names, the tags, a transition into a silence; the user, 2026-11-25): part 1 when it holds that (`tts ... --max-parts 1 --execute`, paid once and kept), else first a short `narration-trial` of those passages (the voice lock as the one set-up, one take a passage, priced and approved; evidence and listening, not production audio), then part 1. `tts` reads the model's credits per character and the live balance first, and puts each request on the ledger before it is sent and again with its outcome and what it really cost (`logs/elevenlabs-ledger.jsonl`). Until the voice is measured the tool records only the parts it takes to reach a measurable sample (300 words); `--skip-calibration` needs `--reason`, kept on record. A paid request is never billed twice: one with no known outcome (cut off, timed out, a server error) is taken from the account history when ElevenLabs finished it, else nothing is sent until the user has checked the account usage and `--retry-unreconciled ATTEMPT_ID --reason "..."` allows exactly that retry (05).
3. `narration-measure`: the real pace (while speaking and overall), line gap, tag costs, and pauses next to the references', measured exactly as the references were; every line heard (a missed line is re-heard on its own window; still missing, it blocks until re-recorded or confirmed by the user's ear), no tag read aloud, the joins; and `delivery.json`, how the voice moves beat by beat next to what each beat asked for. A beat meant to lift that measures flat is rewritten in the parts not yet recorded.
4. `plan-check`: `real_forecast_s` against the export window. The planning pace is a forecast, never a target: when the real narration and the locked length disagree, diagnose (voice, density, delivery or length) and take the evidence to the user for a controlled decision (the length reapproved, a substantive change to words not yet recorded, or another narrator). Never force the delivery: no tag, setting or audio edit for pace, no words cut or padded only to fit; a hold may be shortened when its moment allows, never lengthened to reach the window. Then `holds`: each planned pause next to the gap the voice really left there (from the audio); listening previews for the user where a length is in doubt (`holds --preview Lnnn --totals a,b`); and `hold-decide --stage narration` for every pause whose lines are recorded (the whole silence, or `--no-hold`, and why), with its transition: how the voice goes into the silence and out of it (`clean` by default; a fade only on audible room tone, never on the voice) and what the bed does there, judged from how the line lands and the next begins (`holds`: `ending`), never applied by default.
5. The rest of the parts.

Continue only after the account, voice, model and charges are accepted. An edited line later re-records only the part that holds it (`--rerecord-changed`), and so does a part whose pronunciation or voice setup changed, or that was recorded before a direction change to the narrator, unless the narration is kept on record (`direction-reapprove --artifact narration`); a part to redo after listening is `--rerecord-parts N --reason`. **Partial, exhausted, missing or short narration is a BLOCKER.** `audio-check` must pass before stills and edit; it includes one voice setup throughout, every line heard, and narration plus planned holds inside the export window. Preserve partial chunks for resuming; never cut chapters to make a failed recording look done. Create an image style bible from the locked treatment's visual language, then lock the look every still carries (`style-lock --run "RUN" --video NN --style-prompt "<style words>" --departs-when "<what would be another look>" [--palette ...] [--ref ...]`: each still's prompt is its shot's approved `frame_prompt` plus words that keep the locked look (the style words in any order, or a `style_note` saying how), and words reaching for a look the lock names as another mean relocking or a `style_departure`; the stills gate refuses a missing or outdated lock; a change is a new revision with `--why`; manual 04), and a shot plan from each beat's `visual_intent`, every shot saying what NEW thing its picture gives (`new_thing`), and each key moment with at least one shot saying how its picture carries why the moment matters (`carries_significance`: a face, a contrast, a map, a held still; never a forced diagram); `shot-check` before any still is generated (it also lists near-identical prompts and long runs of one composition); generate stills with the run's image engine: FLUX 3 Image Klein int8 on this PC, the default and primary one (`STUDIO/scripts/image_worker.py`, run with `C:\Users\Bulzart\Documents\AI\tools\flux3_python.cmd`, ComfyUI's embedded Python inside the memory guard; a job cannot swap its model files or precision; each still's provenance records what was really drawn: manual 04); OpenAI Images (`openai_images_worker.py`) only on the user's explicit choice, recorded with `static_core.py image-engine --run "RUN" --engine openai --why "<the user's words>"` (`--engine flux-klein` goes back), and never as a fallback: when FLUX fails, report the actual error and stop. A caption the check stops is drawn only when you accept exactly that caption (`accept_caption`: its sha256 and what the stopped words mean) or draw the approved prompt itself (`on_caption_refused`); a still with a `people_question` answers in its look who, if anyone, appears (`"people"`), and someone its prompt does not ask for makes the look an issue: the still is remade, never patched. Both workers refuse a job until the direction, beat map and shot plan pass; each still names its shot and carries that shot's approved `frame_prompt`, and `media-check` gives the same answer free, with the direction each still carries out. Or prepare prompts for user-supplied stills (accepted on record with `direction-reapprove --artifact stills`). Add charts, maps, time lines, restrained illustration animation and on-screen text **when they improve comprehension**; do not turn every shot into a wallpaper slide or a frenetic montage. Optional instrumental music (the gated music worker: each track names the beats it scores) and sound design support the scene rather than bury it.

**F. CapCut and actual export.** First `narration-measure` on the complete narration, then `retime-shots`: every shot is timed to the real narration and its DECIDED holds (each adds only the difference between the whole silence decided and the voice's measured gap, inside that silence, verified in the file; it refuses while a planned pause is undecided, and seam holds restore the voice's gap at a join that holds no pause); with the stills made, each held silence is decided again with its picture (`hold-decide --stage picture`, retimed if its length changed), `edit-plan.json` names the one narration file the project carries (`narration_for_edit`, with its hash), and the edit plan carries the treatment's look and sound and each shot's beat direction, which the moves, joins and bed carry out (09). The project uses those times, never the plan's estimates, and that narration at 0 s, at speed 1, untrimmed. Use connected CapCut MCP/CLI as available to create a *real editable project*, no unsupported invented operations. On this PC one command writes it: `capcut_tools.py capcut-build --run "RUN" --video NN --draft "<project>"` (09: after `capcut-name`, the clone and `capcut-prep`; `--dry-run` first), from the run's decided files only; the MCP's one-call-at-a-time tools are its fallback. Then the captions the user chose (05, 09): `captions-plan` (the script's words timed by the narration's own Whisper words, on the edit's timeline; the word box needs `--box-color` from this video's palette with `--box-color-why`), and, with CapCut closed, `captions-build --draft "<project>"`, then `capcut_tools.py capcut-repair --keep-text`; with captions off, no caption is built. ## Non-negotiables

- One purpose only: **new long-form YouTube videos built from narrated still images/graphics**. No other generation or editing modes, no unrelated prompts/assets, no reuse of source-video images or speech as final footage.
- Ref clips are **evidence about craft**, not license to reproduce another creator's words, distinctive footage, visual identity or unsupported factual claims. Preserve provenance; independent research for facts.
- Source and final frame timing matter. 2 fps (a frame every half second) is a broad coverage rate, **not literally every native frame**: a shot shorter than about a second, a flash or a quick insert can fall between samples. For a brief event, an exact transition or a possibly graphic moment, look closer at that stretch only (`inspect-window --ref V### --from mm:ss --to mm:ss`, a higher rate or `--native`); record the difference rather than falsely claiming every frame was viewed.
- Content: nothing a content review excluded is cited, adapted or brought back in other words or pictures (the direction, the script's content flags, the shot prompts and every generation job are checked against it), the final reviewer reads the export's shot sheets (every shot once) beside its watch sheets, and every picture the edit shows is looked at whole and recorded (`content-review --video NN --pictures --file looks.json`) before `timeline-check` passes. A war, a crime, an accident or a death can be the subject: the facts are told plainly and seriously, never in graphic form (02).
- Pictures: **nothing in a still is ever blurred, erased or covered** (the user, 2026-11-23); what would need hiding is kept out by the frame prompt and the still is made again; a picture the user supplies or a legitimate edit comes in recorded (`still_touchup.py --replace`, manual 04).
- Meaning: a viewer must be able to follow what happened and feel why the important developments matter, from the video alone. Key moments state their significance and what the viewer must know first, before the script; the readers keep what happened, why it matters and whether it paid apart; a gap is fixed at its cause (manual 10 §4, 06).
- The author is responsible for decisions. Machine checks measure objective properties, not taste or truth. No hard formula for hooks, number of chapters, humor frequency, zoom every few seconds or cliffhangers.
- Keep a progressing `RUN/todo.md`; use files as durable handoffs. Resume from what exists. Never run a Claude workflow. Never spend credits on a failed preflight. Final product is an actual CapCut project and its exported, verified video.
