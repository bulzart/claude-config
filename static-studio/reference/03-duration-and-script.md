# 03 — Runtime promise, architecture and narration script

## The length lock

A video's length is fixed in **`length-lock.json`** before a script is checked or narration is paid for, in one of three ways. The user chooses; recommend the first.

**The story's own length** (`--mode story`). The subject says how long it needs (`runtime.natural_minutes`, 02), each treatment says how long its telling runs (its `movements`, 10 §2), and the length is the chosen treatment's total. `length --mode story` run before a treatment is chosen only records the user's choice (`length-intent.json`). Run again after the treatment decision, it locks that treatment's movements added up, rounded to five seconds, with the pace of the narrators `decision.pacing` marks relevant (or `--refs`: any analysed reference). A total outside the subject's own range means one of the two judgements is wrong: revise it, or say why this length is right (`--why`). A story smaller than the run's long-form minimum is another candidate or angle, or a shorter film taken to the user, never padding. Tell the user the figure before any paid narration. The written script measures the story better than any plan: when the first whole draft shows another size, re-lock to it before narration is recorded (`length --mode story --minutes N --revise "why"`; the earlier lock stays in `history`, with `revised_by`). Once narration exists, a new length is the user's decision like any other.

**Standard and own minutes** are the user's figure, locked before the subject is found. Standard is derived from the IDs of one comparable cohort of the locked niche (the niche map's `comparable_cohorts`, 01): the same niche, a comparable story form, one long-form runtime family. Mean, median, min, max, and spread are shown. Short-form or other niches can contribute transferable ideas, *never runtime defaults*. Long-form baseline here is at least 8 minutes (configurable at `init --min-longform`); you can select a higher floor for your channel. For long-form references in different duration clusters, split them. A 9-minute detailed how-to should not inherit the runtime of a 43-minute military epic.

Example:

```bash
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" niche-check --run "RUN"    # facts.cohorts: IDs, durations, median
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" length --run "RUN" --video 1 \
  --mode standard --cohort-id "single-case-investigations" --refs V004,V005,V006 --language en
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" length --run "RUN" --video 1 --mode story --language en
#   -> the story's own length: records the choice now; run again after the treatment decision, it locks
```

Do **not** pass `--pace-wpm` manually. The `length` command computes it automatically from the word counts and durations of the selected reference transcripts in `10-media/`. The computed value and a per-reference breakdown are stored in `length-lock.json` and printed in the output, with the references' `pace_profile` (below). The lock also reports the script's word budget (`target_words`). Only override with `--pace-wpm` if you are explicitly calibrating to a specific narrator sample and have a measured reason to diverge from the references: it needs `--pace-why` (four words or more, recorded as `pace_why`). References whose paces differ by more than 20% (the fastest over the slowest above 1.2) are not averaged into a pace no narrator has: choose the references whose narration this video follows, or give the pace with its reason. A reference narrated in another language than the run's cannot set the computed pace either: choose references in the run's language, or give `--pace-wpm` with `--pace-why` (01).

The locked Standard is the comparable cohort **median** rounded to five seconds. A cohort (`--cohort-id`, 01) can hold references of different story forms, whose lengths are made for different kinds of video: Standard then refuses unless something says why their lengths compare. A cohort of the niche map says it in its `why_comparable`, which the lock records as `forms_why`; otherwise `length --forms-why "..."` does; the lock records `story_forms` and `forms_why`. The references always come from the locked niche (`niche-lock`; a run made before niche discovery matches its card labels or cohort instead). Own length uses `--mode own --minutes 18`, its references (for the pace and the pauses) from the locked niche. Neither can be changed automatically by audio/edit outputs, and a chosen subject whose own size lies outside the figure waits at the direction lock for another candidate or angle, or for the user's decision on record (`subject.runtime_decision`, 02). If the subject genuinely cannot fill the requested runtime with meaning, research a richer adjacent angle or ask the user **before paid generation** whether to explicitly revise length; document revision, do not silently overwrite the original lock. The CLI deliberately rejects overwriting a lock: a new length, once the user has approved it, is `length --revise "why" --user-approved "<their own words>"` with the new `--mode` and its options (refused without both; the previous lock is kept whole in the new lock's `history`, beside `revised_because` and `approved_by_user`), or a new video ID. The beat map, the script and every later gate then follow the new lock.

**A cohort shares the niche, not necessarily the format.** The references a length comes from share this video's niche and audience; they may not share its telling: one story followed closely or a broad survey, a single source read aloud or many events, one narrator's density of facts or another's. When the locked treatment is chosen, compare its format, density and narration with the cohort's (the decision's `length_fit`, 10 §3) and with the references the pace was taken from. When they match, the length and pace stand. When they differ, say why the runtime still fits this telling, or bring the user the evidence (what this telling needs, what the references do, the length it suggests) and let them decide whether the length changes; the lock never moves silently, and an editorial problem (a slow middle, a late payoff) is never solved by changing the target. The opposite holds too: the length is never reached with filler. A runtime the material cannot fill is a research problem (a richer angle, stronger material) or the user's length decision, never repetition, recaps or a slower read. A length is never locked again on the pace of the first recorded part alone: a narrator's opening runs faster or slower than the body, and the forecast is taken from at least two parts of different kinds.

## Length arithmetic in service of quality, not padding

An English narration pace varies significantly by niche. The `length` command measures it from the selected reference transcripts and stores the result as `pace_wpm` in `length-lock.json` — use that number, not a generic estimate. Never assume 143–145 WPM as a planning default: real YouTube narrators have measured anywhere from about 170 to 210 WPM, and only this run's comparable references say where this video sits. Using a low estimate produces a script hundreds of words short of target.

`narration_s = spoken_words × 60 / pace_wpm + line_gap_s × lines + tag_time_s`  ·  `edit_s = narration_s + added_silence_s`, where each held pause adds only `max(0, whole silence wanted − the voice's own gap at that line)`

Pace is measured three ways, always with the same functions for the references and for our voice (`pace_profile`, `pace-profile`):

- **active WPM** (`pace_wpm`): words over the time Whisper marks as speech, pauses between its speech segments left out. How fast a narrator talks *while talking*. This is the `pace_wpm` in the formula above, where the line gaps, tags and holds are added on top.
- **overall WPM**: words over the span from the first word to the last, every pause included. How many words a minute of the narration really holds.
- **runtime WPM**: words over the whole video, intro and outro included.

The word budget comes from the overall rate: `target_words = target_s × overall_wpm / 60`, the words a comparable narrator fits into this runtime with their own pauses. Never multiply the runtime by the active rate: that counts no pauses at all and overstates the budget. `target_words_speech_only` is kept beside it as that upper bound. Here `line_gap_s` is the voice's own silence after each line: about 0.4 s before this voice is measured, then its measured value. `tag_time_s` is what the inline tags add, and `added_silence_s` is what the held pauses add on top of the voice's own gaps (estimated from the proposals until each is decided against the recorded voice, below). Spoken words exclude `[tags]`. A 430-word script will not magically become 12 minutes by giving every image a slow zoom, or by holding silence (below): when the words alone fall short of the export window and silence is what reaches it, `plan-check` and `retime-shots` refuse. For per-language estimates with characters or syllables, manually calibrate the voice sample and record an alternate estimate; do not mislabel English WPM as native pacing. Generated audio is ultimately authoritative, with hard checks after creation.

Speaking speed is not engagement. No pace range is universal, and lower is not better. A faster narrator is not more entertaining and a slower one is not duller: the pace is the voice's (measured on its first part, never forced, since Eleven v4 has no speed control), and the script is sized to it. Once recorded, each beat's pace is read against this narration's own median and its own natural variation (05: `pace_signals`, evidence examined under "Pace" in the editorial review, never a fixed percentage). The pace a script is planned at comes from the references whose narration this video's telling resembles (their format and density, not only their niche); a pace measured on a denser or looser format is a question to answer in `length_fit`, not a number to inherit. More words than the runtime holds at that pace are a selection problem: choose and cut, never plan a faster read to fit them in. And before a faster narrator or a denser script is considered, look at what the readers said about following it by ear: a listener already struggling with names and places at this pace is not helped by more of them per minute.

**The narrators this narration follows: weighed, never one convenient reference** (the user, 2026-11-25). The pace the length was locked at comes from the references the length came from, before the treatment and the script exist; it is a planning figure. Once the treatment's narrator is chosen, the decision weighs the reference narrators this narration follows (`decision.pacing`, 10 §3, 07): each one named, `relevant` or not, and `why`, against this film: the narrator's personality and delivery, the format, the information density and how familiar its terms are, the sentence structure, the emotional course, the natural variation, the pace while speaking against the overall pace (a narrator who speaks fast between long silences can be slow overall), and the pauses and what the audience must catch. `plan-check` shows each reference's measured profile beside this script's own density (names and numbers per 100 words, sentence length), the relevant narrators' range (while speaking, overall, and how far they swing within a film) and how the forecast moves across that range (`pacing.sensitivity`), with the voice pace that would land the locked length (`voice_pace_that_fits_the_lock_wpm`). Use several references where several genuinely match, each for what it matches (one for the narrator, another for the density): the range and the reasons decide where this narration belongs, never an average and never a score. When only one reference genuinely matches, it may stand alone, with its uncertainty said (`uncertainty`); never manufacture a second match. Where the niche has several measured narrators, more than one is weighed, and the references the locked pace came from are among them. A measured voice from another production is one voice in another film: technical history, never evidence for this one's pace.

**Narrator and length: neither forces the other.** The narrator is chosen for the treatment and its quality (personality, charisma, naturalness, emotional range, clarity), never rejected because its natural pace differs from the forecast, and its pace never silently overrides an approved length. Its real pace is measured on real recordings (a representative calibration first, 05; a short preview or probe never decides), with its natural variation, its pauses and its delivery read across the beats. When that pace and the locked length truly disagree, find where the problem is: the voice (a pace that loses the names, or a delivery that misses the treatment), the script's density, the delivery the beats ask for, or the length; keep the strongest creative result, and take any length change to the user (`length --revise --user-approved`). Nothing re-locks automatically, and the planning pace is never a target the delivery is pushed toward (05, step 3).

**This `pace_wpm` is a planning figure, not the final word-count authority.** It is measured from the *reference narrators*, before any TTS voice has been chosen for this video. A synthetic voice does not necessarily speak at the same rate as the humans in the references. Once a voice is picked and measured on real recordings (`05-voice-and-edit.md`: `voice-pick`, the representative calibration, `tts --max-parts 1`, `narration-measure`), its measured pace and line gap (the video's `60-voice/video-NN/voice-lock.json` → `measured`) **supersede** this reference figure. `plan-check` then reports `real_forecast_s` against the export window, and `audio-check` requires it there; a real narration outside it goes to the user with its evidence (05, step 3), never forced to fit. It also lists the pace numbers (`pace_sources`: references while speaking and overall, script, voice while speaking and overall) and warns when the script's `pace_wpm` disagrees with the voice's. Writing the full script before a voice exists is fine; finalising it without re-checking against the voice that will speak it is how a correctly planned script ends up the wrong length. A voice preview's or a short probe's pace is never this number: short samples read fast and scatter.

Use **content-rich length**: chapters earn runtime through explanation, examples, cause-and-effect, character decisions, counterarguments, concrete research and room to think. A script that comes out 900 words short is first a question about the length, not about the script: the story as written is its own size. Where the story set the length, re-lock to that size yourself before narration is recorded. A Standard or own length is the user's: take the size to them before anything is added (`plan-check` reports it as `length_conflict`, with what to do first); a shorter length (`length --revise --user-approved`) is the first answer. Material is added only when the film lacks something the viewer needs, which the direction or a reader names; never to reach a length, and never as reiteration. Cutting for length is a boredom pass, not a trim: take out first what a listener would drift through (the same point made again, a fact with no consequence for anyone, a list read aloud), and keep the vivid detail that carries a beat, even when it costs words.

**The first whole draft is the story's own size.** Write the first complete draft at the size the story needs, before fitting anything to the lock. `plan-check` records its word count once (`40-plans/video-NN/script-size.json`). When the script later stands more than 15% above that draft, `plan-check` asks what the added lines carry: answer with `plan-check --grew-for "..."` (which value, development or viewer need they serve; "to reach the length" is refused, because that is the user's length decision). The answer is kept with the size it was given for, and a script that grows well past it is asked again. The 15% is where a look is asked, never a limit: a draft that was thin and grew for good reasons says so in one sentence.

**From research to script: select, never invent.** Research usually holds more than a video can carry. Choose what enters the script by what the viewer needs at that moment: a fact that answers a question they are carrying, makes a stake or a consequence concrete, gives a person a face, or sets up a moment that matters, over a fact that is merely true and interesting. Scale and texture make an abstract value felt ("a fine of one shield" means little until the viewer knows what a shield cost or what a day's labour bought): find that context in the research, and when it is not there, research it. A number, a price, a date or a quotation enters the script only from a claim the ledger holds with its source; a disputed one is said as disputed and a legend as a legend (the beat's `hedges`), a rounded figure says "about", and nothing is invented to make a line land, not even a plausible round figure. When a stretch needs stronger material, the answer is more research or another selection, never a made-up detail, a composite or an implied certainty.

**A key line says an uncertain fact as surely as it is known, and no surer.** `plan-check` lists every line of a key moment, of the payoff or of the ending that is tied (claims.json `line_refs`) to a claim the research did not confirm (likely, disputed, legend), beside what the research held, and writes an entry for each in `40-plans/video-NN/claim-wording.json` (07). Answer each one: `"how": "hedged"` with `"words"`, the words of the line that carry the doubt or say whose finding it is, quoted exactly (they must be in that line, or in an earlier line of the same beat named in `"in_line"`); or `"how": "plain"` with `"why"`: what the line states is the part the research does confirm, or why it is safe to say without a qualifier. A reworded line or a changed claim asks again, and the earlier answer is kept under `superseded`. A claim that gets its second source needs no answer: raise its confidence in claims.json. The code never judges the wording and holds no list of hedging words: it shows the line beside the research and checks that the answer is for the words as they are. A hedge is not a weakness in the telling: "one study found" and "the records suggest" are how a careful narrator sounds.

## Macro arc and micro arcs

Before writing the full narration, choose a flexible structure of **movements**, not a preordained 3-act or “hook every 15 seconds” rule. For each movement decide:

- what the viewer currently understands, wants to know and may be feeling;
- what **new information, action, evidence, or meaningful contrast** it delivers;
- how a specific visual can convey something the narration leaves open;
- how it changes stakes/interpretation and where the viewer can think for themself;
- what early promise it fulfills or new question it genuinely raises.

The movements come from the locked treatment (10 §2, its `movements`): the order in which the viewer should learn, which is not automatically the order in which things happened. Before writing, ask of each stretch before the video delivers what its opening promises: what does the viewer get here on the way (a discovery, tension, a laugh, an explanation that clicks, a reason to care), and would they rather have this now or the thing they came for? Background is strongest when it answers a question the viewer is already asking: often the promised material can come first and the background follow through the questions it raises. Every wait raises the bar for what fills it. There is no minute by which anything must appear; a long build that rewards is a choice, a long build that only postpones is the most common way a well-researched script loses its viewer.

Let some moments breathe. In a long video, too much forced tension becomes tiring; a funny human detail or quietly revealing image can reset the experience. Each chapter should have a mini-arc, but it should also feed the main narrative. Plant and pay off specific details with causal continuity, not an arbitrary twist. For history, guard chronology, geography, actors and evolving knowledge. For an instructional topic, guard step dependency; a later step cannot assume an unshown setup or material.

**A line that sums up reads as the end, wherever it stands.** The title spoken aloud, a return to the opening picture, the music resolving: each tells a YouTube viewer the film is over, and all three together did so nearly three minutes before one film's real ending (a cold viewer wrote "the film appears to end at 13:06", 2026-11-28). Either the summing-up line is the true ending, or what follows it is promised inside the same breath (a question left open in that line, the picture and the sound already leaning into the next part). The largest thing a film says late (an institution conceding its method, a verdict, a reversal) gets plain words and room: said in seventeen seconds of abstract wording it passed two cold viewers as one more citation, and no picture added afterwards slowed it down. Such a line is found and weighed in the script, where it costs nothing to change; once the narration is recorded, its cure is a new recording.

**Two cases set side by side, and a rule taught in round numbers.** When a film sets two cases beside each other (two buildings, two years, two methods), it says what differs between them, from the source, or it does not set them side by side: taking a figure away does not answer "the numbers do not add up" while the comparison itself stays unexplained. A rule given to the viewer in round numbers (a fifth of the grip for each degree) is recalled at the first real case, and it is never worked against the source's own sums in front of the viewer: where the two disagree, the film says which one the case is measured by.

**What the viewer knows at each turn.** Keep the audience's understanding in view as the story moves: at each turn, what have they learned, what does this moment answer, what new question does it open, and do they know why it matters? Write it where it changes decisions, in the beat map's `viewer_question`, `experience` and, when a beat leans on something given earlier, `depends_on` (`{"what", "from": an earlier beat or line}`). Something held back on purpose is `{"what", "withheld": why the uncertainty serves the story, "revealed_in": a later beat or line}`; plan-check refuses a beat that leans on a later one without saying so. plan-check also lists, as facts, where several names the viewer must hold arrive at once and where a name returns after a long absence. Decide each case: a short reminder, a picture that puts a face to a name, another order, a detail cut because it does not matter yet, or nothing, when the density or the mystery is the point. Productive complexity stays; accidental confusion goes. Classify each signal under a "Comprehension" heading in `editorial-review.md`: its line ids, one kind (`necessary_complexity`, `intentional_mystery`, `accidental_confusion`, `poor_sequencing`, `redundancy` or `missing_context`) and the reason (five words or more). One line may answer several signals that share a root (a name in common, or one beat); a line that groups signals sharing nothing answers only the first related group, and the rest (`comprehension_signals_grouped_without_a_shared_cause`) are answered each on its own line. plan-check blocks until every signal is classified. A name returning after a long absence inside a `key_moment` beat is what that moment rests on: `intentional_mystery` is right for it only when the beat withholds it on purpose (a `depends_on` entry with `withheld`); otherwise plan-check refuses the classification (`key_moment_dependency_called_a_mystery`) and the dependency is made available where it is needed: planted with its significance where it first appears and re-anchored in the line itself, the picture reinforcing the words, never replacing them. The signals stay facts, never a reason to simplify by default. Never simplify, slow down or explain by default: one clearer sentence or one picture usually does more than a paragraph. A `key_moment` beat also states what it means for the viewer (`significance`: `matters_because`, `reward`, and `depends_on` or `significance.self_contained`; a contested claim names its `hedge` line): plan-check refuses a key moment without it, and the readers are asked the same three things separately (10 §4, 06).

## Exact file structures, in `40-plans/video-NN/`

`blueprint.json` has two tiers. **Universally required:** `audience_promise`, `narrative_approach` (a brief description of the specific creative strategy—e.g. `"decision-reconstruction narrative"`, `"step-by-step tutorial with failure modes"`, `"reflective essay comparing two outcomes"`), `why_this_length`, `original_angle`, `audience_knows` (six words or more: what the intended viewer already knows, so nothing they know is explained and nothing they lack is assumed), `visual_grammar`, `factuality` (`factual`, `hypothetical`, `fiction`), a nonempty `research_sources` array for factual pieces, and either `ending_payoff` (where the video builds to one) or `reward_shape` (how its value builds up, where it is delivered along the way: a tutorial, a tour of a mechanism, a run of jokes). **Conditional (include when the format calls for it):** `central_question` for curiosity-driven or investigative structures; `human_lens` for biographical, decision-based, or witness-led narratives. Add optional `title_options`, `chapter_outline`, `character_map`, `scene_world_rules`, `viewer_thought_space`, `sources_for_facts`, `pacing_notes`, `story_risks`.

`script.json` is written **after** `direction-lock` (10): the locked treatment decides how the narration sounds, so the words are written in its voice. It contains `language` matching the lock, calibrated `pace_wpm`, and `lines`: one object per numbered narration line, with `id` (`L001`...), `text`, and optional `pause_total_s`, `pause_via`, `pause_purpose`, `pause_picture`, `pause_sound` (a planned pause, below) and `note`. For long-form, line = spoken sentence or short group of connected sentences, not individual words. Write for the ear: vary syntax, believable phrasing, breathing and emotional pacing. No voiceover of arbitrary on-screen design direction. Reading time is estimated from the final exact text, never an outline. Once audio exists, rewrite lines rather than adding or removing them: recorded parts keep their line boundaries, so only the part that holds an edited line is re-recorded.

Each thing lives in its own place. `text` is the canonical narration, exactly the words the viewer hears (captions come from it), plus any inline delivery tag the story calls for (`"[pause] At ten o'clock..."`); it is sent to ElevenLabs as written. Pronunciation goes in `pronunciation.json` (05), applied only to what is sent. A note for the team goes in the line's `note`, never sent. plan-check refuses anything else in `text` that would reach the voice as markup: SSML (`<break>`: v4 doesn't support it), `{braces}`, `*asterisks*`, a stage direction in parentheses, a note in brackets, IPA.

`beat-map.json` contains `target_length_s` equal to lock and `beats` with `id`, `start_s`, `end_s`, `line_start`, `line_end`, `narrative_job` and the beat's direction from the locked treatment (10 §4): `experience` (what the viewer should feel or understand by its end), `narration_direction` (register, energy and what the words do here), `visual_intent` (what the pictures show that the words don't; composition, motion, atmosphere), `sound_direction` and `pace` (read against the film's own pace; `pace_relative`, `slower`, `same` or `faster`, says it outright). The treatment evolves beat by beat; it is not one setting for the whole film. Mark `chapter_start: true` on the first beat of each chapter where the story itself starts again: recording parts then break only there (05). Add `viewer_question`, `new_value`, `emotional_shift`, `evidence_ids`, `shot_family`, `callback_to`, `risk` where valuable, and `key_moment` (a few words: "the reveal", "the climax") on the beats where a change of timing would change the film (held silences changed there after the script read are read again) (pauses live on the script's lines, below, not on beats). Time spans cover **0 → target** without gaps or overlaps; line ranges cover **every script line exactly once**. Don't auto-fill empty minutes with rest footage. When actual narration is available, retime stills while preserving the lock and story's intent.

**Silence is a tool, not an ingredient.** The default is the narrator's own rhythm: the breath and the short gap the voice leaves after every line (it differs from line to line, several-fold within one part, so each one is measured, never assumed). Most revelations, jokes and turns land inside that rhythm, carried by the writing (a short line after a long one, a plain statement) and by the picture, which can go on showing the reveal while the next line starts. A held silence is for the few moments where the viewer needs time the voice does not give: a picture that is still revealing something, a consequence that has to land before anyone speaks again, an ending that settles. Before planning one, ask: does the voice's own delivery already give this moment its time? What will the viewer see and hear while it lasts? Would they wait, or want the voice to go on? There is no quota and no formula: not "a pause after every reveal", not a length per joke or per ending, not lengths that grow through the film, not a reference's pause copied as a target, never silence to reach the length. Some films have no held silence at all; others need several, and a long one can be exactly right when the picture and the moment carry it.

**A planned pause is a proposal, decided later on evidence.** It is decided here, by whoever judges the story's structure (the audio stage never chooses its own moments), and written on its line:

- `pause_total_s`: the **whole** silence the viewer should hear after the line, the voice's own gap included. Never the amount to add: the edit adds only the difference with the gap the voice really leaves there, once that is measured.
- `pause_via`: `"edit"` (a hold the edit adds: exact, free, adjustable after recording, the picture holds) or `"tag"` (a pause tag at this line's end or the next line's start: the voice performs it, its cost is counted inside the narration and it is fixed once recorded). One way per silence: an edit hold on a line that also carries a pause tag would stack the two, and `plan-check` refuses it.
- `pause_purpose` (what the viewer takes in or feels while it lasts), `pause_picture` (what is on screen and what changes in it, or why one frame holds) and `pause_sound` (whether the bed goes on, drops out or gives way to one story-world sound). A convincing sentence is not an approval: the length is decided against the recorded voice and the picture.
- `pause_landing` (how this line should land going into the silence: flat and final, a question left hanging, a soft settle) and `pause_resume` (how the next line should start again: a fresh lift, a quiet continuation). The performance around the silence is planned with it (05, "Narration as a performance").
- A line with a delivery cue says its scope in `cue_scope`: the words the cue should colour and where the delivery returns (a cue colours the rest of its own line; 05).
- The line before a planned silence is written to go into it the way the moment needs: a line meant to land ends like it (a full stop, a short final clause), a setup meant to hang can stay open; the next line is written to start again (10 §5). Delivery tags only where the writing can't carry it (05).

The length written here is provisional: it is a guess made before any voice exists. Once the narration is measured, each planned pause is decided against the gap the voice really left there, and again with its picture (05: `holds`, `hold-decide`); the decisions live in `40-plans/video-NN/holds.json`, apart from the reviewed script, and nothing undecided is inserted.

The older form, `pause_after_s` (over 1 s: silence added on top of the voice's gap; up to 1 s: nothing), is still read as it was written and reviewed, so an older run resumes unchanged; `plan-check` warns, and each such pause is decided against the recorded voice like any other. `plan-check`'s `hold_signals` (warnings) point out lengths that step up or down through the film or are all alike: a sign the length may have come from a position or a habit rather than the moment. `pace-profile` shows how often the comparable narrators pause: that is the register of the genre (do these narrators stop at all?), never a length to match.

**Silence never stands in for story.** There is no fixed limit on held silence: some films want long reflective holds. But a video earns its runtime with what it says and shows, and an underwritten script does not become a full one by adding holds: when the words alone (with the voice's own gaps) fall short of the export window and the held silence is what reaches it, `plan-check` refuses (`runtime_reached_with_held_silence`), and so does `retime-shots` on the real narration. The holds are part of what the cold reader reads: the script packet shows every one as the viewer would hear it ("silence: about N s in all, the voice stops, the picture stays. On screen: ... Sound: ...") and asks the reader, as a first-time viewer, whether each is needed or makes them wait. Holds changed in the script after the last read beyond a small tweak need a new read. The tweak is counted from that read, all changes together (two lines and about five seconds in all, every nudge added, however small), so a run of small edits can't pass one at a time. A hold changed in the opening (the first beat: the hook), the ending (the last beat) or a beat the map marks `key_moment` (a reveal, the climax, an emotional turn) needs a new read whatever its size: a six-second closing hold added after the read is read again. An older script whose holds were written before every hold had to say what it is for: when it resumes, look at each one and write the purpose it really serves; a hold that only fills runtime is removed and the plan fixed (the story, not an invented reason). `plan-check` shows how much of the runtime the words alone fill (`holds.content_forecast_s`) and how much the holds would carry (`runtime_carried_by_holds_s`). When the planned content cannot justify the runtime, the answer is the story: research a richer angle or strengthen the beats, never pad. A decision taken after recording (`hold-decide`) does not touch the reviewed script and asks for no new script read: a text reader cannot judge 1.2 s against 2.5 s, the final watch review sees the silences as they are.

**Delivery tags** (05's catalogue: `STUDIO/data/elevenlabs-audio-tags.json`) are chosen in the same pass, from the creative strategy's sense of how the narration should sound: wherever a cue gets a moment's performance directly, never as a quota or decoration. They are written inline in `text`, and each kept tag gets one line under "Delivery and tags" in the review (plan-check refuses a tag without one). Every tag costs measured time, which `plan-check` adds to the forecast. Budget pauses and tags out of the word count before the calibration, not after a full recording runs long.

`editorial-review.md` is the writer's frank self-audit (not a numeric-only scoreboard): 5 best passages, 5 likely viewer exit/boring/confusing risks, devices repeated until they turn into a tic (`writing_signals`), claims requiring checking, missing texture, beat-to-beat repetition, visual argument opportunities, and what was revised. Do it before asking for the cold read, so the reader's attention goes to what you could not see yourself. The creative strategy lives in `direction.json` (02, 10), not here. These sections are read by the checks:
- **"Delivery and tags"**: one line per kept tag and per planned pause (line, tag or hold, why this moment needs time the voice does not give, why this length and not shorter or longer). A reference's pause is evidence to interpret (what that silence did in its own scene), never a length to copy and never the reason on its own; the voice's usual gaps are context, not a ceiling.
- **"Content flags"**: one line per line `plan-check` lists under `content_flags`, saying whether it was rewritten or why it stays. These are words that may break 02's rules on holidays and religion, indecency, gore and distress, and swearing. A flag is a question, not a verdict; an unanswered flag blocks paid generation.
- **"Comprehension"**: one line per comprehension signal `plan-check` lists, or per group of signals that share a root (the ids, one kind and the reason: "What the viewer knows at each turn", above). plan-check blocks until every signal is classified.
- **"Pace"**: a line per pace signal of `narration-measure` (05, `pace_signals`) naming the beat id (or `overall`), with five words or more: what the measured pace does there and whether it stays. Beats with one shared cause may share a line, and the examination can conclude the pace stays. The audio gate blocks until every signal is examined.

**The cold read (06, 10 §7).** Before the first paid narration, and after the opening contest (10 §4: the packet waits for its decision), `review-packet --kind script` writes the title and the bare script (no plan, no rationale) for ONE fresh reader, and `review-record` seals its findings. 06 has how each finding is answered, when a fresh read follows (a major finding; a change to a line the reader's verdict rested on) and what a problem that comes back needs. A reviewer critiques the *actual script*, not the prose of its plan. Edits after the last read that no finding asked for pass only when small (a length trim); anything more needs a new read, because the approval no longer describes the script.

Command gates:

```bash
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" plan-check --run "RUN" --video 1
```

This validates:
- the lock, the chapter/line map, basic blueprint completeness, the script language and what reaches the voice (05);
- the tags, the planned pauses, the content flags and the comprehension signals, as the sections above say;
- that `direction.json` is complete and locked, unchanged since its lock, with the lock made before the script (or recorded as late), and that every beat carries its direction;
- that no beat changed since the last passing plan-check while its lines did not: revise those lines, or keep them with `plan-check --keep "why they still fit"` (07);
- that the script had its cold read, every finding is answered and the read still describes the current script (06);
- and it lists `writing_signals` (never blocking);
- the predicted duration: within 8% before the voice is measured, then `real_forecast_s` inside the export window. **A passed machine check is not an approval to make a boring 1700-word text**; it only means the length promise and coverage are structurally plausible. The producer then confirms true informational substance and natural voice flow. No paid narration or stills before both gates pass.

## A note on title and thumbnail

The working promise is written with the subject (`direction.json` → `subject.selection.promise`: a working title, a thumbnail idea in words, what a viewer who clicks expects; 02) and revised as the film takes shape. Develop several accurate **package concepts** around it while researching. The title/thumbnail should invite curiosity without falsely promising an event not actually explained. The opening must give early evidence of value; how early is for this subject and its references to say (02). Prepare `70-edit/video-NN/publishing.md` with proposed titles, final description, chapters with exported timestamps, sources/credits/licensing and thumbnail prompt/assets. Thumbnail composition may be more striking than the body style but should not misrepresent the actual subject or falsely depict a photo of a historical event. Evaluate packaging with the *finished* video, not in isolation.
