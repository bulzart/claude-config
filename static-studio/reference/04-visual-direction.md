# 04 — Direct the image-led film, not a wallpaper slideshow

## Visual manifesto

The core format is **voice + still images and meaningful graphics edited into a YouTube long-form film**. It is neither silent reference-video recreation nor a sequence of generic prompts. Art and explanation are equal storytelling instruments. A good shot **answers, complicates, compares, humanizes, locates, foreshadows, contrasts or lets the viewer think**. Repeating the narrator's nouns with a random illustration is not enough.

The look is not chosen here on its own: it is the locked treatment's `visual_language` and `audiovisual_relationship` (`direction.json`, 10), worked out in detail, and each beat's `visual_intent` says what the pictures do at that moment. Create `60-visuals/video-NN/style-bible.md` from them with: medium (documentary illustration, map/diagram, stylized paintings, clean product/tech renders as appropriate), palette, lighting, texture, character/prop/world identities, typography overlay rules, image composition grammar, historical or physical restrictions, and rule for factual photo vs invented illustration. Maintain visual continuity across an event: geography, facing direction, sunlight, military insignia, scale, tools, injury/damage state, attire, who holds which prop. Scenes cannot teleport silently. Change shot scale to earn perspective, not to fake energy.

For every beat, make a **visual argument sheet** `shot-plan.json`, each segment with ID, beat ID, its script lines (`line_start`/`line_end`, contiguous across all shots: the real timing comes from these), approximate start/end (planning only; `retime-shots` replaces them with the real narration's, 05), frame prompt, composition, what NEW thing the viewer learns/feels from the picture, continuity anchors, whether diagram/map/callout/overlay is appropriate, how a visual idea evolves across adjacent shots, and whether it requires verified imagery. `new_thing` is required on every shot (what the picture gives that the words don't); a shot without one is decoration. Run `shot-check` before generating stills: it refuses a shot without its beat or its `new_thing`, a beat without a picture and non-contiguous lines, and lists prompts that are nearly the same and long runs of one composition (a callback, or the same picture twice?). A deliberate callback is a shot with `"reuses": "S010"`: it shows that earlier shot's still again, so it needs no frame prompt and nothing is generated for it. `retime-shots` refuses a plan that fails it. Look for these meaningful shot families:

- **Establish / orient:** clean geography, timeline, workspace, spatial relationship, scale or environment.
- **Decision / human:** the expression, hand movement, choice, consequence, ordinary object or lived limitation that makes an abstraction concrete.
- **Explain / compare:** diagram, overlay arrows added in CapCut, contrasting models, labelled relationship, map movement, before/after or cause/effect state.
- **Reveal / reframe:** deliberate change in perspective that creates an earned “oh, that's why” moment.
- **Breathe / reflect:** an evocative image held long enough for thought, rather than continuous informational strain.
- **Callback:** return to an earlier object/detail and visibly resolve or deepen it.

No requirement that every category appear every minute. A slow map animation is valuable when tracking route/constraints; decorative sparkle is not. Select the rhythm from comparable references in the same cluster, then adapt. A 14-minute thoughtful history film needn't cut at the rate of a TikTok Short.

## Static plus subtle motion (image-led, no forced video generation)

Allowed editing tools: modest Ken Burns movement, pans, reframing, layer masks, depth planes/parallax when achievable, wipe-on annotations and hand-drawn highlight, light temperature changes, overlays, comparison split screens, maps with moving pins, animated simple shapes and purposeful captions. Use CapCut for overlays and transitions where possible; don't bake normal subtitle text, motion arrows, social stickers or heavy VFX into generated base images merely because the model can. However **visible text is allowed when it is intrinsically part of the factual scene**, such as a label on an important document, signage, diagram or needed interface. Historic or scientific data graphics should be legible, accurately laid out and checked rather than trusting model-rendered gibberish.

Music/SFX create dimension but shouldn't erase room for a quiet moment. A sound cue should have a narrative job (recognition, scale, spatial setting, surprise, transition, or an emotional punctuation). Avoid constant sound effects, templates, forced whip pans and musical crescendos every chapter. Treat narration, illustration and music as one composition.

## Drawn and animated explanations (encouraged, not an exception)

A drawn layer — lines, arrows, shapes, a building list, a growing bar, a circled detail — that relates to what is said at that moment and shows, points at, builds up or stresses it better than the still alone does is **one of the film's main tools, not decoration for the rare chart**. Plan for it at every stage: the shot plan, the stills, and the edit all look for where a drawing would teach or land something the still cannot.

**Not only numbers.** Generalize past "a statistics animation": a quantity or comparison; steps in order or a process running; a cause and what follows it; a relationship or contrast; a place, route, direction or "look here" on the picture; a timeline or a before/after; a definition or a list built item by item; a key word or name written beside the thing it names (never a line of narration — that's the captions' job); something to notice, circled, underlined or lit. Read the script beat by beat and ask what the viewer needs to see now that the still cannot show. A moment nothing drawn would show better is left to the still.

**What feels right, not piled on.** The test is whether the drawing would be missed if it were gone. A still with nothing on it is a normal, good moment; a quiet stretch isn't a gap to fill. Signs of too much: a drawing competing with the narration or captions, two animating at once, a drawing on every still for minutes with no room to breathe, a build that takes longer than the words it explains, the same kind of animation repeated with nothing new to show. Fewer, simpler and well-timed beats more. How many is the agent's call per video — never a quota, never a gate, and not a target to hit either.

**Never lie.** What a drawing states matches the narration and the facts file exactly — the same number in the same unit, a process in the source's order. A drawing never makes a result look larger or a cause stronger than the source supports.

**Where they live: CapCut, never the image generation.** The still stays a plain scene; the image model is never asked for a chart, arrow, number or word (the no-subtitle-like-text rule above still applies to every still). A drawing is a separate, animated, transparent-background clip rendered by `STUDIO/scripts/draw_tool.py` from a JSON spec (lines/arrows, ellipses/rects, polygons, text; each element has its own entrance — draw, wipe, fade, grow, pop, or type for text — timed to the word it elaborates) and placed on its own overlay track in CapCut above the still (`09-capcut-toolkit.md`). When a still will carry a drawing of some size, plan room for it in the still's own prompt (a plain wall, an empty tabletop, an open floor) as part of the scene, never as "space for text" — and never with the drawing itself drawn into the image.

Every content rule above still applies inside a drawing: no subtitle-like text, no sticker/emoji/icon/badge, nothing religious or superstitious, nothing indecent, gory or distressing, every figure a clearly adult one.

## Image prompt contract

Before creating an image, specify (a) exact beat/visual job (the beat's `visual_intent` and the shot's `new_thing`), (b) scene and continuity anchors, (c) primary readable subject and visual hierarchy, (d) viewpoint/framing and reserved caption space, (e) texture, look and light (the style bible, which carries the locked treatment's visual language), (f) physical and factual restrictions, (g) what must **not** appear, especially copied source overlays. (g) is a checklist for inspecting the result, never words in the prompt: the prompt describes what *is* there instead (see the rule below). Images should look like the **same film** even when topics/scenes vary; justified stylistic contrasts may be marked as such in the style bible. Verify every result with visual inspection: hands, objects, text, map boundaries, chronology, scale, logos, number of people, weapons/equipment accuracy where relevant, and whether the picture communicates the intended new idea.

For a niche with real historical artifacts/places/equipment, start by researching visual references and source provenance; either use licensed originals with attribution, or create a clearly illustrated interpretation based on grounded descriptions. Never turn model imagination into documentary evidence. For quantitative diagrams, prefer deterministic chart/map rendering over hallucinated numbers/labels.

**Nothing indecent, gory or distressing (02's rule, applied to pictures).** Every person is dressed for the scene, and the prompt names their clothes from top to bottom — a model left to guess undresses a person whose moment suggests it (a shower/bath/changing-clothes line draws bare skin unless the clothing is explicit in the prompt). A line about an undressed moment is shown by its dressed moment or by its object instead (a shower becomes the running water in an empty bathroom, or the person afterward, dressed, hair wet, towel round the neck). A medical or injury moment shows the care, never the harm: hands helping, the equipment, the patient awake with eyes open and sitting up — never a wound, a contorted face, a collapse as it happens, or a corpse. A survival pose low on the ground is drawn with the person clearly alive in it (head raised or turned so the open eyes show, mid-move). Feelings stay short of extremes (worry, alarm, relief, a frown — never agony or terror). Every person and character is drawn as a clearly adult figure (an age in years in the prompt; adult facial proportions — jaw, chin, mature features — never a round chubby child's face). The prompt names what *is* there, never what must stay out — a prompt that says "no blood" tends to draw some. The thumbnail follows the same rule: its face reads as surprise, worry or curiosity at a glance, never terror or pain.

**Content flags on prompts.** `shot-check` and every generation job read each picture prompt for 02's subjects and for any theme a cited reference's content review excluded (`prompt_content_flag_unanswered`). Rewrite the scene, or give the shot (or job item) a `content_note` saying why the flagged word is not that content ("blood orange is the sky's colour; nothing is hurt"). Never answer it with a negative phrase ("no blood"): change what the picture shows. Style words added around an approved frame prompt cannot bring in a flag of their own.

**Every picture looked at.** Before the edit passes, every picture the timeline shows is looked at whole and recorded: `content-review --run "RUN" --video NN --pictures --file looks.json`, one entry per file with `clean` or `issue` and what it shows. A record holds for that exact file; a remade picture is looked at again. An `issue` is remade or replaced, never kept.

## Engines (pick for each run; no other generation modes)

- **OpenAI Images API** (if the user's API credentials and endpoint permissions are configured): use `STUDIO/scripts/openai_images_worker.py --job <job.json>` with `run`, `video` and a list of `{id,prompt,file}` stills (each `id` a shot of the shot plan), model/quality/size, initially **without** `--execute` to inspect count, access and its `production_gate` answer, then **with** `--execute` only after approving cost. Current selectable model IDs include `gpt-image-2.5-flare` and `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` (verify access and current API pricing). The worker saves image PNGs and resumable manifests, not the API key; this API generation path does not handle image edits/references and must not be falsely claimed to do so. For image-to-image, use a properly supported current provider API or local worker. Verify real outputs and continuity before the edit.
- **Local FLUX 3 Image Klein** (optional; existing ComfyUI setup): use `STUDIO/scripts/image_worker.py <job.json>` in the correct ComfyUI portable Python environment (`AWL_IMAGE_ROOT` and `AWL_IMAGE_PYTHON` may already be configured). It accepts `{"op":"generate", "run":"RUN", "video":1, "images":[{"shot_id":"S001","prompt":"...","out":"...png","width":1920,"height":1088,"seed":123,"steps":16,"upsample":true,"refs":[]}]}` as a starting structure; use dimensions divisible by 16 and use correct aspect/crop in CapCut. `{"op":"check"}` verifies local dependencies. This worker was preserved from the user's Static tooling, not an unrelated video generator. When its memory guard stops it (free RAM under 1 GB or free commit under 3 GB), find what holds memory by **private bytes** (`Get-Process | Sort-Object PrivateMemorySize64 -Descending`), not by working set. Working set hides the background apps that hold commit. Name them to the user, who decides what to close; never close the user's programs yourself or loosen the guard.
- **User-supplied art:** write a complete prompt sheet, accept the new files, verify orientation/quality, record that these pictures are the film's (`direction-reapprove --artifact stills --reason "..."`), then edit. A pre-existing reference video's frames are **never** silently repurposed as newly generated assets.

**The production gate.** Both image workers and the music worker refuse a job before anything loads or is paid unless it passes `static_core.media_gate`. The job names its `run` and `video`. The creative direction is locked and unchanged since its lock, and the beat map passes its checks. For stills, `shot-check` passes and each still names a shot (`shot_id`; `id` for OpenAI) whose approved `frame_prompt` its prompt carries word for word, with style-bible words around it. Nothing the job uses was left behind by a direction change (10 §3). `static_core.py media-check --job FILE --kind stills|music` gives the same answer by hand, free, plus `direction`: each still's beat `visual_intent` and `experience` and its shot's `new_thing`, each track's beats' `sound_direction`, and the locked treatment's visual language or sound. The prompts and captions carry those out. A job's `purpose` is `shots` (the default), `treatment_preview` (before the lock: each item names a `treatment` and a sample `moment`, and its prompt carries that sample's picture) or `thumbnail` (the locked direction, no shot). Every film still and track made is recorded with its shot or beats and the direction revision it was made under (`60-visuals/video-NN/stills-provenance.json`, `70-edit/video-NN/music-provenance.json`), and `timeline-check` traces each CapCut clip's picture through that record.

Optional music bed: `STUDIO/scripts/music_worker.py <job.json>` with user's existing ACE-Step 1.5 environment. It can create instrumental tracks; check first. The job names `run` and `video`, and each track the `beats` it scores; its caption carries out those beats' `sound_direction` and the treatment's `sound`. Do not run locally if the engine/environment is absent. Other licensed music sources are acceptable with documented rights, but the command should not fabricate licenses.

## Long-form visual inventory and pacing

Prepare images/graphics for the **whole narration**, not just the opening. The references' measured rhythm (`av-profile`: shot lengths, moving share, cuts per minute, by third of the film) is evidence for choosing this film's rhythm, not a target to copy. The number of stills depends on how much a shot changes, not an arbitrary seconds-per-image average. Some 20-second explanatory diagrams work; a static single face for 75 seconds may be dead air. Use beat map and actual speech timing to decide. For every quarter of the film, confirm there are visual discoveries, explanatory imagery or a consciously justified sustained hold. Plan especially around chapter transitions, complications and the ending. Don't spend the entire image budget in the first three minutes.

The edit should support 16:9 at least 1080p, keep important subjects in safe margins, use licensed local fonts only (do **not** bundle font binaries), respect low-volume playback readability, and retain enough negative space for chapter labels when those genuinely help comprehension. For publication thumbnail, develop separate 16:9 image concepts optimized for a small display, not a random frame crop.
