# 04 — Direct the image-led film, not a wallpaper slideshow

## Visual manifesto

The core format is **voice + still images and meaningful graphics edited into a YouTube long-form film**. It is neither silent reference-video recreation nor a sequence of generic prompts. Art and explanation are equal storytelling instruments. A good shot **answers, complicates, compares, humanizes, locates, foreshadows, contrasts or lets the viewer think**. Repeating the narrator's nouns with a random illustration is not enough.

The look is not chosen here on its own: it is the locked treatment's `visual_language` and `audiovisual_relationship` (`direction.json`, 10), worked out in detail, and each beat's `visual_intent` says what the pictures do at that moment. Create `60-visuals/video-NN/style-bible.md` from them with: medium (documentary illustration, map/diagram, stylized paintings, clean product/tech renders as appropriate), palette, lighting, texture, character/prop/world identities, typography overlay rules, image composition grammar, historical or physical restrictions, and rule for factual photo vs invented illustration. Maintain visual continuity across an event: geography, facing direction, sunlight, military insignia, scale, tools, injury/damage state, attire, who holds which prop. Scenes cannot teleport silently. Change shot scale to earn perspective, not to fake energy.

**The style lock: one look for every still.** Once the direction is locked and the style bible written, `static_core.py style-lock --run "RUN" --video NN --style-prompt "<style words>" --departs-when "photorealistic, flat vector, neon" [--palette "#RRGGBB,#RRGGBB"] [--ref STYLE_IMAGE]... [--why "..."]` fixes the look: `60-visuals/video-NN/style-lock.json` (`rev`, `style_prompt`, `departs_when`, `palette`, `refs` with their sha256, the chosen treatment's `visual_language` sha256, the style bible's sha256, `history`). `--departs-when` is required: comma-separated, what would be ANOTHER look than this one. A change is a new revision and needs `--why`. The stills gate needs a current lock: it refuses one that is missing (`style_lock_missing`), outdated because the chosen treatment's visual language or the style bible changed (relock with `--why`), or pointing at a style reference that changed. The lock is an identity, not identical wording. A still's prompt is the shot's approved `frame_prompt` plus added words: it carries the locked style words in any order, or says in `style_note` (five words or more) how its own wording keeps that look (`item_N_does_not_carry_the_locked_style`). Words added beyond the frame prompt and the style words (a shot's own light, framing, atmosphere, rendering) are allowed, as many as the shot needs, with their reason in `style_note` (`item_N_adds_words_beyond_the_approved_prompt_and_the_style`). Added words that reach for a `departs_when` phrase are a style change, and no note covers them (`item_N_reaches_for_another_look`): relock (`style-lock --why`), or, for one picture on purpose, say why in `style_departure` (six words or more). A thumbnail carries the look (the style words or a `style_note`) or gives a `style_departure`, and its whole prompt is checked for a departure phrase. FLUX's caption must keep at least half of the style words and add no departure phrase the request did not have (below). `style_note` and `style_departure` go on the still's record, with its `style_lock_rev`; stills made before a relock stay on record under their revision. A drawing whose colours sit far from the palette is a warning only: variation is allowed.

For every beat, make a **visual argument sheet** `shot-plan.json`, each segment with ID, beat ID, its script lines (`line_start`/`line_end`, contiguous across all shots: the real timing comes from these), approximate start/end (planning only; `retime-shots` replaces them with the real narration's, 05), frame prompt, composition, what NEW thing the viewer learns/feels from the picture, continuity anchors, whether diagram/map/callout/overlay is appropriate, how a visual idea evolves across adjacent shots, and whether it requires verified imagery. `new_thing` is required on every shot (what the picture gives that the words don't); a shot without one is decoration. A shot of a beat that carries a contested, speculative or assumed value, or of a key moment whose significance is stated as contested or speculative, also says how its picture keeps that uncertainty (`uncertainty_shown`, four words or more: a reconstruction shown as one, a question left visibly open, a picture that asserts nothing about it); a callback that reuses a still needs none. It goes to the image job's direction and to the still's record. Run `shot-check` before generating stills: it refuses a shot without its beat or its `new_thing`, a shot of an uncertain beat without its `uncertainty_shown`, a beat without a picture and non-contiguous lines, and lists prompts that are nearly the same and long runs of one composition (a callback, or the same picture twice?). A deliberate callback is a shot with `"reuses": "S010"`: it shows that earlier shot's still again, so it needs no frame prompt and nothing is generated for it. A picture can also change on a word inside a line, where the sentence brings something new to see (a person named, the next item of a list, the last words of a turn): give that shot `"start_word": N` (the line's Nth word as the captions show it, from 2) and the same `line_start` as the line the shot before it ends on; `retime-shots` cuts in the pause the audio measures before that word, or just before the word when there is none. Cut on the word that brings what the new picture shows, never before it (a person seen before the words say who she is reads as someone else). `retime-shots` refuses a plan that fails it. Look for these meaningful shot families:

- **Establish / orient:** clean geography, timeline, workspace, spatial relationship, scale or environment.
- **Decision / human:** the expression, hand movement, choice, consequence, ordinary object or lived limitation that makes an abstraction concrete.
- **Explain / compare:** diagram, overlay arrows added in CapCut, contrasting models, labelled relationship, map movement, before/after or cause/effect state.
- **Reveal / reframe:** deliberate change in perspective that creates an earned “oh, that's why” moment.
- **Breathe / reflect:** an evocative image held long enough for thought, rather than continuous informational strain.
- **Callback:** return to an earlier object/detail and visibly resolve or deepen it.

No requirement that every category appear every minute. A slow map animation is valuable when tracking route/constraints; decorative sparkle is not. Select the rhythm from comparable references in the same cluster, then adapt. A 14-minute thoughtful history film needn't cut at the rate of a TikTok Short.

## Static plus subtle motion (image-led, no forced video generation)

Allowed editing tools: modest Ken Burns movement, pans, reframing, layer masks, depth planes/parallax when achievable, wipe-on annotations and hand-drawn highlight, light temperature changes, overlays, comparison split screens, maps with moving pins, animated simple shapes and purposeful captions. Use CapCut for overlays and transitions where possible; don't bake normal subtitle text, motion arrows, social stickers or heavy VFX into generated base images merely because the model can. However **visible text is allowed when it is intrinsically part of the factual scene**, such as a label on an important document, signage, diagram or needed interface. Historic or scientific data graphics should be legible, accurately laid out and checked rather than trusting model-rendered gibberish.

Music/SFX create dimension but shouldn't erase room for a quiet moment. A sound cue should have a narrative job (recognition, scale, spatial setting, surprise, transition, or an emotional punctuation). Avoid constant sound effects, templates, forced whip pans and musical crescendos every chapter. Treat narration, illustration and music as one composition.

## Drawn and animated explanations (encouraged, not an exception)

A drawn layer — lines, arrows, shapes, a building list, a growing bar, a circled detail — that relates to what is said at that moment and shows, points at, builds up or stresses it better than the still alone does is **one of the film's main tools, not decoration for the rare chart**. Plan for it at every stage: the shot plan, the stills, and the edit all look for where a drawing would teach or land something the still cannot.

**Not only numbers.** Generalize past "a statistics animation": a quantity or comparison; steps in order or a process running; a cause and what follows it; a relationship or contrast; a place, route, direction or "look here" on the picture; a timeline or a before/after; a definition or a list built item by item; a key word or name written beside the thing it names (never a line of narration — that's the captions' job); something to notice, circled, underlined or lit. Read the script beat by beat and ask what the viewer needs to see now that the still cannot show. A moment nothing drawn would show better is left to the still.

**What feels right, not piled on.** The test is whether the drawing would be missed if it were gone. A still with nothing on it is a normal, good moment; a quiet stretch isn't a gap to fill. Signs of too much: a drawing competing with the narration or captions, two animating at once, a drawing on every still for minutes with no room to breathe, a build that takes longer than the words it explains, the same kind of animation repeated with nothing new to show. Fewer, simpler and well-timed beats more. How many is the agent's call per video — never a quota, never a gate, and not a target to hit either.

**Never lie.** What a drawing states matches the narration and the facts file exactly — the same number in the same unit, a process in the source's order. A drawing never makes a result look larger or a cause stronger than the source supports.

**Show the relationship, not just the things.** When the words state a relationship (a cause and its effect, a comparison, a route, who reports to whom, what changed), the picture shows that relationship: two things side by side are not yet a comparison. Reveal it in the order the viewer needs it when order matters; a complete composition is better when the whole is the point. A still that carries atmosphere, emotion or a joke needs no diagram, and motion is never there to fill time.

**Words on screen a viewer can read.** A label, a number or a name the viewer is meant to read has to be legible at the export size on a phone, clear of the captions, and on screen long enough. timeline-check lists drawn words under 2.4 % of the frame height, inside the caption band, or shown for less than about 0.3 s a word (never under 1.5 s), as facts: enlarge the label, frame the still closer (the drawing follows the framing), move it, or keep it when it is texture, not a message.

**Where they live: CapCut, never the image generation.** The still stays a plain scene; the image model is never asked for a chart, arrow, number or word (the no-subtitle-like-text rule above still applies to every still). A drawing is a separate, animated, transparent-background clip rendered by `STUDIO/scripts/draw_tool.py` from a JSON spec (strokes in several hands, rings, ticks and crosses, words typed, decoded or handwritten, counters, stamps, paper cards, pins and string, icons travelling a route, radio pulses, hatching, Morse marks, groups with keyframed moves; each element has its own entrance timed to the word it elaborates; the full list in `09-capcut-toolkit.md`) and placed on its own overlay track in CapCut above the still (`09-capcut-toolkit.md`). When a still will carry a drawing of some size, plan room for it in the still's own prompt (a plain wall, an empty tabletop, an open floor) as part of the scene, never as "space for text" — and never with the drawing itself drawn into the image.

**A drawing sits on its still as the edit shows it, on the word it is said on**. Place each drawing by looking at its still (coordinates in frame fractions of the still), and keep one table of every still's framing in the edit (for example `70-edit/video-NN/frame-crop.json`: the scale, 1 = covering the canvas, and any shift that hides a painted paper margin or a stray detail at an edge). The moves file starts every still's move from that framing, and the drawing's spec carries the still's whole move (`still_motion`: its scale and shift at the clip's start and end, the values capcut-motion keys, linear between): the renderer moves and scales every element with the picture frame by frame, so the drawing stays on what it marks and a still with a drawing keeps its camera move. Place every element inside the part of the still the edit shows all through the move, and the drawing clip starts with its still and lasts as long (the move's clock is the clip's). Time each entrance by the word it is said on, found in Whisper's own words for that line (Whisper writes numbers as digits, "315", "2,000", and can miss a short repeated phrase), never by the word's position in the script line; a word Whisper never heard is timed from the narration's own audio and recorded with how it was measured. Move every time onto the edit's clock with the silences the edit inserted (edit-plan `holds`: `at_audio_s`, `hold_s`), and stop if the edit is longer than the narration but no inserted silence was read.

**Show the relation, not only the event.** A picture of what happens rarely shows why it matters. When a moment's significance lies in a relation (who is affected, what changes, where a force is and where it is needed, before and after, the problem and its answer), plan the picture that makes the relation visible: both places on one map, the same view before and after, a drawn path that is stopped, a counter growing against a limit, a reaction. Choose it for the moment, not by habit: an atmosphere, a face or one striking image can serve better than an explanation, and not every scene needs a diagram, a label or motion.

**Drawn animation craft**. A drawing is a small piece of motion design, and the engine gives it a full vocabulary; pick by what the moment says, never to use everything.
- *What each piece is for.* A route or a journey: an icon travelling it (a plane, a ship, an envelope, a marker dot) with its trail behind. A number: shown whole as it is said, and held. A counter runs only when the thing itself grows before the viewer (a tally filling, a distance being covered), from where it really starts, and it lands before its number is said: a stated figure counting up from zero shows wrong figures on its way ("1 shield = about 0 coins"), and a counter still running while the number is spoken reads as a wrong number. A list: items typed and ticked one by one on a card. A name or a role: a file card dropping in, typed. A verdict, a status or an order: a stamp that slams (`DECEASED`, `GO TO BRITAIN`). A doubt or a correction: the film's own handwriting (a red question mark, "too late", "here"), a tick or a cross. A signal: radio arcs, Morse marks lit with the key sound's own presses (measured from its audio, never guessed). A sequence in time: a timeline card the story moves along across shots. A group or a network: cards pinned and strung, which can also come apart. An invented thing beside a real one: the run's own convention of hands (for example ink for what was real, pencil for what was invented).
- *How it moves.* Entrances with weight: a slight overshoot that settles (back, elastic, bounce), a card that drops and rotates into place, a stamp that comes down from above, a pen tip on a line being drawn, an arrow head that rides the pen. Stagger a set (cards, squares, question marks) so the eye can follow, one focus at a time; let it settle and hold long enough to read; leave cleanly (fade, undraw, shrink, fall) before the cut when the next shot doesn't continue it. A drawing can carry on across shots (the same card on the next still, its state moved on). Words that must be read are typed or written, not decoded: scrambled letters are read as a garbled label (two of two final watch reviews, 2026-11-23); keep a scramble for texture.
- *Sizes that read.* Words a viewer must read at about 3.4 % of the frame's height or more (the check's floor is 2.4 %); handwriting larger, about 5-6 %; strokes thick enough to survive a phone (rings and crosses about 5-12 thousandths of the short side); icons at about 3-7 % of the short side. Contrast with what is under it: a paper tag (`plate`) under typed words on a busy picture, a light colour or a glow on a dark one.
- *Look before placing.* `draw_tool.py preview` composites chosen moments over the still as the edit frames it; look at every drawing that way (and at full size where something is small) before rendering, and again in the export. **Read every position off the finished still, never off its prompt**: `preview --grid` draws the still's own tenths over the tile, moved by the framing, so a position goes straight from the sheet into the spec (on the first film that placed its drawings from the prompts, nearly every position was wrong: the model puts the pile, the ground line and the window where it likes). A mark needs contrast with the ground it really lands on (a blue line on blue ground and ink on a dark wall both vanished). A mark timed on a line's last words lands on the cut and is gone in a quarter of a second: time it on the start of its phrase, or carry it onto the next shot. The caption line sits at 0.85 of the frame's height: a label, and anything in the still the viewer must see, stands above 0.78. A mark timed on a spoken number looks for it the way the timed words hold it (one word, often in figures: "35", not "thirty five"). The drawing typeface has no true minus sign (U+2212): write an en dash. The images file is written before the drawings, because a drawing carries its still's move; written the other way round, the drawing slides against its picture.
- *The render record.* `draw_tool.py render` (with an optional `--still`; by default the shot's still, as `capcut-build` finds it) writes `<name>.render-record.json` beside the .mov (09 has what it holds); a failed render leaves no record. `capcut-build` refuses a stale render (the spec, the still or the clip changed since it was rendered). `timeline-check` checks every drawing too: a stale render is an issue; a counter's final figure must be one its shot's lines say (in digits or in words), or the spec's `counter_note` (five words or more) says why; a counter that first shows another figure than the one it lands on is listed, to be looked at (true of something that grows, or a statement the film does not make?); drawn words pass the picture content rules (a `content_note` answers 02's plain words, never cut material); colours far from the locked palette are a warning.

Every content rule above still applies inside a drawing: no subtitle-like text, no sticker/emoji/icon/badge, nothing religious or superstitious, nothing indecent, gory or distressing, every figure a clearly adult one.

## Image prompt contract

Before creating an image, specify (a) exact beat/visual job (the beat's `visual_intent` and the shot's `new_thing`), (b) scene and continuity anchors, (c) primary readable subject and visual hierarchy, (d) viewpoint/framing and reserved caption space, (e) texture, look and light (the look is the style lock's, which comes from the style bible and the locked treatment's visual language; the shot's own light is in its frame prompt, or in added words with their reason in `style_note`), (f) physical and factual restrictions, (g) what must **not** appear, especially copied source overlays. (g) is a checklist for inspecting the result, never words in the prompt: the prompt describes what *is* there instead (see the rule below). Images should look like the **same film** even when topics/scenes vary; justified stylistic contrasts may be marked as such in the style bible. Verify every result with visual inspection: hands, objects, text, map boundaries, chronology, scale, logos, number of people, weapons/equipment accuracy where relevant, and whether the picture communicates the intended new idea.

For a niche with real historical artifacts/places/equipment, start by researching visual references and source provenance; either use licensed originals with attribution, or create a clearly illustrated interpretation based on grounded descriptions. Never turn model imagination into documentary evidence. For quantitative diagrams, prefer deterministic chart/map rendering over hallucinated numbers/labels.

**Nothing indecent, gory or distressing (02's rule, applied to pictures).** Every person is dressed for the scene, and the prompt names their clothes from top to bottom — a model left to guess undresses a person whose moment suggests it (a shower/bath/changing-clothes line draws bare skin unless the clothing is explicit in the prompt). A line about an undressed moment is shown by its dressed moment or by its object instead (a shower becomes the running water in an empty bathroom, or the person afterward, dressed, hair wet, towel round the neck). A medical or injury moment shows the care, never the harm: hands helping, the equipment, the patient awake with eyes open and sitting up — never a wound, a contorted face, a collapse as it happens, or a corpse. A survival pose low on the ground is drawn with the person clearly alive in it (head raised or turned so the open eyes show, mid-move). Feelings stay short of extremes (worry, alarm, relief, a frown — never agony or terror). Every person and character is drawn as a clearly adult figure (an age in years in the prompt; adult facial proportions — jaw, chin, mature features — never a round chubby child's face). The prompt names what *is* there, never what must stay out — a prompt that says "no blood" tends to draw some. The thumbnail follows the same rule: its face reads as surprise, worry or curiosity at a glance, never terror or pain.

**Content flags on prompts.** `shot-check` and every generation job read each picture prompt for 02's subjects and for what a cited reference's content review rules out (excluded, or never to be shown). What a review rules out is rewritten out of the prompt; no note answers it (`shows_what_a_content_review_rules_out`). 02's words can be plain: rewrite the scene, or give the shot (or job item) a `content_note` saying why the flagged word is not that content ("blood orange is the sky's colour; nothing is hurt"). Never answer it with a negative phrase ("no blood"): change what the picture shows. Style words added around an approved frame prompt cannot bring in a flag of their own. A prompt that shows a child (not inside a negation) is refused, and no note answers it: every person in a picture is an adult. (02 lists the words the checks flag.)

**Every picture looked at.** Before the edit passes, every picture the timeline shows is looked at whole and recorded: `content-review --run "RUN" --video NN --pictures --file looks.json`, one entry per file with `clean` or `issue` and what it shows. When the cited references' reviews rule anything out, every picture is checked against that list and the file says so (`"never_show_checked": true`); a picture showing any of it is an `issue`. A still with a `people_question` on its record also answers `"people"`: `"none"` (or no one, nobody, no people; a note may follow), or who appears. When someone appears that its prompt does not ask for, the look is an `issue` with the category `people_not_asked_for`, and the still is remade, never patched; until its look answers, `timeline-check` counts the still as not looked at. A record holds for that exact file and for the content reviews it was checked against (`themes_sha256`): a remade picture is looked at again, and when the reviews change, the timeline asks for those looks again. `release-check --thumbnail FILE` requires the thumbnail looked at clean too; without `--thumbnail` it warns that none was named. An `issue` is remade or replaced, never kept.

**One still at a time, and what to do with a fault** (the user, 2026-11-22: stills strictly one per worker run). Each job makes one still and it is looked at when it lands, so a memory-guard stop costs one picture and a fault is caught before more of the same; a heavy app opened meanwhile (CapCut holds about 2 GB) is the usual cause of a guard stop, so the next still waits for it to close. A fault in what the picture *shows* (a wrong object, a person who should not be there, a map pulled into a still life, a symbol or flag the moment does not want) is remade: the plan's `frame_prompt` reworded to describe what is there, never a negation, then a new seed, or an edit with the still itself as the reference ("keep ... exactly; change only ...") when the rest is right. Words shared by every prompt travel into every picture: a place in the style opening ("of 1940s Europe") pulled a map of Europe onto blank paper, so keep places out of it. **Nothing in a still is ever blurred, erased or covered** (the user's rule). What would need hiding is kept out by the frame prompt, which describes what is there instead, and the still is generated again: a sheet shows its plain blank back, a book lies closed in a plain cloth cover, a newspaper's print is painted as soft grey stripes with no letters at all ("too small to read" still makes the model write legible nonsense), a map is an outline of coast, rivers and hills, money sits in a tied leather wallet, a wax seal is smooth and plain, veterans are bareheaded with plain ribbon bars, a clock face has dots instead of numerals and its hands are described by direction ("the hour hand level, pointing right at the three o'clock dot"); "There is no writing, lettering or numbers anywhere in the picture." closes a prompt that needs it. Such a remake is made from the people's references, never as an edit of the flawed still (an edit keeps the lettering or the patch), and looked at whole and at full size where lettering could hide (a page, a spine, a sign, a seal, a banknote). The generator records each still's fingerprint: `timeline-check` and `release-check` refuse a still whose file changed after it was made and one that carries a blur or an erase from the touch-up log, and no reapproval accepts either. `STUDIO/scripts/still_touchup.py` only paints a small crisp mark the picture needs (a character, a shape), never over a fault, and refuses to blur or erase. A picture made or corrected elsewhere (one the user supplies; a crop, a colour or contrast correction, a retouch that makes it right) comes in with `still_touchup.py --replace FILE --shot SNNN --kind user_supplied|edited --why "..."`: checked (it opens, has the still's shape and enough pixels), kept beside the original, recorded, and looked at again. That is legitimate editing; hiding something is not.

**Try the look on real stills before the shot plan is written.** Words that read well can draw badly, and a fault in the look's shared words is in every picture: make two or three trial stills of different kinds (a place, a diagram, a person; `purpose: treatment_preview` before the lock) and look at them whole before 80 prompts are built on those words. What one full film found (2026-11-28; three re-locks of the look came from these):
- The look's words are short and visual. "Coloured only in X and Y" puts both colours into every picture; give each picture the colour of each of its areas instead ("the ground is one plain field of flat cold blue from the left edge to the right edge and down to the bottom edge").
- "On ... paper" draws a paper margin and a frame line; "the lower fifth plain" can leave a bare band; "edged in bold black" becomes a box. Say what fills the picture to each edge.
- A diagram (a cross-section, a map) is drawn straight from its prompt (`"upsample": false`): the caption writer adds colours, people and objects to a diagram. Places and scenes gain from the upsampler.
- An edit by reference keeps the framing; it can still shift the colours a little, and it puts a new thing where the picture suggests, not where the words say. Frame the pair so both places work.
- A count is not obeyed in a direct draw ("five-storey" came out as ten). When a building has to match another still, make it as an edit of that still, or describe its shape ("wider than it is tall, five rows of windows").
- **A place or a thing the film returns to is made once and edited from then on.** Four separately made stills of one power station came out as four buildings, and a final reviewer had to rely on the words to know it was the same place. Make the first still, then every later view as an edit by reference of it (or as a return to it under a new drawing); the shot plan names that first still in the later shots' `refs`.
- The film's central measure is drawn bold enough to count on a phone from the moment it is introduced (five grip marks came out as "a few thin white dashes" at 10 px; at 17 px with a dark edge they count), and the words a chart needs ("CAN HOLD", "CARRIES") are set at 4 % of the frame's height or more.
- Fewer things that invite people and lettering (a bus stop, a fence with a sign) pass the caption check the first time.
- Snow, a white floor or pale sky at a still's edge looks like a paper margin to any measure, and a frame line inside a real margin is under-read. The framing table is checked on the export: sample every shot near its start and its end, look at the edges, and reframe what shows paper.

## The director's kit (`STUDIO/scripts/director_kit.py`)

A long film is some eighty stills, eighty drawings and forty sounds, each placed on a spoken word. Nobody writes that many plan files by hand: the director writes a few short scripts of their own and keeps them **in `RUN/workbench/`, never outside the run**. A film whose makers live somewhere else cannot be resumed or revised from its own folder (the first full film's did, 2026-11-28, and had to be brought home afterwards). Those scripts hold what is this film's alone: its shots and prompts, where things are in each still, its colours, how its stills move, its cues. What is the same for every film comes from the kit, so no film builds it again:

- **When a phrase is spoken.** `Kit(RUN, NN).word_time(51, "Lift the pipes")` gives the second on the edit's clock (every decided hold added). `python director_kit.py word-time --run "RUN" --video NN --line L051 --phrase "..." --shot S036b` prints it, with the time inside that shot. A phrase that comes after a spoken figure is counted from the line's end (`rev=True`, `--rev`). Drawings and sounds are timed with it, never by eye.
- **The drawn parts.** `Overlay` builds one shot's draw_tool spec from parts: `tag`, `word`, `hand`, `readout` (a flip-number card; a value at 0 stands from the cut, which is how a read-out stays across a cut: a drawing belongs to one shot and is drawn again, already in place, in the next), `tick`, `cross`, `ring`, `arrow`, `line`, `bracket`, `grip` (bold counted marks that can let go), `panel`, `bar`, `strip`, `scale`, `wind`, `stamp`, `dots`; `d.at(line, "phrase")` times a part on its word. The palette is the film's (`paper`, `ink`, and the roles `main`, `alert`, `light`); with none given every mark is ink. `Drawings` names them in shot order, writes them, makes preview sheets with the still's grid and renders them (two or three processes on separate lists of shots: a render takes most of a minute).
- **One job and one worker run per still.** `still-jobs` writes `60-visuals/video-NN/jobs/still-SNNN.json` from the shot plan and the locked look (a section, a map, a diagram or a chart is drawn straight from its prompt; `jobs/overrides.json` changes one still, or every job under `"_job"`). `stills --run "RUN" --video NN run S001 S004` draws them one worker run each, keeps a small preview of each to look at and a line in `jobs/runs.jsonl`; `--todo N` takes the next ones whose reference stills exist. `--wait-commit-gb 15` waits until the PC has the memory the picture maker needs and says what is short, instead of letting the guard stop it half a minute in; it closes nothing and changes no setting.
- **Framing.** `Framing` measures the border a look leaves round its stills and the scale that hides it (`margins`), keeps one framing for a still and everything made from it, dissolves a variant in over its family, lets a framing set by hand win, and writes `frame-crop.json` and `capcut-images.json`. How a still moves is the film's own rule, passed in. `hide_band_y` gives the `y` that puts a band at a still's edge under the frame.
- **Sound.** `Sound` places each bed and single sound on a word or a shot, writes the two level plans and has `bed-levels` bake them into `sound-placements.json` (05, 09). `tone` makes a held tone a small speaker can play; `loop` makes an ambience long enough for its scene.
- **Looking at the export.** `frames` makes contact sheets of named shots or seconds of an exported film; `edges` lists the shots whose still shows its own border in the frame (places to look, never a verdict).

The kit decides nothing: it holds no palette, no position, no motion rule and no cue. A film that needs another kind of drawing writes its own elements (`Overlay.add`), and a film with no drawings uses none of it. The calls are in the file's own header.

## Long-form visual inventory and pacing

Prepare images/graphics for the **whole narration**, not just the opening. The references' measured rhythm (`av-profile`: shot lengths, moving share, cuts per minute, by third of the film) is evidence for choosing this film's rhythm, not a target to copy. The number of stills depends on how much a shot changes, not an arbitrary seconds-per-image average. Some 20-second explanatory diagrams work; a static single face for 75 seconds may be dead air. Use beat map and actual speech timing to decide. For every quarter of the film, confirm there are visual discoveries, explanatory imagery or a consciously justified sustained hold. Plan especially around chapter transitions, complications and the ending. Don't spend the entire image budget in the first three minutes.

**What the plan really shows.** `shot-check` counts it before a still is made (`pictures`): how many pictures the film has, how many shots show one seen before with nothing new on it (by shots and by time; a return that carries a drawing of its own is counted apart, as something new on a ground the viewer knows), the picture shown most and for how long in all, the longest a picture stands, the beats that open on a seen picture, and two-sided pictures (a split screen, a before and after) with nothing drawn on them to say which side is which. Beside it stands the plan's rhythm next to the film's models (10 §1): cuts a minute, how long a picture stands, pictures seen again. It also names the longest unbroken stretch on one picture, counting its returns and the stills edited from it by reference (`longest_on_one_picture`; a glance away of under five seconds does not break it), and from 25 s on it asks about it: shot by shot such a stretch looks varied, because each shot has a drawing of its own, while the viewer sees one picture. Where the words there are abstract (figures, studies, a list of sources), give each idea its own picture, and prefer the film's own measure and its known pictures to a new neutral ground: the only major finding of the first full film's final review was 36 s of one landscape under thin lines, in the one part that was all argument (2026-11-28). A callback is a choice and a held picture can be the strongest shot of the film; one picture standing for a fifth of the film because nothing else was planned is neither. Outside every model on cuts a minute or on pictures seen again, `shot-check` waits (and the stills gate with it) until the plan changes or `craft-choices.json` says why this film does it (07). When the answer is more pictures, plan them: a new picture costs about a minute of this PC and no money.

The edit should support 16:9 at least 1080p, keep important subjects in safe margins, use licensed local fonts only (do **not** bundle font binaries), respect low-volume playback readability, and retain enough negative space for chapter labels when those genuinely help comprehension. For publication thumbnail, develop separate 16:9 image concepts optimized for a small display, not a random frame crop.
