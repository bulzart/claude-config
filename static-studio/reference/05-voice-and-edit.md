# 05 — Narration, sound and CapCut: build the actual long-form watch

## Voice is an actor, not an audiobook metronome

The narrator should sound like a person who understands and *cares* about the subject. Delivery must track what the viewer is discovering: animated when an insight is surprising, restrained when a human consequence lands, clear in explanations, conversational around ordinary examples. Vary phrasing and pauses naturally. Do not insert monotonous sentence-ending rises, repetitive emphases, forced inspirational intonation or a dramatic growl for every fact. The script is written natively for the target language, not word-for-word translation from an English draft. Dates, names, foreign places and uncommon terms are previewed and their pronunciation handled (below).

The narrator's persona comes from the locked treatment (`direction.json`, 10): who is talking, their relationship to the viewer, their energy range and licence for humour or emotion. **Each video chooses its narrator** for its own treatment (`voice-pick`, below); videos of one series may share a voice on purpose, for its identity, and a video with a different treatment may need a different one. Nothing about a subject decides the voice: a history film may want a wry friend, a science film a hushed witness. Once a video has its narrator, every part of it is recorded with exactly that voice and those settings; a change is a deliberate revision, never a silent switch.

The narration is a performance and it is directed (00 §5, the Voice desk): each beat has an intention for the narrator, the writing is made for the mouth, and the lines the video stands on are looked at after recording and recorded again when they do not do their job (below). What holds a listener over a long watch is contrast: pace, weight and closeness change with the story. Faster is not better, louder is not more engaging and more tags are not more feeling; and one even, careful tone for a whole video is not the safe choice either: it is wallpaper, however fine the voice. A calm, thoughtful passage can be the one people remember, when it is a choice made for that passage.

## What Eleven v4 takes, and what it doesn't

From ElevenLabs' own v4 pages (verified 2026-11-20; re-read them when the model or behaviour changes): only `eleven_v4` and `eleven_v4_turbo` are used (the user's standing rule; the tools refuse any other model). Both "use two voice settings: Stability and Similarity"; "Style and Speed sliders are not available in Eleven v4, and SSML is not supported"; and "Eleven v4 doesn't apply `voice_settings.speed`". So the narration is shaped by separate things, each in its own place:

| What | Where it is decided | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| The narrator's identity | `voice-pick` (the video's voice lock) | the voice itself: character, register, and most of its pace |
| Stability | the voice lock | consistency between generations against expressive range (lower: broader emotional range; higher: steadier, can turn monotonous) |
| Similarity | the voice lock | how closely the output keeps to the voice |
| Writing | `script.json` | the performance: sentence length, punctuation, line breaks, a question, direct address |
| Delivery tags | inline in a line's `text` | a change of register where the story turns, sparingly |
| Pauses | `pause_total_s` + `pause_via` | a pause tag (performed) or an edit hold (exact) |
| Pronunciation | `pronunciation.json` | IPA or a respelling, applied only to what is sent |
| Speed | nothing | v4 has no speed control: the pace is measured, never steered |

Stability is chosen for the narrator, never to manipulate pace: the voice's real pace is measured on real recordings (a representative calibration, then the parts), and when it and the locked length disagree, the evidence goes to the user (below). No value is right for every narrator; decide it for this one, and when credits allow, hear it (a probe or a trial, below).

## Inline audio tags: chosen by the story, from the whole catalogue

`STUDIO/data/elevenlabs-audio-tags.json` holds every tag ElevenLabs documents for Eleven v4 / v4 Turbo, with what each does, an example, a pipeline status, and for pauses and reactions an approximate time cost. The file also says how tags behave and what this setup has measured. Statuses: `usable` and `use_with_care` are the agents' call; `not_for_narration` is excluded by a standing rule (distress, crude, sound effects, national accents, dialogue; the reasons are in the file), and plan-check refuses it. A tag the file does not list is allowed: the vocabulary is open (`[amused, conspiratorial]`, `[hushed, as if not to wake someone]`: whatever the treatment's narrator needs), and it is judged on the calibration part.

The catalogue is information, not a recipe, and ElevenLabs v4's expressive controls are part of every narration: each video's script carries purposeful delivery and/or pause cues where the story calls for them (the user, 2026-11-22). They are creative controls, not decoration and not a fix for unclear writing: a confusing passage is rewritten or given a better picture, never covered with a tone. There is no quota per line, minute or chapter, and a moment the sentence already carries needs nothing (a curious line does not need `[curious]`). Where a cue gets a moment's performance directly, use it: a line that should settle `[softly]`, a performed beat inside a thought `[short pause]`, a reflective turn `[thoughtful]`, restrained humour `[deadpan]`, a hushed or tense passage, the voice taking a pause and coming back `[pause, then normally]`; chosen from what is documented for the active model (the catalogue's sources) or already observed on this voice (`cue_observations`). A script with no cue at all is an exception: plan-check refuses it unless `script.json` carries `tags_exception` with the reason and the user's own approval in their words. Never regenerate a recording only to add tags: a re-recording needs a concrete improvement to the performance or the story, and then it uses them.

1. **The creative direction** (`direction.json`: the strategy's `sound_and_breath`, the locked treatment's `narrator` and `writing_voice`, and each beat's `narration_direction` in the beat map) says how *this* narration should sound and breathe.
2. **The references' measured delivery.** `pace-profile --run "RUN" --video NN` gives their pace and how they pause, on the same yardstick `narration-measure` later applies to our voice. A choice that departs from them on purpose is a creative decision, and the review says why.
3. **The script writer** (03) writes each chosen tag inline in the line's `text`, where it acts: before the words it affects. A pause cue goes before the words that follow the silence: at the start of the next line ("[pause] Nobody answered."), or before the words inside a line. ElevenLabs writes "Place a tag before the words it affects" and every example puts a pause before the next words or on its own line; a pause cue at the end of a line is undocumented. plan-check warns about one. It is sent exactly as written. Every kept tag has one line under **"Delivery and tags"** in `editorial-review.md` (the line, the tag, why this moment); plan-check refuses a tag that has none.
4. **The audio stage executes** what the script carries. It never adds, moves or drops a tag (there is no global delivery prefix). A tag that misfires goes back to the script stage as a finding.

How tags work (the catalogue's `how_tags_behave`):

- A tag carries through its line. Place a new one only where the delivery changes. Several qualities go in one bracket (`[tense, cautious]`); plan-check refuses opposite qualities on the same words (`[whispering, shouting]`, or `[calm] [excited]` stacked) and warns on stacked brackets.
- Tags depend on the voice. A delivery outside a voice's training data may be ignored, rendered oddly or read aloud. `narration-measure` checks every tag: one whose own words Whisper heard was read aloud, and blocks the edit until the part is re-recorded or the user confirms by listening.
- **Pace tags do not set the video's pace.** Measured on this setup: a pace tag had no lasting effect over a real part.
- **Pause tags cost real time inside the narration** and are fixed once recorded; plan-check adds every tag's time to the forecast (the voice's measured cost once there is one).
- Punctuation already does much of the work: a comma, an ellipsis, a dash, a line break.

## Narration as a performance, planned before generation

The words, their punctuation, the voice, any cue and what the picture and music do around them are one performance, planned before anything is paid for. For each important moment (a reveal, a joke's button, a turn, the end of a section, the ending) the script line says, with its pause when there is one (03): how the line should land (`pause_landing`), whether a silence is needed and what it gives the viewer (`pause_total_s`, `pause_purpose`), what is seen and heard while it lasts (`pause_picture`, `pause_sound`), and how the next line should start again (`pause_resume`). plan-check requires landing and resume for every pause in the whole-silence form. The vocal delivery, the length of the silence and the music's move are three separate choices that work together: a `[softly]` is a delivery, not a volume fade; a `[short pause]` or `[pause]` is a performed pause of uncertain length, not a timing command; the silence's length is decided on the recorded voice (`hold-decide --total`) and the music's move with the picture (`--bed`).

- **Scope.** Every script line is sent on its own text line, and a cue colours the rest of its own line (the catalogue's `how_tags_behave`). So a cue goes right before the words it should colour, the line ends where the change should end, and the line says so in `cue_scope` (the words it colours and where the delivery returns: "the last sentence only; the next line is back to the dry, level voice"). plan-check requires `cue_scope` on every line with a delivery cue. After recording, `narration-measure` records whether the next line carried the change on (`cue_observations`: the next line's added time and ending level); a spill is a finding for the script, never fixed by editing the audio.
- **Part boundaries are technical, never a performance change.** A part is one request to ElevenLabs. When the beat map marks the story's chapters (`chapter_start` on the first beat of each), `tts` keeps whole chapters in one part (up to about 7,000 characters sent, inside the request wait), so the only fresh start falls where the story itself starts again; neighbouring words go as `previous_text`/`next_text` either way. `narration-measure` compares each join's last landing and the next restart with this voice's usual (`seams[].boundary_effects`) and warns when a join inside a scene sounds like an ending or a fresh beginning: listen there, and re-record across that join when it does.
- **What a cue does, as evidence.** `narration-measure` keeps every cue's observed effect with the voice lock (`measured.cue_observations`: the cue, its line, voice, model and settings, the silence it left beyond the usual gap, the time it added to its own line against this voice's pace, the next line, the take), so takes can be compared and variation shows. plan-check turns them into bands (`tag_estimates`, `tag_time_range_s`, `real_forecast_range_s`): this voice's own observations first, else the catalogue's value measured on another voice as a band (x0.5-1.5), else a labelled assumption. Several cues in one passage interact: the band is not an exact sum, and the first part recorded is listened to as a whole. The pace already includes the voice's ordinary gap after each line (counted once, per line): a cue's estimate is only what it changes beyond that, in delivery as well as in silence. Existing evidence comes before any paid calibration (`narration-trial` only when a real question remains).
- **Request size and credits are not timing.** Request limits count the exact text sent (tags and pronunciation forms included). The credit estimate uses this account's verified billing (the ledger's cost headers, the `tts` dry run's `credit_estimate`), with the documented rate as the upper bound the live balance is checked against.

**The lines the video stands on.** The first line, the turn, the payoff and the last line carry more than their share: a viewer decides to stay on the first and remembers the video by the last. After the calibration part, and again when the narration is complete, draw each of them: `static_core.py voice-sheet --run "RUN" --video NN --lines L001,L060-L062` writes one sheet per line or run of lines (`50-audio/video-NN/voice-sheets/`): the pitch and the loudness every 10 ms over the words, each silence with its length, the words that were pushed (higher, louder or held longer than the words around them), how the line ends, and what its beat asked for printed above it. Read it as an editor reads a waveform, beside the beat's `narration_direction`: is the word that should carry the line one of those pushed; does the line breathe where the thought turns; does it land low where the moment closes, or lift where it hands on; does it rush through the one thing the viewer must catch? A line that does not do its job is a concrete mismatch (below): its part is recorded again (a new take differs; `--rerecord-parts`, or a `narration-trial` that compares set-ups for that passage) and the takes are read together (`voice-sheet --file takeA.mp3,takeB.mp3` draws each and stacks them on one zero line). The sheet is a drawing of the sound, never a hearing of it: it cannot show warmth, charm or whether a line is believable. Where a person's ear is offered, it decides; where it is not, the director's reading of the sheets decides and the report says nobody heard it.

**Which recordings to keep or redo.** Against a revised direction or script, a recorded part is kept when it still fits. It is redone for a concrete mismatch: changed words or structure, a delivery that works against the scene's purpose, a pronunciation or intelligibility problem, a transition into or out of a silence that does not work, a performance that differs between parts, or a new narrator or treatment that changes the whole voice. Choose the smallest coherent scope that resolves it (one part, a chapter, or everything when the script or narrator changed substantially or old and new takes would not match), with enough context around it for the performance to stay natural. Never re-record only because a passage has no tags, a narrow pitch number or a difference from a reference. What needs ears the agents do not have is prepared as a focused comparison for the user (the part files, `holds --preview`), with the measurements beside it, never instead of it.

**The approved narration.** A re-recording keeps the earlier take: `--rechunk --archive-existing --reason "..."` moves the whole folder aside (its reason stays in the new manifest's history), `--rerecord-parts` moves single parts to `superseded/`. The narration approved for production is the edit plan's `narration_for_edit` (the file and its hash, rebuilt by `retime-shots` from the current recording and the decisions in force); line times, captions, shot timing, music placement, joins and export expectations are recomputed from it, and timeline-check refuses a project carrying any other narration.

**What never reaches the voice.** A line's `text` holds the spoken words and their tags, nothing else. plan-check refuses SSML (`<break>`, `<phoneme>`: v4 doesn't support it), `{braces}`, `*asterisks*`, a stage direction in parentheses (`(whispers)` would be read aloud), a note in brackets (`[note: ...]`, `[1]`), and IPA written into the text (it belongs in the pronunciation list). A note for the team goes in the line's `note` field, which is never sent.

**Pauses: one decision, realised by the voice, by the edit, or by both as a measured residual.** The voice's own delivery is the default: its breath and its gap after each line. A pause the story wants beyond that is written in the script as the whole silence wanted (`pause_total_s`, the voice's own gap included) with `pause_via`, `pause_purpose`, `pause_picture` and `pause_sound` (03):

- `tag`: a pause tag in the sent text, for a hesitation that belongs to the performance (the narrator stops to think, a beat inside a sentence). The voice decides how long it really is; it costs credits, is fixed once recorded, and `narration-measure` reads what it produced. It reads a cue at a line's start or end on the audio's own silence there (the basis the holds and the edit use), never on Whisper's word ends alone, which can place one line's end 0.4 s late and hide a short pause. No ElevenLabs page gives a pause tag's length or a way to set one (SSML breaks are not supported on v4): the exact silence is always the edit's.
- `edit`: a hold, for a silence the picture or the moment needs (a reveal still building, a consequence landing, the ending settling). `retime-shots` adds only the difference between the whole silence decided and the gap the voice really left there. It is exact, free and adjustable after recording, which makes it easy to change, not automatically right.

**Each cue's result is judged with a tolerance, never to the millisecond** (the user, 2026-11-22: the model's own timing can't be steered exactly; close is a success). `narration-measure` gives every cue a verdict (`cue_outcomes`, kept with each `cue_observations` entry): a pause cue that carries a planned whole silence is *achieved* when its silence is within max(0.3 s, 25 %) of the plan, *short* or *long* otherwise, judged on its length and its place in the words, never on its clock time (0.8 s planned after "ashore" at 3.2-4.0 s, 0.78 s at 3.7-4.48 s: achieved). A delivery cue is *as asked* when its line moved the way the cue asks against this take's usual line (volume: speech level, 1 dB; tempo: speaking rate, 5 %; energy: pitch range, 1 st), *no clear change* or *opposite* otherwise; a cue on none of these axes ([curious]) is listened to. `holds` shows the same verdict for every silence planned through a cue (`cue_result`): an achieved one is kept as performed (`--no-hold`); a short one is topped up to the whole silence or re-recorded with the cue placed or worded better; a long one stays (the voice is never cut). Any tag that serves the moment may be used, researched in the catalogue and ElevenLabs' pages; each one is measured after recording and recorded with its verdict, so the next video plans with this voice's real responses.

One silence is realised once. In the whole-silence form (`pause_total_s`) a pause cue may carry part of it and the edit adds only what the cue's measured pause leaves (`pause_via: edit` with the cue in the text; plan-check marks the pause `residual_of_tag`, and hold-decide measures the take before anything is added): a residual, never a second pause. A pause cue is never assumed to take no time: until the line is recorded with it, the planned hold is the whole silence less the voice's usual gap and less the cue's expected pause (its time is counted once, in the narration's tag time); the recording and hold-decide then reconcile it on the real gap. In the older added-on-top form (`pause_after_s`) a cue and a hold on the same moment would stack, and plan-check refuses it; a join between recorded parts that carries a pause gets no seam hold on top, and CapCut adds nothing (the narration is placed whole, at speed 1; timeline-check). After recording, a performed pause that came out short can be topped up by a decision (the whole silence wanted, measured against what the tag produced), never by adding a second pause blind.

**After the narration is measured, every planned pause is decided on evidence.** `holds --run "RUN" --video NN` lists each one with the gap the voice really left after that line (`narration-measure` reads it from the audio: the quiet run around Whisper's boundary, which on its own is off by up to about 0.2 s), this voice's own range of gaps, the beat's pace and the words around it. `holds ... --preview Lnnn --totals 1.0,1.6` writes short previews of that moment (the voice's own gap and each whole silence named) to `70-edit/video-NN/hold-previews/Lnnn/`, with a listening guide, for the user's ears: no agent here can hear them. Then `hold-decide --line Lnnn --stage narration --total S | --no-hold --reason "..." [--listened "the user's words"]`. A decision made on a part that is later re-recorded no longer stands and is decided again. Once the stills exist, each held silence is decided again with the shot on screen while it lasts (`--stage picture`; previews then show the still): a decision made before the picture existed is a proposal the picture can overturn. A pause tag is decided the same way: kept as performed, or topped up.

**The transition into and out of a held silence is decided with it, never applied by default.** A silence feels meant when the voice lands the line before it and starts the next one again; it feels like a dropout when the voice was left hanging. `holds` gives the facts for each pause (`narration-measure` reads them from the audio; `ending`, next to this voice's usual in `endings_usual`):

- how the line ends: the pitch of its last clear vowel against the line's (below zero falls), how long and how soft its end is after that vowel (`soft_end_s`, `end_level_db`: a creaky or breathy landing shows here, because a creak cannot be pitch-read at all), and its punctuation;
- how the next line begins: its pitch reset against that ending and its lead-in before the first vowel (a breath, unvoiced consonants);
- what the silence holds (`inside_silence`): where an added silence goes, the level of the voice's own quiet there, any breath or click in it.

The choices, judged in the scene, never by rule:

- **Clean** (the default): the voice exactly as recorded. Its own ending, its own breath, an immediate stop. Often the right one: a deadpan joke or a hard reveal wants the cut to silence, not a softened one.
- **Placement**, automatic and not an effect: the added silence goes in the middle of the silence's longest quiet stretch, never into a word, a release or a breath. A breath in the second half of a silence stays with the next line, which it leads into; a release just after the last word stays with that word.
- **Fade** (`--into fade`, `--out-of fade`): only on the voice's own quiet next to the splice (its room tone), from 20 ms inside that quiet stretch to the splice, at most 0.3 s. The command refuses one that would touch a syllable, a release or a breath, or that would change nothing anyone can hear, which with clean synthetic narration is the usual case. A fade earns its place on a recording with audible room tone, where an added digital silence would sound like the air switching off.
- **The bed** (`--bed continues|enters|stops|dips|swells|none`; required at the picture stage): often the strongest lever. Music entering in the silence after a reveal, stopping for an unanswered moment, or carrying the last image to its end makes the silence part of the film. A bed that `swells` there is lifted in its own file (`bed-levels` with a `lift` over the silence: below and 09), and `timeline-check` reads back how far it really comes up. Music or effects never cover dead air.
- **Delivery and writing** (parts not yet recorded): a line meant to land before a held silence ends like it: a full stop, a short final clause, and, where a cue gets the landing directly, a tag from the catalogue (`[softly]`, `[quietly]`, `[thoughtful]`, or `[pause, then normally]` when the voice itself should take the pause), each with its line under "Delivery and tags". A recorded part is re-recorded for a transition only on the user's yes (it is paid): first decide whether a different length, the picture or the bed already serves the moment.

`holds --preview Lnnn --totals 1.3 --into clean,fade --out-of clean,fade` renders the transitions side by side (a fade that cannot be done here is listed with why, not rendered); the user's ears decide. The decision (`hold-decide ... --into --out-of --bed`) records the facts it was made on. `retime-shots` applies the fades, records each pause's times on the edit's clock (`times_in_edit`: the silence from the end of the line to the next line) for the bed's move, and checks that outside the added silences and the decided fades `narration-edit.wav` is the recording sample for sample. `timeline-check` checks that the bed enters, stops, goes on or is absent around each silence as decided (from timeline.json's music and ambience tracks). A dip or a swell is a volume move it cannot read back, so it is listed to listen to.

## Pronunciation

Names, places and unfamiliar terms the voice may say wrong go in `40-plans/video-NN/pronunciation.json`: `{"terms": [{"term": "Hagaru", "say": "/həˈɡɑːruː/", "how": "ipa", "confidence": "likely", "source": "..."}]}`. `how` is `ipa` (between forward slashes, which v4 reads inline; it is sent inside double quotes, as ElevenLabs' examples write it) or `respell` (plain letters, as the docs suggest for models without phoneme tags). `confidence` is `confirmed`, `likely` or `uncertain`, with the `source` of the first two. An uncertain entry is flagged, never treated as settled: hear it on a probe or a trial, and let the user listen.

The list changes only what is sent: `script.json`, and the captions made from it, keep the canonical spelling. Use it selectively, for the words that need it. A change to the list marks the recorded parts that say those words (`pronunciation_changed_parts`). After recording, `narration-measure` lists where each term was said and how Whisper heard it: a listening list, not a verdict (Whisper mishears names too).

## Choose the narrator: candidates, then a decision for this treatment

**Quality first, pace measured later** (the user, 2026-11-25). Choose the narrator for the treatment: its personality, charisma, naturalness, emotional range, clarity, its fit for the subject, and whether it can make both the explanatory passages and the most personal ones land. Never choose or reject a voice to fit the locked length or a planning pace: a preview's pace is a few seconds read by someone else, and a voice's real pace is measured on the calibration part and the parts after it, with its variation across the beats, its pauses and its delivery. When that real pace and the locked length truly disagree, diagnose before acting (03, "Narrator and length"): the voice, the script's density, the delivery or the length; any length change is the user's, never automatic.

1. `static_core.py voice-candidates --run "RUN" --video NN --count 5 [--search TEXT] [--design N --design-description "..." [--design-text "..."] [--execute]]`: up to 5 candidates, free from the account's voices and/or billed Voice Design. The description states the narrator the locked treatment needs: character, register, age, accent and delivery. Voice Design is billed by the characters of its preview text (three voices a call): without `--execute` the designs are only priced; with it the live balance is checked first and each call goes in the spend ledger. Each preview is transcribed and its `preview_wpm` recorded, but **a preview does not predict the real pace**. No agent here can listen: each candidate gets `measured_delivery` (pitch range and movement, loudness contrast; a short sample, indicative only). Shortlist for the treatment's narrator from the description and labels, what the preview said and that measured delivery, and give the user the listening files: each preview, and any real recording a voice already has on this model (another episode's parts). The measurements describe a short preview; they never choose. The narrator is locked on the user's listening choice (or their word that the choice is yours), never on previews, pitch numbers or an estimated pace alone (the user, 2026-11-25).
2. `static_core.py voice-pick --run "RUN" --video NN --voice-id ID --settings '{"stability":S,"similarity_boost":M}' --reason "why this voice and these settings fit this narrator" [--model-id eleven_v4|eleven_v4_turbo] [--name "..."]`: the editorial decision, after the direction is locked and the user has listened (the reason says what was heard and chosen). It writes `60-voice/video-NN/voice-lock.json` (voice, model, settings, the reason, the treatment and direction revision it was chosen for, `voice_rev`). Both settings are required; anything else (speed, style, speaker boost) is refused. A Voice Design pick is first saved to the library. Another video may use the same voice (its lock lists `same_voice_as`); a voice measured on one episode keeps its calibration for another with the same voice, model and settings.
3. A change of narrator for a video is `voice-pick --replace --reason "..."` (the old choice stays in `history`). Parts recorded with the old setup are re-recorded before the narration is complete (another voice: `tts --rechunk --archive-existing`; other settings: `tts --rerecord-changed`). A direction change to the treatment's narrator marks the voice too: choose again, or keep it with `direction-reapprove --artifact voice --reason "..."`.
4. `static_core.py voice-probe --run "RUN" --video NN [--text "a real passage with its [tags] and names"]` (optional, short, billed): the passage sent exactly as the script would send it (tags in place, pronunciation applied; `--no-pronunciation` for the plain spelling), with the video's voice lock unless another `--voice-id` and `--settings` are given. It reports the words heard back, tags that came back as words, and `measured_delivery`. Its WPM is not the real pace.

## Trials before production (optional, billed, approved first)

To learn what this voice does with this treatment before paying for the narration, `static_core.py narration-trial --run "RUN" --video NN --plan trial.json` runs a controlled comparison: a few passages chosen from the treatment's own moments (an opening, an explanation, a turn), each under a few set-ups. The plan names the passages, the variants (voice, model, stability, similarity, tags as written or none, pronunciation on or off, neighbouring context or none), the runs, and the questions; each question compares runs that differ in exactly one thing, and every run answers a question. Each passage says the delivery it is meant to have (`intended_delivery`), and the plan names the user's spending cap (`budget`: `{"id", "max_credits"}`, the credits they authorised; every plan under the same id shares it, and a later plan can't raise it: the cap is registered in `RUN/60-voice/trial-budgets.json`). Without `--execute` it is checked and priced: the expected credits (the text) and the most it could cost (the context fields too, since ElevenLabs does not document whether `previous_text`/`next_text` are billed); `--check-balance` adds the live balance and the model's credits per character, free. The plan, its cost and its questions are shown to the user, and it is recorded only after their approval.

**The first recording of a new narrator: a representative calibration** (the user, 2026-11-25). Before thousands of credits go into the narration, the smallest paid recording that shows the whole performance: a few passages of this script's own lines, together covering the plainest stretch of this script (an explanation, a step, a description) and the stretch where the treatment asks the voice for the most (a turn, a quoted document, a joke's landing), the hardest names (pronunciation applied as it will be), the tags as written, and a transition into or out of a planned silence where the script has one, each sent with its neighbouring lines as context (`context: neighbours`), as production will send it. The plan has one variant, the voice lock (`{"id": "A", "context": "neighbours"}`), and its questions compare two of these passages, so only the passage varies and every take answers one; one take a passage. Priced and shown to the user with its boundaries, recorded only on their yes; its takes are evidence and listening, not production audio. Its pace is read passage by passage, with the trial's uncertainties (a passage of 30-70 words moves several words a minute with one pause), never as one number for the film and never against the planning pace as a target. When part 1 already shows all of this, it is the calibration instead (step 3 below).

Recording goes one take at a time, and the tool keeps the money safe by itself: every request is priced again and put on the ledger before it is sent and again with its outcome, and one that would pass the user's cap is not sent. Re-running the same command continues where it stopped: a recorded take is never sent again, and a request cut off by a crash is looked up in the account history (free) instead of being paid twice. A recorded take whose request no longer matches the plan is refused: a changed plan is a new trial id. `--takes P2-A` records only the named takes this time (to see what one costs before the next).

Each take keeps its evidence in `report.json` (its exact request, its cost and its measurements; 07). For the user's ears there is a blind set: `listening/` holds each question's takes as `Q1-A.mp3`, `Q1-B.mp3` (in an order unrelated to the variants) and `listening-guide.md`, with a sheet to fill in; `answer-key.md`, one folder up, says which take is which and is opened after listening. The measurements are evidence; which take sounds more natural is for the user's ears, and no setting is changed for production until they have listened.

## ElevenLabs process; hard stop on partial narration

1. **Preflight.** `plan-check` must pass (tags valid and explained, no markup, pauses realised with their purpose, pronunciation list valid, content flags answered, forecast in range). `static_core.py tts --run "RUN" --video NN` **without** `--execute` takes the voice, model and settings from the video's voice lock (a `--voice-id`, `--model-id` or `--settings` given must equal the lock: a check, never an override). It shows the parts, the beats each one carries with their `narration_direction`, the characters to record and, when the model's rates were read for this run, the expected credits (`--read-rates` reads the model's credit rate for this forecast: a free call). With `--execute` it reads the model's credits per character from ElevenLabs (`GET /v1/models`: `character_cost_multiplier` times any `cost_discount_multiplier`; characters are not assumed to equal credits) and the live balance (`GET /v1/user/subscription`), and refuses, with nothing sent, unless the balance covers the expected credits times `--credit-safety-factor` (default 1.15) and leaves `--keep-credits`. A part's characters are its whole sent text, tags and line breaks included. Every billed request (narration, probe, trial, Voice Design) goes in `RUN/logs/elevenlabs-ledger.jsonl` with the balance just before and just after it: the real cost, measured. **A paid request is never billed twice.** Each one goes on the ledger before it is sent and again with its outcome (07 has the outcomes). OpenAI Images keeps the same record in its `images-manifest.json` (`pending`, then `ready`, `failed` or `uncertain`; a retry names the image file). A request with no known outcome is never sent again on its own. For speech (a narration part, a probe, a trial take) its audio is first looked up in the account's history (free) and taken from there when ElevenLabs finished it (recorded as `recovered_from_history`); a Voice Design call has no history to look in. Otherwise the job stops until the user has checked the account's usage; then `--retry-unreconciled ATTEMPT_ID --reason "what was checked"` allows exactly that retry, on the ledger. A process lock keeps two runs of the same video's `tts` from sending at once. `--assume-cost-ratio` (0.1-1.0) replaces ElevenLabs' rate only with a ratio measured in that ledger, and is recorded.
2. **Parts follow the story.** New parts are whole beats, short neighbouring beats together, up to about 3,500 characters (`--max-chars`, a guideline); a longer beat is split between lines where it already breathes (a planned pause), never inside a line. A join therefore falls where the story turns, an edited line re-records about one beat, and part 1 is the opening: it is the calibration only when it shows what the script asks of the voice (step 3). Each part is sent with the spoken words of its neighbours as `previous_text`/`next_text` (whole lines, tags removed), which ElevenLabs documents for continuity when generations are joined. Parts already recorded keep their boundaries.
3. **Calibrate on what the whole script asks, with the smallest paid recording that shows it** (the user, 2026-11-25). The first paid recording of a new narrator (or new settings) shows its whole performance ("Trials" above says what that takes). When part 1 holds that, it is the calibration, paid once and kept: `tts ... --max-parts 1 --execute` records only part 1 and stops. When it does not (an opening with no letters, tags or difficult names), the representative calibration under "Trials" comes first, and part 1 follows as the first production part. Either way, until this voice, model and settings has a real measured pace, the tool records only the parts it takes to reach a measurable sample (300 words, counting parts already recorded with it and not yet measured); recording one part at a time does not get round it, and `--skip-calibration` needs `--reason`, which goes in the manifest's history. Then `narration-measure` (below) and `plan-check`: its `real_forecast_s` (narration plus edit holds) against `export_window_s`. **The planning pace is a forecast, never a performance target.** When the real narration and the locked length disagree, gather the evidence (the pace per passage and beat with its natural variation, the pauses, what the beats ask) and diagnose before acting (03, "Narrator and length"): the voice, the script's density, the delivery or the length. Then take it to the user for a controlled decision: the length reapproved (`length --revise --user-approved`), a substantive change to the words of the parts not yet recorded, or another narrator. Never force the delivery to fit: no setting or tag for pace, no edit to the recorded audio, no words cut or padded only to reach the window. A hold may be shortened or dropped when its moment allows it; it is never lengthened to reach the window.
4. **Produce the rest.** The same command without `--max-parts`. Finished parts are kept and never re-billed. A failed request, a short or unreadable response, or a balance too low for the next part stops the recording there (`BLOCKED`): the same command resumes from the first missing part. Each finished part records the voice, model, settings, direction revision, the fingerprint of the exact text sent, its request id and the credits billed (`part_records`: `credits_billed`, and `recovered_from_history` when its audio came from the account history).
5. **Changing lines after recording.** Recorded parts keep their line boundaries, so editing a line changes only the part that holds it. The dry run lists `changed_recorded_parts`, `pronunciation_changed_parts`, `parts_recorded_with_another_voice_setup` and `parts_made_for_an_earlier_direction`; `tts ... --rerecord-changed --execute` re-records just those (old takes move to `superseded/`, never deleted). A part to redo after listening or a check (a line not heard, a tag read aloud) is `--rerecord-parts N --reason "..."`. Adding or removing lines breaks the boundaries and forces a full re-record (`--rechunk --archive-existing`). After a direction change to the narrator, recorded parts that still serve the treatment can be kept with `direction-reapprove --artifact narration --reason "..."`.
6. **Measure and check.** `static_core.py narration-measure --run "RUN" --video NN` transcribes each part with Whisper medium (the references' yardstick) and aligns it to the script on comparable words (a number said as words and written as digits is the same word; a name Whisper spells differently is paired by its letters). It reports:
   - the real pace while speaking and overall with every pause, the natural gap after each line, and what each tag cost; from 300+ words it writes the voice lock's `measured`;
   - **every line heard.** A line the long pass missed is listened to again on its own (Whisper medium on a short window). Found, it was a transcription miss, and its words keep the times that second listen heard (the captions follow them; `captions-plan` names any run of words that would still flash by unread); still missing, it blocks the edit until the part is re-recorded or the user confirms by listening (`--confirm L105 --reason "who listened and what they heard"`, kept until that part changes). A line of six words or more that lost a run of four or more consecutive words is listened to again on its own too, even when most of it was heard; a run still missing blocks as `narration_words_missing`, confirmed the same way;
   - **every tag performed, not read aloud** (the same block and the same confirmation);
   - possible repeated words, and **each join**: the gap against the natural one, the pace, level and pitch range on each side, words said twice across it, and `level_step_lu`, the voice's level step across it (3 s of speech on each side): a step of 3 LU or more is a warning to listen there. The recording is never edited for it; re-recording a part is the user's call;
   - each beat as heard: pace while speaking and overall, its longest gap, numbers per minute, next to what the beat asked for; and `delivery.json` (pitch range and movement, loudness contrast, rate per beat and on each side of every join, next to the references measured the same way). A beat asked to lift that measures flat is rewritten in the parts not yet recorded (10 §5). A calm, intimate narrator measures narrower than an energetic one: compare with what each beat asked for, never with a target.
   - **`pace_signals`**: evidence about the delivery against what each beat asked, never a verdict and never a fixed percentage (lower is not better, and v4 has no speed setting). Each beat is read against this narration's own median pace while speaking and its own natural variation, and against what it asked for: its `pace` words (slow, unhurried, reflective, measured, calm... / brisk, quick, urgent, fast, rapid...) or an explicit `pace_relative` (`slower`, `same` or `faster`). A signal is a beat that asked to be slower and runs clearly faster than usual, one that asked to be faster and runs clearly slower, one of the densest beats in numbers running fast (unless it asked for speed), or the whole narration outside every reference's pace (beat `overall`). A beat that asked for speed and has it is never a signal. Each signal carries its evidence (07 lists it), the locked planning pace among it as a forecast, not a target. Examine each under a "Pace" heading in `editorial-review.md`: a line naming the beat id (or `overall`), five words or more; beats with one shared cause may share a line, and the examination can conclude the pace stays. The audio gate blocks until every signal is examined (an older `line-times.json` with `pace_findings` is read the same way).

   `audio-check` then covers every line recorded, the hashes and a complete join, one voice setup throughout, every line heard, no words missing, no tag read aloud and every pace signal examined (from `narration-measure` on these exact parts: it blocks when the complete narration was not measured, or when `line-times.json` describes an earlier recording), the duration against the audio tolerance, and **narration plus the planned holds inside the export window**. An error, a partial voice, a credit cutoff or a missing part is **blocking**: a failed recording is in progress, not a shorter deliverable.
7. **Quality listen.** No agent here can hear audio. What stands in for listening: the line-by-line alignment, the re-heard lines, tags read aloud, the joins, `delivery.json`. What only ears catch (a mispronounced name, a breath, fake enthusiasm, a bad join, strange emphasis, noise) is offered to the user as an optional listening list with exact times, never a gate; once the film is cut, `sound-review` writes that list for the whole film (the listening sheet, below). Re-record only the parts that need it, with approval and credit accounting. ElevenLabs output is not deterministic.

Supported backend: ElevenLabs `POST /v1/text-to-speech/:voice_id` with `text`, `model_id`, `voice_settings` (stability, similarity_boost), `language_code` and `previous_text`/`next_text`; `GET /v1/models` and `GET /v1/user/subscription` (free) before any billed call. Model limits come from ElevenLabs (10,000 characters a request for v4). Never put the key in JSON, the ZIP, logs, scripts or `CLAUDE.md`. `--execute` is off by default so agents never spend unexpectedly, and a credit-exhausted partial result never becomes "final".

## Music and layered sound

Music and sound carry out the locked treatment's `sound` and each beat's `sound_direction` (beat map): whether there is a bed at all, where it changes, where it drops to silence, what a single sound is for. They are decided there, with the story, not here. Use an instrumental bed when it suits this story and adds value. For instance, a reflective passage may want it almost subliminal, an exciting reconstruction a gradual texture change around meaningful turns, a demonstration clarity over score; the references' measured level under the voice (`av-profile`) shows what their audiences are used to, not what this film must do. Never drown speech. Distinguish neutral atmosphere, story-world sound, illustrative SFX, and musical punctuation. Silence can be an intentional dramatic tool. If using ACE-Step 1.5, ensure its environment exists and output is instrumental; validate duration, clipping, license and whether loops sound repetitive. Never fabricate unavailable sound layers. Keep an audible narration bed with consistent loudness across chapters and on small speakers. A bed is made through the gated music worker (04): each track names the beats it scores, and `timeline.json` records it under `audio_tracks` with those beats. **How loud a bed sits is set from measurements, not by ear-guessing a slider** (2026-11-22): the references' `av-profile` gives the level under their voice (`gap_level_rel_speech_db` per window: a reference with a bed shows it, one near -45 dB has none); measure each generated track's level over the stretch where it sounds and the narration's speech level the same way, and bake into a copy of each file the gain that puts the bed that many dB under the voice (`bed-levels`, 09: CapCut's export ignores a clip's volume), chosen per track for its moment (the one bed meant to be felt a little higher, a calm verdict a little lower), with the reason kept beside the numbers. `bed-levels` answers with the context for that choice (`context`): what the film's models play between their words, and the one listening study on the question (television documentaries, 22 listeners: commentary at least 10 LU over the music, and up to 15 still pleasing; Torcoli and others, 2019). Context, never a rule. **A bed is heard where nobody speaks.** One level for a whole film leaves the music as quiet in a silence as under a sentence, so it is never really heard. Give a track a `lift` (09): it rises by the dB you choose wherever the voice is silent for at least the seconds you name, and at the moments you name (a key moment landing, a chapter turn, the ending), with its reason; the rise ramps inside each silence and is back at its level under the next word. A lift needs room: a whole rise takes about a second and a half with no voice (up and back), so under narration that never stops there is nothing to rise in, and `bed-levels` says how much room each track had (`lift.full_rises`, and a `note` when no silence was long enough). Where the music is meant to be heard, plan that room first: a held silence the music carries, music that enters before the words, or music that plays on after the last. A sound effect under words sits well under the voice (a radio tone shares the speech band); one that plays in a silence can be clearly heard. `export-check` then reports the real level under the voice in the export, and `sound-review --file` reports it for each piece of music where it plays (below): the measurements that count.

**What the music tools really give (found on a full film, 2026-11-28).** ACE-Step turns "one sustained low note" into struck or plucked notes: a held tone that must not move (a ground tone, a room) is synthesized instead and recorded as such. A piece played again in other beats than the ones it was made for has that reuse written into its record (`music-provenance.json`: the beats, and a note saying why), or `timeline-check` reports it as playing outside its beats. After any retime, a bed's start inside its file plus its length must still fit the file. Where two beds lie under one held silence, the swell is baked into the one that is meant to be heard; `timeline-check` measures every bed under the silence and reports the largest move. **Most viewers hear the film on a small speaker.** A phone or a laptop gives almost nothing under 200 Hz, so a tone built on 55 and 110 Hz measures as a bed and plays as silence: the part it carries alone and every held silence it fills are bare voice there. A held tone gets overtones from 220 Hz up; `sound-review` lists any placed sound with under a twentieth of its energy above 200 Hz. A bought or generated sound is looked at before it is placed (its level over time and where its energy lies): a "steam hiss" arrived as nine tenths rumble under 40 Hz, and `bed-levels`, which sets the gain from overall loudness, then put it 5 dB under the voice where 17 was asked. `sound-review` now lists a bed that plays 6 dB or more above the level it was baked for. A delivery cue such as `[quietly]` changes how a line is said, not how loud its file is: the level of a line that must sit back is read after recording, never assumed.

**Sounds placed by the story, not by the clock.** A bed's entrance and exit and every single sound belong to a moment of the narration (a line's first or last word, or the silence after a line). Record them that way: `static_core.py sound-anchors --run "RUN" --video NN --file <placements.json> --to-anchors` writes each time field as an offset from its narration landmark (`at_anchor`, `end_anchor`); after a re-recording, a new or changed hold or a retime, `--resolve` moves every placement with its moment, and the project is built from the resolved times. Check the result where a sound must land exactly (a chord on a word, a stop inside a silence).

**The sound of the cut, measured, and the few passages worth a person's ears** (2026-11-26). No agent can hear. `static_core.py sound-review --run "RUN" --video NN` measures, from the run's own files, what the sound of the checked project does: each piece of music or ambience against the voice where it plays (how many dB under it, and how long it is heard with no voice over it), how much of the film has music and the long stretches with none (ambience is counted apart: a room tone under everything is not music), a sound that stops before its file ends while still sounding between two lines (a cut-off; one that plays to the film's last frame ends with the film), the air before each chapter's first line and each key moment beside the film's ordinary gap between lines, the fastest and slowest lines, the names the transcript did not recognise (a name heard as written, as written with a possessive s, or as its planned respelling was said as planned and is not listed; the least recognised come first), and the held silences nobody has listened to. It also says whether music is ever heard with no voice over it and how far it rises there above its level under the words (`music_heard_alone`), and what the whole mix plays in the voice's pauses, read exactly as the references' gap level is (`sound_between_the_words`), beside the film's models (`beside_the_models`, 10 §1): a mix far under every model, or one that never moves while theirs do, is changed or given its reason (`craft_choices_asked`; `release-check` asks for it on the finished file). Run it after `timeline-check` and read its `look_at` and `turns` as places to examine, never as faults; change what is wrong before the export, because an export costs a CapCut run. After mastering, run it again with `--file` on the mastered file: that file is then measured wherever no voice covers a sound and set beside what all the project's sounds playing there ask for together (sounds overlap: a faint piece of music over a room tone is heard alone in no file), and it says when the file plays a passage 6 dB or more away from that (a level the project's read-back does not carry, or another build). Its `file_against_project` gives the number of passages measured and the largest difference: the short answer to "does this file play what the project asks". `release-check` asks for the review of the exact file it releases, and the final reviewer is given the same facts as times, kinds and levels (06).

It also writes the listening sheet (`80-deliverables/video-NN/listening-sheet.md`, and a `.json` to fill in): a short list of passages, each with its time in the film and one question: the opening, each key moment's landing, the names the transcript recognised least, the quietest music and the least quiet sound the words must be heard over, a cut-off, a chapter turn with little air, a silence nobody listened to, the fastest line, the ending. Give it to the user with the film. It is short on purpose: a person's attention is the scarce thing. What they say is recorded in their own words: `human-review --kind listened --user-words "..." --answers listening-sheet.json`. A listen is offered, never required and never implied: `release-check`, `deliverable` and `status` say whether one is on record.

What it is not: a judge. It uses no model and gives no verdict on how a voice sounds, whether the music suits the story or whether a pause works. Every figure is a level, a time or a count, and every question on the sheet is for a person. On a finished production pass `--out DIR`: the record and the sheet are written there and the run is only read.

## Editing into CapCut

The picture is generated as *clean stills*. Add editable captions, arrows, highlights, chapter cards, lower thirds, diagrams, labels, controlled zoom/crop, simple wipes and optional licensed overlays in CapCut **only when helpful**. Social media captions/badges/emoji are not defaults. No static rule requires constant movement; an image can hold when it carries thought. Resist transitions that create discontinuities; vary macro shot family because a narrative turns, not just because a timer expired.

On this PC the project is written with `STUDIO/scripts/capcut_tools.py` next to the MCP and capcut-cli, and exported from CapCut's own window with `capcut_desk.py`; the commands, their order and the files they read are in `STUDIO/reference/09-capcut-toolkit.md`. Look the MCP's and the CLI's real tool names up before using them: do **not** fabricate tool calls or assume there is a CapCut render/export API. If a tool fails, use a supported route or stop with a clear blocker; never call a JSON manifest an exported edit.

**Captions: the user's pick at the start, never switched.** `state.json` holds `captions` (`on`/`off`) and `caption_style`, asked once in the start dialog (`captions-choice`): `luckiest-guy` (Luckiest Guy, capitals, white with a black outline and a shadow, three words a caption, the spoken word on a rounded colour box; the box colour is chosen for each video from its own palette, standing out from the pictures behind the line, with the reason) or `montserrat-highlight` (Montserrat Bold, white with a black outline, no box, no shadow; one caption line at a time, the word being spoken yellow `#FFFF00` and every other word white), or `off`: no caption at all. The production agent never changes the choice; the user can (`captions-choice --change "why"`). The words are the script's canonical text (tags never shown; the pronunciation list never reaches a caption), and each word's time is Whisper medium's time for it in the recorded narration, moved onto the edit's timeline by every hold `retime-shots` inserted; never a cue's duration divided among its words. How each look breaks its lines and lights its words (never across a sentence end or a real pause) is the code's (`captions.py`), not a choice to make. `captions-plan` writes `70-edit/video-NN/captions.json`; `captions-build --draft` writes the tracks with CapCut closed and reads them back; `timeline-check` reads them back again from the project named in `timeline.json`: the chosen look, every word shown in order and lit (or boxed) while it is spoken, and, with captions off, no caption track. `captions-preview` draws a few frames as PNGs to look at (PIL, not CapCut's renderer). The fonts are the library's own files (`STUDIO/fonts/`), checked by name: a missing or substituted font stops the build. Languages written without spaces between words are not captioned by these looks.

**Time the pictures to the real narration before placing them.** The shot plan was written against the planned timings, and the real narration never matches them. `static_core.py retime-shots --run "RUN" --video NN`:
- puts every cut in the silence between the last line of one shot and the first line of the next (the silence measured in the audio), from `narration-measure`'s line times; a shot with `start_word` (04) cuts in on that word instead, in the middle of the pause the audio measures before it (or 0.03 s before the word when there is none); it refuses while a line is not heard or a tag was read aloud;
- refuses while any planned pause is undecided or its decision no longer stands (`holds`, `hold-decide`): nothing is inserted from a length written before the voice existed;
- for each decided pause, measures the gap the voice left in `narration.wav` itself and adds only the difference with the whole silence decided (nothing when the voice already gives it), in the middle of that silence's longest quiet stretch, so no word, release or breath is touched and a breath stays with the line it belongs to, applies the decided fades (on room tone only), and keeps the silence on the shot before the cut; at each join between recorded parts that came out shorter than the voice's natural gap, a seam hold of exactly the missing fraction of a second (continuity, never padding; none where the join carries a decided pause; `--no-seam-holds` leaves them out);
- refuses when the narration ends short of the export window and the held silence is what reaches it;
- verifies `narration-edit.wav` with the same measure: its length is the narration plus exactly the added silence, every held silence measures the whole silence decided (within 0.06 s), and outside the added silences and decided fades the voice is the recording sample for sample (no resampling, no shift); otherwise nothing is written;
- rewrites `shot-plan.json`'s `start_s`/`end_s` (the planned values are kept once as `planned_*`), writes `70-edit/video-NN/edit-plan.json` (`holds`: each inserted silence with the voice's gap, the whole silence decided and the one measured; `pauses`: every decided pause and what it became; the decisions it was timed from) and, whenever there are holds, `50-audio/video-NN/narration-edit.wav` (the narration with its holds);
- records `narration_for_edit` in the edit plan: the one audio file the project must carry, with its content hash;
- carries what the editor carries out: the locked treatment's visual language, sound, audiovisual relationship and structure and pace (`direction`, with its revision), each beat's span, and each shot's beat direction. It refuses while the direction, the beat map, the shot plan or the narration was left behind by a direction change.

The project then places `narration_for_edit.file` at 0 s, at speed 1, untrimmed, and each still at its shot's times. Shots the retiming made very short or very long are listed in the edit plan's `notes`, for the director to split, merge or keep.

Write `70-edit/video-NN/timeline.json` **derived from the actual CapCut timeline**, not a fictional plan, with `canvas` (e.g. `1920x1080`), `capcut_draft` (the project folder: the narration is then read from the project itself), `visual_segments` in chronological order, each `{ "start_s": ..., "end_s": ..., "shot_id": "S001", "asset_path": "...", "beat_id": "B01", "purpose": "..." }` (`asset_path` is the picture file CapCut really shows), an `audio_tracks` array (the narration as `{"kind": "narration", "file": ..., "start_s": 0, "end_s": ..., "source_start_s": 0, "source_end_s": ..., "speed": 1}`, and every other sound with its `kind`, `file`, `start_s`, `end_s` and the `beats` it serves), transitions, labels, note on project path and export status. No section should vanish because only enough pictures/audio were rendered for the opening. The actual last narrated beat and the final held frame must reach the locked story destination. Run:

```bash
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" timeline-check --run "RUN" --video 1
```

It checks contiguous visual range, beat links, story purposes, 16:9 canvas and successful audio gate, and the trace back to the plan: each clip is a shot of the current edit plan, for that shot's beat, starting at its retimed time and showing that shot's own still (or the one a callback reuses) made under the current direction; every shot is in the cut; each sound names its beats and plays inside them; music comes from the gated worker, its file unchanged since it was made; the edit plan was timed from the hold decisions in force, and every held silence was decided with its picture (the still on screen while it lasts, unchanged since) and has the bed decided for it in the project (it enters, stops, goes on or is absent there; a dip or a swell is listed to listen to). **The narration in the cut is the approved one, whole:** the edit plan's file, unchanged since the retime, starting at 0 s, at speed 1 (no curve speed), its segments covering the file exactly once from start to end. A narration sped up, slowed, trimmed, cut or replaced is refused even when the project still has the right length. It also refuses a `timeline.json` that no longer matches the saved project (run `timeline-write` again). When it passes, it records the saved project as it shows (`70-edit/video-NN/timeline-check.json`: a snapshot of the pictures, the overlays, every sound clip with its speed and volume, the texts, the canvas, the fps and the media fingerprints); the export is held to that project (below). It does not substitute for playing through the project. Avoid black tails after the main edit and accidental CapCut "extra two seconds" tracks. A picture can intentionally lead/lag narration; the project duration should still respect the length contract.

## Export is a separate deliverable, with separate proof

Default YouTube landscape 1920×1080 or higher 16:9, 30 fps unless the project actually requires a deliberate alternative. For an accessible `output.mp4`:

```bash
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" export-check \
  --run "RUN" --video 1 --file "C:/path/to/output.mp4" --deep
```

The checker uses `ffprobe` for the true exported duration, picture and sound streams, size and aspect, and holds the export to the run's exact size and frame rate (a copy at another size is a review copy, not the master). It holds the export to the project `timeline-check` recorded: unchanged since that check (CapCut's own frame snapping aside), and the export's length equal to the project's within 0.15 s. It finds the approved narration (`narration_for_edit`) inside the export's sound in windows that tile the whole film: a stretch not found, a timing that drifts (a speed change) or jumps (a cut or an insert) is refused; the level of the music and sound under the voice is reported, with a warning when it sits close enough to mask words. With `--deep` it also scans for black and silent stretches: each blocks unless the edit plan's held silences or `70-edit/video-NN/planned-blanks.json` hold it (`{"blanks": [{"from_s", "to_s", "kind": "black"|"silence", "why"}]}`, the `why` in four words or more: an intentional black or silence is planned, never assumed). It also matches the export's picture at each shot against the project's stills by keypoints: a frame clearly showing another still blocks, a frame it cannot read (a full-frame card, a dark frame) is a warning. It records the file by its full sha256 in `80-deliverables/video-NN/export-provenance.json`, with the project it was checked against. Look at the opening, the cuts, the maps and the chapter boundaries on the watch sheets, sample a few intervals in every chapter and the final minute. No agent can listen: the sound is measured (`sound-review --file`, above) and the listening is the user's, from the listening sheet; never write that a file was listened to unless `human-review` holds the user's words. Verify exact titles/captions/names. Don't declare export verification complete when the file isn't accessible. Record export size, duration, settings and any unresolved issues in `80-deliverables/video-NN-release.md`.

**Master the sound to the level the niche's videos play at, before export-check.** YouTube turns a loud video down but never turns a quiet one up, so a mix that keeps the narration's headroom (around -23 LUFS) plays far quieter than the videos around it. `static_core.py master-sound --run "RUN" --video NN --file "RUN/80-deliverables/video-NN/export/<slug>.mp4" --out "RUN/80-deliverables/video-NN/<slug>.mp4" --target-lufs T --refs V006,V030 --why "..."` measures the references you name (the length lock's, or the niche's), applies one gain and a 4x oversampled look-ahead peak limiter over the whole mix (ceiling -2 dBTP by default; no compressor, so the voice and the beds keep their balance), copies the picture untouched, keeps the export, and records the measurements, how hard the limiter worked (the share of sound it took down by more than 1, 3 and 6 dB) and your reason in `80-deliverables/video-NN/master.json`. Pick the target from what the references measure; when the limiter worked hard, offer the user a listen. The mastered file is the one `export-check`, the final watch review and `release-check` read: `release-check` requires that exact file (by its full sha256) deep-checked with no issues, its project unchanged since, and the file the recorded master (`master.json`), never the unmastered export. Each check measures the file's own loudness (`loudness`, and `file_is`: the master, the unmastered export, or a file no record knows): a master that no longer measures its recorded target is refused, and `deliverable --file F` says what any file is. Then `sound-review --file` on the mastered file (above): the sound measured in the file the audience gets, and the listening sheet for the user.
