# 05 — Narration, sound and CapCut: build the actual long-form watch

## Voice is an actor, not an audiobook metronome

The narrator should sound like a person who understands and *cares* about the subject. Delivery must track what the viewer is discovering: animated when an insight is surprising, restrained when a human consequence lands, clear in explanations, conversational around ordinary examples. Vary phrasing and pauses naturally. Do not insert monotonous sentence-ending rises, repetitive emphases, forced inspirational intonation or a dramatic growl for every fact. The script is written natively for the target language, not word-for-word translation from an English draft. Dates, names, foreign places and uncommon terms are previewed and their pronunciation handled (below).

The narrator's persona comes from the locked treatment (`direction.json`, 10): who is talking, their relationship to the viewer, their energy range and licence for humour or emotion. **Each video chooses its narrator** for its own treatment (`voice-pick`, below); videos of one series may share a voice on purpose, for its identity, and a video with a different treatment may need a different one. Nothing about a subject decides the voice: a history film may want a wry friend, a science film a hushed witness. Once a video has its narrator, every part of it is recorded with exactly that voice and those settings; a change is a deliberate revision, never a silent switch.

Faster is not better, louder is not more engaging, and more tags are not more feeling. A calm, thoughtful voice can hold a long watch; a restrained line often lands harder than a dramatic one.

## What Eleven v4 takes, and what it doesn't

From ElevenLabs' own v4 pages (verified 2026-11-20; re-read them when the model or behaviour changes): only `eleven_v4` and `eleven_v4_turbo` are used (the user's standing rule; the tools refuse any other model). Both "use two voice settings: Stability and Similarity"; "Style and Speed sliders are not available in Eleven v4, and SSML is not supported"; and "Eleven v4 doesn't apply `voice_settings.speed`". So the narration is shaped by separate things, each in its own place:

| What | Where it is decided | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| The narrator's identity | `voice-pick` (the video's voice lock) | the voice itself: character, register, and most of its pace |
| Stability | the voice lock | consistency between generations against expressive range (lower: broader emotional range; higher: steadier, can turn monotonous) |
| Similarity | the voice lock | how closely the output keeps to the voice |
| Writing | `script.json` | the performance: sentence length, punctuation, line breaks, a question, direct address |
| Delivery tags | inline in a line's `text` | a change of register where the story turns, sparingly |
| Pauses | `pause_after_s` + `pause_via` | a pause tag (performed) or an edit hold (exact) |
| Pronunciation | `pronunciation.json` | IPA or a respelling, applied only to what is sent |
| Speed | nothing | v4 has no speed control: the pace is measured, never steered |

Stability is chosen for the narrator, never to manipulate pace: the voice's real pace is measured on its first part and the script is sized to it. No value is right for every narrator; decide it for this one, and when credits allow, hear it (a probe or a trial, below).

## Inline audio tags: chosen by the story, from the whole catalogue

`STUDIO/data/elevenlabs-audio-tags.json` holds every tag ElevenLabs documents for Eleven v4 / v4 Turbo, with what each does, an example, a pipeline status, and for pauses and reactions an approximate time cost. The file also says how tags behave and what this setup has measured. Statuses: `usable` and `use_with_care` are the agents' call; `not_for_narration` is excluded by a standing rule (distress, crude, sound effects, national accents, dialogue; the reasons are in the file), and plan-check refuses it. A tag the file does not list is allowed: the vocabulary is open (`[amused, conspiratorial]`, `[hushed, as if not to wake someone]`: whatever the treatment's narrator needs), and it is judged on the calibration part.

The catalogue is information, not a recipe. No tag is ever required, and there is no quota. A curious moment does not need `[curious]`: the sentence can be curious. A revelation does not need shouting or a long pause. Who decides, and from what:

1. **The creative direction** (`direction.json`: the strategy's `sound_and_breath`, the locked treatment's `narrator` and `writing_voice`, and each beat's `narration_direction` in the beat map) says how *this* narration should sound and breathe.
2. **The references' measured delivery.** `pace-profile --run "RUN" --video NN` gives their pace and how they pause, on the same yardstick `narration-measure` later applies to our voice. A choice that departs from them on purpose is a creative decision, and the review says why.
3. **The script writer** (03) writes each chosen tag inline in the line's `text`, where it acts: before the words it colours, or at a line start or end for a pause. It is sent exactly as written. Every kept tag has one line under **"Delivery and tags"** in `editorial-review.md` (the line, the tag, why this moment); plan-check refuses a tag that has none.
4. **The audio stage executes** what the script carries. It never adds, moves or drops a tag (there is no global delivery prefix). A tag that misfires goes back to the script stage as a finding.

How tags work (the catalogue's `how_tags_behave`):

- A tag carries through its line. Place a new one only where the delivery changes. Several qualities go in one bracket (`[tense, cautious]`); plan-check refuses opposite qualities on the same words (`[whispering, shouting]`, or `[calm] [excited]` stacked) and warns on stacked brackets.
- Tags depend on the voice. A delivery outside a voice's training data may be ignored, rendered oddly or read aloud. `narration-measure` checks every tag: one whose own words Whisper heard was read aloud, and blocks the edit until the part is re-recorded or the user confirms by listening.
- **Pace tags do not set the video's pace.** Measured on this setup: a pace tag had no lasting effect over a real part.
- **Pause tags cost real time inside the narration** and are fixed once recorded; plan-check adds every tag's time to the forecast (the voice's measured cost once there is one).
- Punctuation already does much of the work: a comma, an ellipsis, a dash, a line break.

**What never reaches the voice.** A line's `text` holds the spoken words and their tags, nothing else. plan-check refuses SSML (`<break>`, `<phoneme>`: v4 doesn't support it), `{braces}`, `*asterisks*`, a stage direction in parentheses (`(whispers)` would be read aloud), a note in brackets (`[note: ...]`, `[1]`), and IPA written into the text (it belongs in the pronunciation list). A note for the team goes in the line's `note` field, which is never sent.

**Pauses: one decision, two ways to realise it.** A pause the story wants that is longer than the voice's own gap after a line (about 0.4 s) is written in the script as `pause_after_s` with `pause_via` and `pause_purpose` (03):

- `tag`: a pause tag in the sent text, so the voice performs the pause. It costs credits and is fixed once recorded.
- `edit`: a hold. `retime-shots` inserts silence there and holds the picture. It is exact, free, and adjustable after recording.

plan-check refuses a pause over 1 s that names neither, or that doesn't say what it is for.

## Pronunciation

Names, places and unfamiliar terms the voice may say wrong go in `40-plans/video-NN/pronunciation.json`: `{"terms": [{"term": "Hagaru", "say": "/həˈɡɑːruː/", "how": "ipa", "confidence": "likely", "source": "..."}]}`. `how` is `ipa` (between forward slashes, which v4 reads inline; it is sent inside double quotes, as ElevenLabs' examples write it) or `respell` (plain letters, as the docs suggest for models without phoneme tags). `confidence` is `confirmed`, `likely` or `uncertain`, with the `source` of the first two. An uncertain entry is flagged, never treated as settled: hear it on a probe or a trial, and let the user listen.

The list changes only what is sent: `script.json`, and the captions made from it, keep the canonical spelling. Use it selectively, for the words that need it. A change to the list marks the recorded parts that say those words (`pronunciation_changed_parts`). After recording, `narration-measure` lists where each term was said and how Whisper heard it: a listening list, not a verdict (Whisper mishears names too).

## Choose the narrator: candidates, then a decision for this treatment

1. `static_core.py voice-candidates --run "RUN" --video NN --count 5 [--search TEXT] [--design N --design-description "..." [--design-text "..."] [--execute]]`: up to 5 candidates, free from the account's voices and/or billed Voice Design. The description states the narrator the locked treatment needs: character, register, age, accent and delivery. Voice Design is billed by the characters of its preview text (three voices a call): without `--execute` the designs are only priced; with it the live balance is checked first and each call goes in the spend ledger. Each preview is transcribed and its `preview_wpm` recorded, but **a preview does not predict the real pace**. No agent here can listen: each candidate gets `measured_delivery` (pitch range and movement, loudness contrast; a short sample, indicative only). Choose for the treatment's narrator from the description and labels, what the preview said and that measured delivery; list the files for the user, who may listen and overrule.
2. `static_core.py voice-pick --run "RUN" --video NN --voice-id ID --settings '{"stability":S,"similarity_boost":M}' --reason "why this voice and these settings fit this narrator" [--model-id eleven_v4|eleven_v4_turbo] [--name "..."]`: the editorial decision, after the direction is locked. It writes `60-voice/video-NN/voice-lock.json` (voice, model, settings, the reason, the treatment and direction revision it was chosen for, `voice_rev`). Both settings are required; anything else (speed, style, speaker boost) is refused. A Voice Design pick is first saved to the library. Another video may use the same voice (its lock lists `same_voice_as`); a voice measured on one episode keeps its calibration for another with the same voice, model and settings.
3. A change of narrator for a video is `voice-pick --replace --reason "..."` (the old choice stays in `history`). Parts recorded with the old setup are re-recorded before the narration is complete (another voice: `tts --rechunk --archive-existing`; other settings: `tts --rerecord-changed`). A direction change to the treatment's narrator marks the voice too: choose again, or keep it with `direction-reapprove --artifact voice --reason "..."`.
4. `static_core.py voice-probe --run "RUN" --video NN [--text "a real passage with its [tags] and names"]` (optional, short, billed): the passage sent exactly as the script would send it (tags in place, pronunciation applied; `--no-pronunciation` for the plain spelling), with the video's voice lock unless another `--voice-id` and `--settings` are given. It reports the words heard back, tags that came back as words, and `measured_delivery`. Its WPM is not the real pace.

## Trials before production (optional, billed, approved first)

To learn what this voice does with this treatment before paying for the narration, `static_core.py narration-trial --run "RUN" --video NN --plan trial.json` runs a controlled comparison: a few passages chosen from the treatment's own moments (an opening, an explanation, a turn), each under a few set-ups. The plan names the passages, the variants (voice, model, stability, similarity, tags as written or none, pronunciation on or off, neighbouring context or none), the runs, and the questions; each question compares runs that differ in exactly one thing, and every run answers a question. Each passage says the delivery it is meant to have (`intended_delivery`), and the plan names the user's spending cap (`budget`: `{"id", "max_credits"}`, the credits they authorised; every plan under the same id shares it, and a later plan can't raise it: the cap is registered in `RUN/60-voice/trial-budgets.json`). Without `--execute` it is checked and priced: the expected credits (the text) and the most it could cost (the context fields too, since ElevenLabs does not document whether `previous_text`/`next_text` are billed); `--check-balance` adds the live balance and the model's credits per character, free. The plan, its cost and its questions are shown to the user, and it is recorded only after their approval.

Recording goes one take at a time, never two at once (`trial.lock`). Before each request the price is read again, the live balance must cover it, and the budget must still cover its largest possible charge, counted from the ledger with every earlier request under that budget: failures, retries and resumed sessions included. A request that would pass the cap is not sent: the command stops and says so. Every request is on the ledger before it is sent (a `pending` row) and again with its outcome: ElevenLabs' `character-cost` reply header, the balance drop, and `credits_billed`, the larger of the two. A take is saved and put on record the moment it arrives, then measured. Re-running the same command continues where it stopped: a recorded take is never sent again (each is matched by the fingerprint of its exact request), a take saved but not measured is measured, and a request cut off by a crash or a power cut is looked up in the account history (free) and its audio taken from there instead of paying twice; until then the budget counts it at its largest possible charge. A recorded take whose request no longer matches the plan is refused: a changed plan is a new trial id. `--takes P2-A` records only the named takes this time (to see what one costs before the next).

Each take keeps its evidence in `report.json`: the exact text sent with its tags, the context fields, model, voice, both settings, the pronunciation forms applied, the request's fingerprint, the direction it was made under, its cost, and its measurements (duration, active and overall WPM, every pause of 0.3 s or more and where it falls, pitch range and movement, loudness contrast, integrated loudness and true peak, every line heard, tags read aloud, names as heard), the anomalies seen and what the numbers cannot tell. For the user's ears there is a blind set: `listening/` holds each question's takes as `Q1-A.mp3`, `Q1-B.mp3` (copies, in an order fixed by the trial, unrelated to the variants) and `listening-guide.md`: the lines, what each moment is meant to feel like, a neutral prompt per question and a sheet to fill in (natural, flow, charisma, expressiveness, fit, theatrical, monotonous, pauses, pronunciation, continuity, enjoyment, would keep listening). `answer-key.md`, one folder up, says which take is which, with each take's settings and measurements; it is opened after listening. The measurements are evidence; which take sounds more natural is for the user's ears, and no setting is changed for production until they have listened.

## ElevenLabs process; hard stop on partial narration

1. **Preflight.** `plan-check` must pass (tags valid and explained, no markup, pauses realised with their purpose, pronunciation list valid, content flags answered, forecast in range). `static_core.py tts --run "RUN" --video NN` **without** `--execute` takes the voice, model and settings from the video's voice lock (a `--voice-id`, `--model-id` or `--settings` given must equal the lock: a check, never an override). It shows the parts, the beats each one carries with their `narration_direction`, the characters to record and, when the model's rates were read for this run, the expected credits. With `--execute` it reads the model's credits per character from ElevenLabs (`GET /v1/models`: `character_cost_multiplier` times any `cost_discount_multiplier`; characters are not assumed to equal credits) and the live balance (`GET /v1/user/subscription`), and refuses, with nothing sent, unless the balance covers the expected credits times `--credit-safety-factor` (default 1.15) and leaves `--keep-credits`. A part's characters are its whole sent text, tags and line breaks included. Every billed request (narration, probe, trial, Voice Design) goes in `RUN/logs/elevenlabs-ledger.jsonl` with the balance just before and just after it: the real cost, measured. `--assume-cost-ratio` (0.1-1.0) replaces ElevenLabs' rate only with a ratio measured in that ledger, and is recorded.
2. **Parts follow the story.** New parts are whole beats, short neighbouring beats together, up to about 3,500 characters (`--max-chars`, a guideline); a longer beat is split between lines where it already breathes (a planned pause), never inside a line. A join therefore falls where the story turns, an edited line re-records about one beat, and the calibration part is the opening. Each part is sent with the spoken words of its neighbours as `previous_text`/`next_text` (whole lines, tags removed), which ElevenLabs documents for continuity when generations are joined. Parts already recorded keep their boundaries.
3. **Calibrate on the first real part, never a throwaway sample.** `tts ... --max-parts 1 --execute` records only part 1 and stops. The tool refuses several parts before this voice, model and settings has a real measured pace (`--skip-calibration` overrides). Then `narration-measure` (below) and `plan-check`: its `real_forecast_s` (narration plus edit holds) must land inside `export_window_s`. If it doesn't, change the *words* of the parts not yet recorded, or the holds. Never the delivery settings for pace, never the recorded audio.
4. **Produce the rest.** The same command without `--max-parts`. Finished parts are kept and never re-billed. A failed request, a short or unreadable response, or a balance too low for the next part stops the recording there (`BLOCKED`): the same command resumes from the first missing part. Each finished part records the voice, model, settings, direction revision, the fingerprint of the exact text sent, its request id and the credits billed (`part_records`).
5. **Changing lines after recording.** Recorded parts keep their line boundaries, so editing a line changes only the part that holds it. The dry run lists `changed_recorded_parts`, `pronunciation_changed_parts`, `parts_recorded_with_another_voice_setup` and `parts_made_for_an_earlier_direction`; `tts ... --rerecord-changed --execute` re-records just those (old takes move to `superseded/`, never deleted). A part to redo after listening or a check (a line not heard, a tag read aloud) is `--rerecord-parts N --reason "..."`. Adding or removing lines breaks the boundaries and forces a full re-record (`--rechunk --archive-existing`). After a direction change to the narrator, recorded parts that still serve the treatment can be kept with `direction-reapprove --artifact narration --reason "..."`.
6. **Measure and check.** `static_core.py narration-measure --run "RUN" --video NN` transcribes each part with Whisper medium (the references' yardstick) and aligns it to the script on comparable words (a number said as words and written as digits is the same word; a name Whisper spells differently is paired by its letters). It reports:
   - the real pace while speaking and overall with every pause, the natural gap after each line, and what each tag cost; from 300+ words it writes the voice lock's `measured`;
   - **every line heard.** A line the long pass missed is listened to again on its own (Whisper medium on a short window). Found, it was a transcription miss; still missing, it blocks the edit until the part is re-recorded or the user confirms by listening (`--confirm L105 --reason "who listened and what they heard"`, kept until that part changes);
   - **every tag performed, not read aloud** (the same block and the same confirmation);
   - possible repeated words, and **each join**: the gap against the natural one, the pace, level and pitch range on each side, words said twice across it;
   - each beat as heard: pace while speaking and overall, its longest gap, numbers per minute, next to what the beat asked for; and `delivery.json` (pitch range and movement, loudness contrast, rate per beat and on each side of every join, next to the references measured the same way). A beat asked to lift that measures flat is rewritten in the parts not yet recorded (10 §5). A calm, intimate narrator measures narrower than an energetic one: compare with what each beat asked for, never with a target.

   `audio-check` then covers every line recorded, the hashes and a complete join, one voice setup throughout, every line heard and no tag read aloud (from `narration-measure` on these exact parts), the duration against the audio tolerance, and **narration plus the planned holds inside the export window**. An error, a partial voice, a credit cutoff or a missing part is **blocking**: a failed recording is in progress, not a shorter deliverable.
7. **Quality listen.** No agent here can hear audio. What stands in for listening: the line-by-line alignment, the re-heard lines, tags read aloud, the joins, `delivery.json`. What only ears catch (a mispronounced name, a breath, fake enthusiasm, a bad join, strange emphasis, noise) is offered to the user as an optional listening list with exact times, never a gate. Re-record only the parts that need it, with approval and credit accounting. ElevenLabs output is not deterministic.

Supported backend: ElevenLabs `POST /v1/text-to-speech/:voice_id` with `text`, `model_id`, `voice_settings` (stability, similarity_boost), `language_code` and `previous_text`/`next_text`; `GET /v1/models` and `GET /v1/user/subscription` (free) before any billed call. Model limits come from ElevenLabs (10,000 characters a request for v4). Never put the key in JSON, the ZIP, logs, scripts or `CLAUDE.md`. `--execute` is off by default so agents never spend unexpectedly, and a credit-exhausted partial result never becomes "final".

## Music and layered sound

Music and sound carry out the locked treatment's `sound` and each beat's `sound_direction` (beat map): whether there is a bed at all, where it changes, where it drops to silence, what a single sound is for. They are decided there, with the story, not here. Use an instrumental bed when it suits this story and adds value. For instance, a reflective passage may want it almost subliminal, an exciting reconstruction a gradual texture change around meaningful turns, a demonstration clarity over score; the references' measured level under the voice (`av-profile`) shows what their audiences are used to, not what this film must do. Never drown speech. Distinguish neutral atmosphere, story-world sound, illustrative SFX, and musical punctuation. Silence can be an intentional dramatic tool. If using ACE-Step 1.5, ensure its environment exists and output is instrumental; validate duration, clipping, license and whether loops sound repetitive. Never fabricate unavailable sound layers. Keep an audible narration bed with consistent loudness across chapters and on small speakers. A bed is made through the gated music worker (04): each track names the beats it scores, and `timeline.json` records it under `audio_tracks` with those beats.

## Editing into CapCut

The picture is generated as *clean stills*. Add editable captions, arrows, highlights, chapter cards, lower thirds, diagrams, labels, controlled zoom/crop, simple wipes and optional licensed overlays in CapCut **only when helpful**. Social media captions/badges/emoji are not defaults. No static rule requires constant movement; an image can hold when it carries thought. Resist transitions that create discontinuities; vary macro shot family because a narrative turns, not just because a timer expired.

Use the real connected CapCut MCP and CLI after discovering their actual available tool names and schema. Typical actions in the user's existing setup may include listing drafts, adding image/audio/text, creating tracks, setting segment properties, adding keyframes and transitions, reading timeline, validating and saving. Do **not** fabricate tool calls or assume there is a CapCut render/export API. When no CapCut export tool is accessible, produce a **valid editable CapCut project** and explicitly explain the manual export needed. If local CapCut CLI fails, use an available supported route or stop with a clear blocker; never call a JSON manifest an exported edit.

On this PC the project is written with `STUDIO/scripts/capcut_tools.py` next to the MCP and capcut-cli; its commands, their order and the files they read are in `STUDIO/reference/09-capcut-toolkit.md`.

**Time the pictures to the real narration before placing them.** The shot plan was written against the planned timings, and the real narration never matches them. `static_core.py retime-shots --run "RUN" --video NN`:
- puts every cut in the silence between the last line of one shot and the first line of the next, from `narration-measure`'s line times; it refuses while a line is not heard or a tag was read aloud;
- inserts each edit hold (`pause_via: "edit"`) at its line and keeps it on the shot before, and at each join between recorded parts that came out shorter than the voice's natural gap, a seam hold of exactly the missing fraction of a second (continuity, never padding; `--no-seam-holds` leaves them out);
- rewrites `shot-plan.json`'s `start_s`/`end_s` (the planned values are kept once as `planned_*`), writes `70-edit/video-NN/edit-plan.json` and, whenever there are holds, `50-audio/video-NN/narration-edit.wav` (the narration with its holds);
- records `narration_for_edit` in the edit plan: the one audio file the project must carry, with its content hash;
- carries what the editor carries out: the locked treatment's visual language, sound, audiovisual relationship and structure and pace (`direction`, with its revision), each beat's span, and each shot's beat direction. It refuses while the direction, the beat map, the shot plan or the narration was left behind by a direction change.

The project then places `narration_for_edit.file` at 0 s, at speed 1, untrimmed, and each still at its shot's times. Shots the retiming made very short or very long are listed in the edit plan's `notes`, for the director to split, merge or keep.

Write `70-edit/video-NN/timeline.json` **derived from the actual CapCut timeline**, not a fictional plan, with `canvas` (e.g. `1920x1080`), `capcut_draft` (the project folder: the narration is then read from the project itself), `visual_segments` in chronological order, each `{ "start_s": ..., "end_s": ..., "shot_id": "S001", "asset_path": "...", "beat_id": "B01", "purpose": "..." }` (`asset_path` is the picture file CapCut really shows), an `audio_tracks` array (the narration as `{"kind": "narration", "file": ..., "start_s": 0, "end_s": ..., "source_start_s": 0, "source_end_s": ..., "speed": 1}`, and every other sound with its `kind`, `file`, `start_s`, `end_s` and the `beats` it serves), transitions, labels, note on project path and export status. No section should vanish because only enough pictures/audio were rendered for the opening. The actual last narrated beat and the final held frame must reach the locked story destination. Run:

```bash
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" timeline-check --run "RUN" --video 1
```

It checks contiguous visual range, beat links, story purposes, 16:9 canvas and successful audio gate, and the trace back to the plan: each clip is a shot of the current edit plan, for that shot's beat, starting at its retimed time and showing that shot's own still (or the one a callback reuses) made under the current direction; every shot is in the cut; each sound names its beats and plays inside them; music comes from the gated worker. **The narration in the cut is the approved one, whole:** the edit plan's file, unchanged since the retime, starting at 0 s, at speed 1 (no curve speed), its segments covering the file exactly once from start to end. A narration sped up, slowed, trimmed, cut or replaced is refused even when the project still has the right length. It does not substitute for playing through the project. Avoid black tails after the main edit and accidental CapCut "extra two seconds" tracks. A picture can intentionally lead/lag narration; the project duration should still respect the length contract.

## Export is a separate deliverable, with separate proof

Default YouTube landscape 1920×1080 or higher 16:9, 30 fps unless the project actually requires a deliberate alternative. For an accessible `output.mp4`:

```bash
python "$HOME/.claude/static-studio/scripts/static_core.py" export-check \
  --run "RUN" --video 1 --file "C:/path/to/output.mp4" --deep
```

The checker uses `ffprobe` for true exported duration, picture/audio stream presence, size and aspect, plus optional FFmpeg black/silence diagnostics. It also finds the approved narration (`narration_for_edit`) inside the export's sound, window by window along the film: a stretch not found, a timing that drifts (a speed change) or jumps (a cut or an insert) is refused; the level of the music and sound under the voice is reported, with a warning when it sits close enough to mask words. A diagnostic is a **clue**, not proof of an error (intentional silence/black may be right). Visually inspect opening, cuts, maps and chapter boundaries, listen to audio throughout, sample a few intervals in every chapter and watch the final minute. Verify exact titles/captions/names. Don't declare export verification complete when the file isn't accessible. Record export size, duration, settings and any unresolved issues in `80-deliverables/video-NN-release.md`.

## Publishing package

Provide a small set of title/thumbnail **concepts that truthfully promise the story**, not bait; a polished video description, source credits/licensing, optional chapter timestamps based on actual export, and a practical first-comment idea only if it contributes to discussion. Do not replicate the viral references' titles or thumbnails. YouTube Analytics may inform the next video later, but do not invent CTR, AVD or retention results for this one.
