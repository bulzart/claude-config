# 06 — Quality: aim for “10/10” by design, not by a fake scalar score

## Editorial standard

The target is a **long-form YouTube film that is enjoyable, creative, coherent, informative and worth finishing**. There is no honest process that guarantees an objective 10/10 rating, viral performance or high retention for every video. Quality comes from repeated diagnosis and revisions against specific audience experiences, while protecting agency and originality. Use this checklist as an adversarial lens, not a rigid scoring algorithm. A genuine intentional quiet moment is not a defect just because it has no new cut.

**Evaluate against the intended experience.** A tutorial should not be measured by how much dramatic tension it generates. A reflective essay should not be faulted for lacking a formal carrying question. A playful explainer should not be penalised for lacking gravitas. The question is always: does the chosen creative approach actually succeed at what it set out to do?

| Dimension | The passing experience | Red flags demanding rework |
|---|---|---|
| Topic and premise | A real, distinctive reason to care about this story at this length | Generic subject, no organizing question, same reference script with different nouns |
| Originality | A fresh perspective/device/evidence contribution | Template sameness, lifted hook/ending/lines, gimmick rather than insight |
| Narrative architecture | Clear promise, growing understanding, consequential middle, earned payoff | Flat list of facts, weak middle, disconnected anecdotes, abrupt conclusion |
| Voice and human reality | Narrator has personality suited to the subject; human or emotional elements appear where natural for the format | Robotic trivia delivery, forced drama, manipulative tragedy, canned inspiration; also a subject calling for warmth executed with clinical detachment |
| Engagement with dignity | Questions worth considering, moments to infer and reflect, genuine surprise | Clickbait, “and then” chains, cliffhangers on a clock, overexplaining every image |
| Factual and logical integrity | Verified claims, labeled hypotheticals, timeline and object continuity | False quotes, confusing probability and fact, anachronistic visuals, logical teleporting |
| Cinematic language | Pictures contribute understanding, consistent visual world, intentional motion/space/sound | Wallpaper montage, repeated generic frames, pointless zooms, unreadable text |
| Craft and reliability | Natural full narration, balanced music, 16:9 edit, correct locked length, actual export checked | Partial ElevenLabs, short Standard, black tail, unplayable clips, wrong format |

## Red-team at four natural production decisions

1. **After reference synthesis** ask: Did we accidentally treat multiple niches/length classes as one? Are conclusions observed or guessed? Do metrics really exist? Are we imitating a viral video's subject instead of understanding its audience experience? Would someone watching only the stills get a very different sense than someone hearing the narration? Fix by revisiting timestamps and audio.
2. **Before any billable generation** ask: Can this *subject*, not a generic concept, sustain the locked minutes with non-repetitive value? What if the first minute is removed—is there still a story? What would a curious informed viewer learn in each third? Does a source support every consequential claim? Does the voice's own delivery not already give each planned pause its time, and do the words alone reach the length? **Does the script's word count match the pace of the voice that will actually speak it, not just the reference narrators'?** Does every pause and tag come from the story? Is every content flag answered with a real decision, not a reflex "kept"? Where does the video first deliver what its opening promises, and what does the viewer get on the way there? At each key moment, is what it leans on still in the viewer's hands? Does the telling match the format, density and narration of the references the length and pace came from (`length_fit`)? Does a major that two readers found say what the earlier revision missed? What the checks already hold (the cold read and its answers, the comprehension signals, the pauses' fields, the captions, the lock before the script) is in `plan-check`'s answer: read it rather than ask it again. Check `direction.json`, `blueprint.json`, `script.json`, `beat-map.json`, the review records, claim ledger and `plan-check`.
3. **During picture/audio assembly** ask: Does each visual do explanatory/emotional work? Are locations, objects and time consistent? Do sound and narration suit the beat? Did the script actually become natural, complete, intelligible speech? Does `narration-measure`'s pause profile sit where the creative strategy meant it to next to the references'? Does each held silence feel like part of the performance: the line before it landing the way the moment wants (or left hanging on purpose), the next line starting again, the bed doing what the moment needs, no softened punchline, no fade on a voice, nothing added to hide an empty moment? After `retime-shots`, does each still still hold its moment at its real length (the edit plan's `notes`)? Does `delivery.json` show the voice doing what each beat asked (a beat meant to lift that measures flat is rewritten in the parts not yet recorded)? Are `shot-check`'s repetition warnings deliberate callbacks? Does each join keep the voice's rhythm and level (`line-times.json` → `seams`)? What the checks already hold (every line heard, one voice setup, every pause decided on the recorded voice and with its picture, the approved narration whole in the project and in the export) is in their answers. Check `audio-check`, all TTS seams, the image argument and frame context rather than relying on a contact sheet alone.
4. **Before publication** ask: Does the final video fulfill its title/thumbnail promise? Is the middle as alive as the opening? Is the final minute satisfying and untruncated? Is it enjoyable even without a running “dramatic” score? Did the final watch review of this exact export find anything still unanswered? Check actual CapCut readback, `timeline-check`, exported MP4 `export-check`, the export's watch sheets (`av-profile --file`), the final review and `release-check`, facts, titles, audio, visual continuity, chapter rhythm and technical end frame.

## Independent reads: four moments, each on record

The writer cannot see their own boring stretches; a reader who knows nothing else can. Four moments where a fresh eye changes the outcome:

| Read | When | The reader gets | It must not get |
|---|---|---|---|
| `treatment` | before `direction-lock` | the treatments' descriptions, how each unfolds over the whole video (its movements, minutes and what the viewer gets) and three sample moments each, under random labels, and the video's promise when the blueprint already states one (`audience_promise`) | the evidence, the risks, your preference, where you marked each promise as delivered, the chosen approach's pitch, the reference scenes the content reviews cut |
| the openings (`opening-packet`: its own commands, 10 §4) | when the script's first minute can be written, before the script read | two to four openings of the same video under random labels (about the first minute: the words and what is seen), the titles the film would sit beside with ours among them, unmarked, and one viewer's stance | the plan, the direction, the rest of the script, which opening or title is yours or came first |
| `script` | before the first paid narration | the title, the bare script and each silence's length | the plan, the rationale, the writing signals, earlier findings, picture or sound plans, the house rules (the editorial and compliance stages check those) |
| `final` | after export, before release | the export's watch sheets, its parts by time only, where the held silences are and how long, and where music, ambience and effects play and how far under the voice (measured by `sound-review`, 05: times, kinds and levels only) | what each part is meant to do, your hopes for it, your reasons for each silence, which file a sound is or what any sound is meant to do, the reference scenes the content reviews cut |

`review-packet --kind K` writes the packet with the reader's brief (a viewer's questions, the findings format; the standing house rules too for the treatment and final reads; the cut reference material is kept out by the content checks and the editorial stage, never listed for a reader) in a fresh folder outside the run, with its findings file beside it: the reader has no path to the plan, the direction or earlier findings. Then `review-read --kind K` runs the reader itself: its own Claude Code process started in that folder, with Read and Write only and its file tools confined to the folder (a read of a run file is refused), no settings, hooks or MCP servers, and a prompt that is the packet's path and nothing else; the watch sheets are copied in beside the packet. Or spawn ONE new agent of type `static-cold-reader` (Read and Write only, no search tools) whose whole prompt is: read only that file and follow it. Add nothing: no rationale, no plan, no hint of what you hope it finds. How the read was isolated goes on record (`reader_route`, `reader_confined`, written by `review-record`): `review-read` is a process whose file tools are confined to its own folder, and it leaves `review-read.json` there; the agent has Read and Write only and its packet sits outside the run, but it is not sandboxed, so a read by that route is shown with a warning. `review-record` seals the findings with a hash, so they can't be softened later, and starts the answers file. A final reader who wrote words around its times in `at` ("Held silences 00:29, 05:15") is not asked again: `review-record --normalize-at` keeps only the times in `at` and its own text in `at_original`, and nothing else it wrote is touched. Answer every finding:

- `revised`: say what changed (for the script, the lines, by their numbers in the revised script or the ones the reader saw; plan-check checks those lines really changed). A revised finding about what a development means or pays (S, from the reader's developments; K, a key moment the reader did not name) also names its `cause` (in a round recorded since 2026-11-24): `context`, `order`, `motivation`, `causality`, `setup`, `significance_unexplained`, `cognitive_load`, `visuals`, `material`, `treatment`, or `other: <which>`. The fix follows the cause: never more exposition, effects, music or pauses by default;
- `kept`: minor findings only, with why;
- `disputed`: with why. A reader can be wrong; say so plainly, with the reason. A recurring major that is disputed is closed by a `decision` (below), never by the word alone.
- In a round after the first, every **major** finding also says whether it recurs (`recurrence`, below).

**A problem that comes back.** When a later reader names, as major, a problem an earlier reader also found, the revision between them did not reach its cause: two independent readers agreeing is strong evidence, and a kinder verdict from the later reader does not cancel it. `review-record` lists, for each major finding of a new round, the earlier rounds' major findings closest to it (`recurrence_candidates`: the words their lines and accounts share; facts for you, never the decision). Each such finding's answer carries `recurrence`: `recurs_of` (`"none"`, or the earlier finding as `"r1:F1"`); and when it recurs, `root_cause` (what the earlier revision missed, five words or more) and `scope`: `wording`, `order`, `structure`, `treatment`, `material`, `duration` or `dispute`. Rewording is refused as the answer to a recurring major (dispute it with the reason instead, when the readers are wrong). A `structure` or `treatment` cause is answered by re-planning: revise the direction and re-lock with `direction-lock --revise` after that read, then rebuild what it sends back (plan-check holds the script until then). A `duration` cause is a length decision the user makes (the length re-locked after that read). **A recurring major you do not cure is closed by a decision** (rounds recorded since 2026-11-26): when you dispute it, or its scope is `dispute`, the answer carries `decision`: the `options` weighed (two at least, each with its `cure` and its `cost`; leaving the passage as it is may be one), the one `chosen`, what stands in the way of the strongest cure (`blocked_by`: `nothing`, `length`, `recorded_narration`, `budget`, or `other: <what>`) and who decided (`decided_by`: `director` or `user`). A cure blocked by the length, by narration already recorded or by the budget is the user's decision, not yours: put the options to them with the evidence (what both readers said, the lines, what each cure costs), stop work on that finding until they answer, and record `decided_by: "user"` with their own words (`user_words`). plan-check says `..._waits_for_the_users_decision` until then, and `creative.answers_needed` says why each open answer is still open (for the final watch review, `release-check` says it in `answers_needed`). Whether the choice is right stays your judgement or theirs; the record only makes the choice explicit, so a problem two readers found is never closed by default. Then a fresh read of the rebuilt script, by a reader blind to the earlier findings, your rationale and the earlier versions, as every reader is. Readers give no score. The script and final readers say whether they would have watched to the end and, if not, where they would have stopped and what would have kept them (`verdict.watched_to_end`, `would_stop_at`); a stop that none of that reader's own major findings covers becomes one (`X1`), answered like any other. A later reader who would watch to the end is never the evidence that an earlier major is solved.

**The openings are compared, not approved.** One to three readers read the same openings, each in its own order and stance (`opening-packet --readers N`, `opening-read` or one `static-cold-reader` per packet, `opening-record`). They leave no findings to answer: what each opening made them want, where attention slipped, which they would keep watching and which title they would click is the evidence for your decision in `opening.json` (10 §4). Votes decide nothing, and a read counts only for the exact openings it saw: an opening rewritten afterwards is read again.

**A harmless format slip is not a new read.** A reader who joined several line ranges in one string (`"L104-L105, L119-L122"`) or named two treatments in one finding (`"B, C"`) has said what they meant; asking a new reader would cost a read and lose this one. `review-record` puts such slips into the packet's format (a list of ranges; one finding per treatment, the second taking the label as its id suffix: F7, F7C), only when every part is valid, and never touches a word, a judgement or a severity. The file as the reader wrote it stays in the run (`<kind>-r<n>-findings.json`, or, for a file given with `--findings` from elsewhere, a byte copy `<kind>-r<n>-findings-as-written.json`; the record's `findings_as_written` names it) and each change is listed in the record's `format_normalized` (where, what it was, what it became). Anything else that does not follow the format is refused as before; a reader's file is never edited by hand.

**Promise and delivery, from the reader's side.** The script reader also says what the opening made them want, the line where they first got it (or never), and, for each stretch before it, whether it built anticipation or only delayed (`promise`). The reader's own account is the evidence: a stretch felt as delay becomes a minor finding (W1, W2...) with their words quoted; a promise never delivered, a major one; a wait felt as anticipation is the build working, never a finding. The director weighs these against the treatment's movements and the beat map, and fixes the cause (10 §4, "Boredom at its source"): the strongest material postponed, background before the viewer cares, a frame that could open on the promise and give the background through the questions it raises. No minute or share of the runtime makes a wait right or wrong. The reader also notes the first line by which they knew what the video was going to give them (`clear_at`). `plan-check` shows it in seconds at the planning pace, beside the working title's promise and the hooks of the references this direction cites (`creative.opening`), and says so when the film takes longer to say what it offers than every one of them: a place to look at what the viewer gets before that line, never a finding and never a fault by itself.

A **major** finding (a viewer would likely stop watching, lose the thread or stop trusting the video there) gets a second, fresh read of the revised version by a new agent: round 2 for the script and the final review. After round 2 the director decides, and the reasons stay on record for the release sheet. More reads are always allowed and never required, so nothing loops forever. An approval covers the version it read: a script changed beyond a small trim, a treatment never read or rewritten past 70% of the vocabulary its reader saw, or a different export needs a new read. A fresh read follows what a revision touches, not how many words it moved: after a read, a changed line that the reader's verdict rested on forces one however few its words (a key moment's lines; what it depends on, `depends_on.from` and a withheld fact's `revealed_in`; its hedge line; the lines of a beat carrying a researched value, the payoff marked; lines tied to claims, claims.json `line_refs`; lines the editorial review's Comprehension section names as a gap: `accidental_confusion`, `poor_sequencing` or `missing_context`), and a line removed beside such lines counts too (`script_revised_where_it_matters_since_its_last_read:L045(key_moment:B05),...`). The share of words changed is only a warning. When a change on such a line is wording only, say so in the answers file (`revision_notes`: `{"L045": "why its meaning is unchanged, five words or more"}`): no fresh read is forced for it, and noted lines leave the count of lines edited after review. Lines are compared by their words as well as their numbers, so adding or removing a line never makes the lines after it count as revised: revise in whatever shape the story needs, and never edit in place just to keep the numbers. A moved line counts as a change (the order is what the reader followed). Revision notes name lines by their numbers in the script as it now stands; a cut line is named by the number the reader saw. Major findings and reconcile gaps still require the next round. Refining the chosen treatment does not; it is shown at every plan-check.

What the code enforces: the order, the version each read covers, sealed findings, every finding answered, every key moment reconciled, a revision that really changed something, the second read after a major. What it cannot enforce: that the reader really was fresh, that the answers are honest, that the revision fixed the problem. Those are the director's integrity. Never write or edit a reviewer's findings yourself. No army of reviewers or high-cost agents whose job is to add a number.

**A direction approved under earlier planning rules keeps its plan** (10 §3 says which rules each lock sees only as advisory warnings, and what a substantive revision brings in). In the reviews those are the key moments' reconciliation and the fresh read after a revision where it matters (above), and release-check's final review shows them the same way. Sealed findings, every finding answered and the round after a major still hold, and so do safety and content, money, asset integrity and the export checks, for every run. Answers follow the rules of the round they answer: a round recorded before 2026-11-24's recurrence rule owes no `recurrence`.

**An exact topic or story stays the user's.** When the user gave an exact topic or story, the blind treatment read also asks, for each treatment, whether it is still a video about what was asked (only their words, nothing of the plan); a "partly" or "no" becomes a finding (Q1, ...) answered like any other. A niche scope with its `boundary` (02) is asked the same way: whether each treatment is still a video of that niche. The readers stay blind to the research: it reaches them only through the film, and the director weighs their accounts against the key moments and the values the beats carry.

**What happened, why it matters, whether it paid: kept apart.** The script and final readers name the developments that matter most in the video and, for each, what happened, why it matters using only what the video gave them (and whether that came from the video or from their own knowledge: our readers know a great deal, a viewer may know none of it) and whether it paid off; something held back on purpose is marked withheld. They also say what would have been different without it, and when the video let them know why it mattered (before, at the moment, only afterwards, never), judging as a first-time viewer who knows nothing of the subject and may not be a native speaker. `review-record` turns those answers into findings answered like any other: a development whose significance was unclear, or known only from outside the video, is major (S1, S2...); one only partly clear is major on a key moment of the beat map and minor elsewhere, and so is what happened when it was only partly followed; a payoff that missed is major on a key moment and minor elsewhere (half a payoff on a key moment is minor); "I could not tell" counts as not clear whatever the label, an answer naming two choices is read by the worse one, and the reason in the reader's own words is required; a reason that arrives only after the moment is major on a key moment of the beat map (the moment itself passed without its weight); elsewhere it counts only when the meaning was not clear at the time (a setup that is clear when it happens and pays off later is ordinary craft); a key moment of the beat map that the reader did not name among the developments that mattered is a minor check (K1...). A development whose labels say the payoff landed, while the reader's own words (`would_be_different`, `why_it_matters`) plainly say it changes little, is a finding too (major on a key moment, minor elsewhere): the account is the evidence, not the label. A weighed answer ("not much in the plot, but...") is not one. The key moments are sealed with the packet, so a mark moved after the read changes nothing. The final reader is not told what each part is meant to do: compare its part-by-part account with each beat's `experience` yourself when you answer, and treat a part that did not give what it was planned to as your own finding in the editorial review. Every new export gets its own final read, whatever the round. A reader who followed the facts but not why they matter has found a fault in the film, not in the reader: investigate it before answering, never dismiss it because the script is coherent or because a well-informed reviewer liked it. Not every development needs stakes: the reward is the video's own, and the fix is chosen for the cause (10 §4).

**Each key moment reconciled, after every script and final read.** `review-record` puts a `reconcile` block in the answers file for each key moment: `planned` (the beat's `matters_because`, its `reward` and the researched values it carries) beside `reader` (the reader's developments over that moment, in their own words). Fill in its `verdict` and a `note` of five words or more: `same` (they took the planned meaning), `different_but_valid` (another reading the film can stand, saying why) or `gap` (the planned meaning did not reach them). A reader saying it changes little is a gap whatever its labels; a different emphasis is not. A gap names its `cause` (the list under `revised`, above) and is either `answered_by` a finding id of that round or given a `revision` of five words or more; it counts as a major finding, so a fresh read follows. A key moment left unreconciled blocks plan-check (the script read) and release-check (the final read). In the final read a development counts for a key moment when the two share at least 0.25 s (mm:ss names a whole second: 05:16 is 316-317 s; for a moment shorter than half a second, half of it), with no ±1 s slack; in the script read, when its lines fall inside the moment's.

**Three kinds of evidence, each for what it can show.** The first-time reader (the cold read) says whether the story is understandable, interesting and worth continuing by ear. The context-aware reviews (the editorial review, the final watch review, the director's answers) judge whether the treatment, pictures, voice, music, drawings and edit serve the intended experience. The technical checks (plan-check, timeline-check, export-check, release-check) confirm structure, timing, completeness and the file. A script reader cannot say how a tag sounded, a validator cannot say a sequence moves anyone, a measurement cannot say a voice is charming: what only ears and eyes can judge goes to the user: the listening sheet `sound-review` writes (05) names the few passages and the one question for each.

**Fix the cause, not the symptom.** Every finding names its `likely_cause` (a derived finding's is the computed cause; the reader's own `note` stays beside it as `reader_note`). Before choosing a remedy, find what makes the passage dull or confusing: the viewer doesn't know why it matters, the story stops moving, a point repeats, the picture restates or contradicts the words, a payoff is too far away, a name or a cause was never set up, too much arrives at once, the delivery is uniform. Then fix that: a consequence or stakes, a clearer relationship, another order, a stronger picture or reveal, a cut, a different delivery. Faster cuts, louder music, more captions, more animation or shorter pauses do not rescue weak storytelling, and a fast passage that is clear and engaging is not slowed because a number differs from a reference. A picture can reinforce a relation the words make; it never rescues one they do not. And no consequence, prediction or certainty is added to make a moment land: two things linked by place or time are told as that link (10 §4).

## Concrete anti-slop safeguards

- No later frame can depict an object/character in a different place/state without a shown or implied logically valid transition. No unmotivated jumps in distance, time, weather, lighting or scale. When two scenes must be separated, include a readable cut/ellipsis or bridge.
- Whatever form the story takes, its own logic holds from start to finish. What that logic is comes from this video's approach, not from a niche rulebook. Some examples: a step can't rely on a tool or step not yet shown; a hypothetical's first changed assumption stays in force; a reconstructed event keeps its map, chronology and equipment grounded and labels uncertain details as reconstruction; an essay's argument doesn't quietly change its terms.
- No script contains a holiday/religious feast scene (Thanksgiving, Christmas dinners, etc.), a dramatized religious ceremony/practice, or a superstition/supernatural claim (omens, luck, ghosts, astrology, curses) as a narrative or illustrative detail. Reject it on sight during red-team review and replace it with a different detail, even if accurate and sourced. Plain, unavoidable factual statements (e.g., a culture's religion shaped the event) are fine; dramatizing them as a scene is not.
- Every still, the thumbnail and every line are checked against 02's "Nothing indecent, gory or distressing" rule: skin where clothes belong, a bathroom/bed/shower scene, blood or a wound, a corpse, a face in agony, a person lying limp, a survival pose that doesn't read clearly alive, a child-looking face, graphic medical/injury detail, swearing, or an intimate body part named. A hit is a rework reason — rewrite the line/prompt to tell the fact (a number, an outcome) without its graphic form, never add a "no blood"-style negative prompt instead of actually changing the scene. The look at every still and at the thumbnail is recorded, and a still with a `people_question` counts as not looked at until its look answers it (04, "Every picture looked at"). Say plainly what was and was not seen: every picture in full, the export through watch sheets only (a frame every 5 s), a reference through its 2 fps samples plus the closer looks taken. Nothing excluded by a reference's content review comes back, in any words or picture.
- Read the finished beat map and shot plan for places a drawn/animated explanation (04) would teach or land something better than the still alone — a quantity, a process, a route, a timeline, something worth circling — and for any already planned, whether it's earning its place (would it be missed if cut?) rather than decorating. Neither a long stretch with nothing drawn nor a drawing on every still is automatically wrong; ask whether the specific moment calls for one and act on the answer, don't default either way.
- Narration and visuals are complementary. A still image isn't compelled to include a logo/label, and a visual-only inference need not be narrated twice.
- A chapter is not complete because the words stop. The promised consequence/answer and a sensible closing beat must be present.
- Paid generation errors are explicit. A partial or financially exhausted TTS recording does **not** earn a shortened completion status.
- A record that is there and cannot be read stops the command and is named (a damaged or half-written JSON file, an unreadable row of the paid ledger). It is never taken for "nothing recorded yet": that would re-send paid work or let a second paid job through. Restore the file (its history, or the step that writes it) or remove it when it should not exist; never work around it.

## Revisions are actionable

Instead of “quality: 7/10”, record `problem → evidence timestamp/line → audience impact → fix → recheck`. For example: “B3 repeats B2's explanation and shows the same three generic paintings → audience learns nothing new for 84 seconds → replace B3 with a sourced decision dilemma and map comparison → validate new script words and image count.” This preserves freedom and provides a measurable improvement. Use precise findings rather than decorative confidence badges.

**Answering a reader, learned on one full film (five script reads and two reads of the export, 2026-11-27 and 28).**

- An answer that states a fact about the film (a level, a time, what a picture shows, what a sound is) is checked in the file before it is written. One answer said a loud ambience was "the lift written for one gap"; measured afterwards it sat 7 dB under the voice for 21 seconds and was the wrong sound. A wrong answer closes a finding that is still true.
- A line written to answer a reader is checked against the film's own earlier statements and against earlier readers' findings before the next read: a fix for one reader brought back another reader's "the sums do not add up".
- A revision that answers major findings grows the script. Count the words before polishing, and let each minor finding's fix pay for itself in words cut elsewhere.
- A later reader's major findings are often an earlier reader's in a new form: before revising, set them beside the earlier round's and name the cause they share (`recurrence`).
- A finding the record derives as major from a reader's "partly" is weighed by the reader's own words, which the finding quotes. Where their account shows the meaning reached them, dispute it with those words; where it shows the meaning arrived late or thin, it is a major whatever else they liked.
- A beat no reader names among what they were given, after two reads, is a candidate for the knife, however much it cost to research.
- A cure that needs more evidence (a named person, a documented case) is a research step with its own small budget, decided with the user when it costs real tokens or money, never patched with wording.

## Release sheet (plain language)

Write `80-deliverables/video-NN-release.md` with: the locked treatment (and whether the direction was locked late), each later revision of the direction and what followed it, each research correction re-checked after the lock (release-check refuses one that wasn't) (updated, or reapproved with its reason); each read's rounds, its major findings and how they were answered, with any closed by the director's decision named; the `release-check` result for the exported file and its measured loudness; the sound review of that file (how far under the voice the quietest and the least quiet music play, anything it listed to look at and what was done about it) and that the listening sheet went to the user; what a person did with that file (`human-review`: listened, watched, both, or nothing on record: say which, and never imply a listen or a watch that is not on record); locked target and comparable reference IDs; actual script words and calibrated pace; actual audio duration and completeness result; CapCut project and measured timeline duration; actual export path and probed duration or `NOT EXPORTED/NOT VERIFIED`; manual opening/middle/ending checks; outstanding factual/visual/audio issues and resolutions; publishing assets/title/thumbnail; honest release status (`ready`, `blocked`, or `project-only`). Do not assert a creator can guarantee engagement or that a provider API returned results it did not.
