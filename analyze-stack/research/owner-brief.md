Task 3: build our new multi-pipeline skill. Once the product question is settled, the skill asks which output mode we want. There are three modes:

Mode 1, full replication. The original video is reproduced 1:1 as a sequence of Seedance 2.5 prompts (a 45-second video becomes 3 prompts of 15 seconds, a 1-minute video becomes 4x15, and so on). Each prompt covers its exact time slice of the original and describes everything in it in full detail (subjects, actions, camera, lighting, setting, dialogue, sound, on-screen text logic), so that when all the clips are generated and joined in order they reproduce the original video as exactly as possible, with our product swapped in if we chose that.

Mode 2, best-segment replication. We pick the strongest part of the original (the "favorite 45 seconds", not always 45, just an example; one continuous segment or a few segments) that fits the target length, and replicate only that part 1:1 in the same way as Mode 1. The skill proposes which segment(s) to keep based on the viral analysis and lets me confirm or change the choice.

Mode 3, condensed remake. Take a long video (say 4 minutes) and recreate it as a shorter version (90 seconds, or 20, or 50, whatever I choose) that is still the same video, only compressed. This is not a highlight reel of random moments and not a new video "inspired by" the original. It is the same story, concept, message, characters, setting, style, tone, humor and payoff, told in less time, the way a skilled editor would re-cut a 4-minute video into 1:30 without losing the point. The skill builds a timestamped beat map of the original, scores every beat by importance (necessary for the story, carries a viral trigger, signature visual moment) and produces a compression plan: which beats are kept, merged or dropped, and why, with the time budget spread over the kept beats so that the hook lands in the first seconds, the beats stay in the original order, the iconic moments and quotable lines survive (verbatim where they matter), repetition, filler, dead air and secondary detours are cut, and the ending or payoff is preserved. From that plan it writes a compressed storyboard and script for the target length and splits it into X-second Seedance 2.5 prompts, with continuity between consecutive prompts (the end state of one clip is the start state of the next: same characters, wardrobe, product, setting, lighting and camera language). The test for success: someone who watches only the 90-second version takes away the same story, message, emotional arc and reasons to share it as someone who watched the full 4 minutes, with no drift from the original context and no invented plot, characters or messaging. It must also be possible to pin specific moments as "anchors" that are replicated exactly while everything around them is compressed (a hybrid of Mode 2 and Mode 3).

For Modes 2 and 3 the skill asks for the target duration and the seconds per prompt (I decide, from 5 to 30, within whatever Seedance 2.5 actually supports according to the saved spec notes; equal lengths by default, uneven lengths only if I allow it), and for Mode 3 also whether dialogue should be kept verbatim (compressed) or may be paraphrased, and whether the original ending or CTA must be kept as is. The skill should recommend a shorter target when that is what it takes to keep every shot physically feasible and natural.

Then it asks about style: keep the produced prompts in the same style as the original (real footage stays real footage, anime stays anime, sketch stays sketch) or swap to another style (sketch -> real footage, real footage -> anime, and so on) without drifting from the original video context, only changing the style.
