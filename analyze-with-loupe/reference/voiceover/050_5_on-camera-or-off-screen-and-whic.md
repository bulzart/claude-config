## 5. On-camera or off-screen, and which voice

- **The form per method.** **native**: on-camera (a speaking character whose lips the take drives) or off-screen (the take drives the action
  timing; nobody in the frame speaks). **lipsync**: on-camera videos get the sync pass; a video with no visible speaker takes the overlay
  form, without a sync pass. **overlay**: always off-screen; a person in the frame may react, never mouth the words.
- **On-camera** needs a speaking character the voice fits: the voice's labels against the character's registry row (apparent gender and age),
  its accent label against the preference, the face visible at the phrase starts.
- **The voice:** the character's own (`voiceover.voices`) when the user paired them; else the run's voice where it fits that character; else
  the off-screen form with the run's voice. Never ask: write the choice and its reason in the plan.
- One speaker per take. Two speaking characters: two takes (each its voice), placed in turn, on-camera only with a model whose guide allows
  several dialogue speakers.
