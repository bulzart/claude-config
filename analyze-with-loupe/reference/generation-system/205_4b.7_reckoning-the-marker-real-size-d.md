### 4b.7 Reckoning the marker: real size, distance, perspective, occlusion
*Achieve:* the marker of §4b.6 stands where the product will stand, at the size it really has at that depth, in
the scene's perspective and occlusion, so the model copies a spot and a size that are already right. *Avoid:* a
size read off the product's own photo (a packshot fills its frame), a distance taken from the shot size, a size
from one estimate. *Miss and cause:* the user (2026-10-22, [U]): the product in the prepared video must have the
right size, its proportions, dimensions and distance calculated by comparing it with the original video's objects
of known size and their distances, its real dimensions taken from the client assets.
1. **Real dimensions from the client assets:** height, width (a can's diameter) and depth from the registry's
   affordance sheet, which takes them from the spec images, the product sheets and `product_properties.txt`
   (`anomaly-catalog.md` §1, §2.7 step 1), the variant the plan names; never from how large it looks in its own
   photo. `--product-h-cm` and `--product-w-cm` of `T placeholder` take these numbers.
2. **The distance, derived from the frame** (`anomaly-catalog.md` §2.7 step 2), on a full-resolution frame of the
   prepared source (`T frames --grid` labels pixel coordinates): the surface the product will stand on, and at
   least TWO neighbours of known real size at or near the product's depth (a hand 18 cm, a head 23 cm, a laptop
   30–36 cm wide, a keyboard 44 cm, a mug, a bottle; the standard-size table of §2.7), each with its depth ratio
   (product distance / neighbour distance, read from the surface, the occlusion order and the converging lines),
   and the lens the look implies (a phone's main lens about 26 mm).
3. **Two estimates that agree.** Each neighbour gives an implied product height, `px × (product cm / neighbour
   cm) / depth ratio`; the distance and the lens give another (`T scale --product-cm H --distance d --lens f`).
   Pass them to `T placeholder` as `--basis "<neighbour>: <cm> = <px> px at depth ratio <r>"` (repeatable) with
   `--distance-m`, `--distance-basis` and `--lens`; the sidecar records them with the implied heights and their
   spread (`implied_spread_pct`) and decides nothing. **The marker is built only when two independent estimates
   agree within about 25 %** (the rule of thumb of §2.7); further apart, re-derive the depth on the
   full-resolution frame or take another neighbour before drawing. The drawn height is the agreeing value,
   written in the plan with its basis lines.
4. **Perspective.** The camera height against the product decides what shows: a camera above it shows the top
   ellipse and a base ellipse curved down, a camera at its height straight edges, a camera below it the base
   ellipse. The render is turned to match, its label turned about thirty degrees off the lens the way a person
   leaves a thing, never squared to it (`anomaly-catalog.md` §2.15 sign 5).
5. **Posture and support** as §4b.1b: on its base, or lying flat on its wide face (a render already foreshortened
   for the camera height, drawn with `--aspect-from render`), always on a surface the frame shows, never leaning
   on something the frame does not have.
6. **Occlusion.** A static marker only where nothing passes in front of it in the window. Where a hand, an arm or
   an object passes in front, give a per-frame mask (`--occlusion-mask`, or `--skin` for a hand inside the
   product's box) so the occluder stays in front, or choose another spot (§4b.1c). Nothing may pass through the
   marker (`anomaly-catalog.md` §2.14).
7. **Where the marker cannot be static.** Over a cut, a punch-in, a new set-up or a camera move the spot moves in
   the frame: one `--window` per stretch with its own spot and height (a punch-in scales the height by its zoom),
   each reckoned as above. A handheld drift of a few pixels is held by shorter windows that follow it; an orbit or
   a pan that sweeps the spot cannot be held by static windows: the anchor class changes (in hand, the swap of a
   source object, §4b.1b) and the plan says why.
8. **The label** sits on the side with more free room, never over a face, the key action or a hand's path
   (`--label-side` moves it), readable at 720×1280, and its words name no brand and no product.
