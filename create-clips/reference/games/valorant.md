# Valorant match broadcasts

Written from model memory [M]: the broadcast wins over this page. Where the page and the picture disagree, trust the picture and note it.

## The match
- Two teams of five on one map; a series is BO3 or BO5. Rounds: the first team to **13** wins (12 rounds a half, the sides swap after round 12); at 12-12 overtime follows and is won by two rounds.
- A round: a buy phase, then live. The attackers plant the **spike** at a site; the defenders defuse it. A round ends when a team is eliminated, the spike explodes or is defused, or the clock runs out (defenders win without a plant).
- Each player plays an **agent** with abilities (smokes, flashes, walls, heals, an ultimate charged over several rounds): a play is as much the abilities as the gunfight.
- **Economy:** credits from wins, losses and kills. **Pistol rounds** are round 1 and round 13. Eco, force and full buys as in other tactical shooters; a **thrifty** is a round won on a low spend and a story by itself.

## What the broadcast shows
The scoreboard at the top centre (round scores, the round clock, the spike when planted), the alive agents and their ultimate charges on both sides, the kill feed top right, replays marked; a caster desk between maps. Measure the places on a real frame (`T grid-frame`).

## What a highlight is
An **ace** (five kills in a round), a **clutch** (1vX), a **thrifty**, a perfect ultimate (a team wiped by one ability), a spike **ninja defuse** or a **last-second defuse**, an operator flick, a **retake**, a perfect team **execute**, a fake that fools the defence,
a **throw** (a round given away), a caster who loses it, a crowd that explodes. Lexicon: ace, clutch, thrifty, eco, force, retake, execute, entry, trade, lurk, ult, util, spike, site, rotate, match point, decider, tech pause.

## The unit: the round
Same logic as Counter-Strike (`cs2.md`): the end of a round is the score change, its start is where the buy phase ends; pistol rounds, the round after a clutch or an upset, timeouts, half-time and map-point rounds are the quiet ones that matter; a standard execute with a clean trade is usually noise.
Traps: abilities make the picture busy (a kill can hide behind effects: count on the kill feed and the frames); replays; a late score update; the sides swapping at half-time.
