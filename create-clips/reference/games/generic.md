# Any other game: how to write its profile

A game the library has no page for (Rocket League, a fighting game, a battle royale, a sports game, a racing game, another shooter or strategy game) gets its profile written in the run, by the profile agent, from the recon and the research:
`RUN/20-analysis/game-profile.md`, one page, with the same sections as the pages next to this one. Nothing here is a verdict about the game; it is the list of questions to answer, each tagged [W] web, [S] this broadcast (rec time), [M] model memory or [I] inference.

1. **The match:** how many sides, how a game or a series is played, what ends it, how long it runs.
2. **What the broadcast shows:** where the score, the clock and the kill or event feed are on screen (measure on a real frame: `T grid-frame`), what a replay looks like, how the broadcast is structured (desk, replays, breaks).
3. **The unit:** what repeats and can be told apart. A round (a bomb round, a rally), a goal, a point, a lap, a fight, a set, an engagement, an objective. How its start and its end show on screen (a score change, a clock reset, a banner, the casters' voices).
4. **What a highlight is:** the plays that fans share, with the words the casters and the community use for them.
5. **Quiet but not noise, usually noise, never skip:** the units that look dull and matter, the ones that are filler, the ones that are the story.
6. **Lexicon and names:** the terms, the team and player name spellings, how Whisper may mishear them.
7. **Traps:** replays, pauses, late score updates, side swaps, a desk that cuts away, an overlay that hides the action.

Examples of units, to start from: a goal and the build-up to it (Rocket League, football games), a round (any round-based shooter), a rally or a point (tennis games), a kill-streak or a final circle (battle royale), a set and the comeback inside it (fighting games),
a lap or an overtake (racing). The units rules in `reference/game-clipping.md` (impact, the four keep levels, how much to take) apply to all of them; only the words change.
A new game's profile stays in the run: the library's pages are not edited from a run.
