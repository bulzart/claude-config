# The portfolio: which videos get made (planner's guide)

You have the block files (`20-analysis/blocks/block-NN.json`), the brief and the criteria. You decide what gets built, then write one brief per video.
Two kinds are made at the same time: shorts and long-form. The agents decide how many of each; nothing here is a quota.

## 1. Merge what the scan found
- `RUN/20-analysis/stream-summary.md`: the stream in time order, from the block summaries (what the stream was, the acts it fell into, the guests and when they came and left,
  the turns, the technical trouble). A reader should get the night in two minutes.
- `RUN/20-analysis/moments.json`: all moments, ids kept, duplicates and overlaps merged (when two block agents saw one moment from two sides, keep the widest honest bounds and the better title), the second look's recovered moments included (`second-look.json`: `SL-` ids, `found_by: "second-look"`, `why_missed`; a recovered moment that a block moment already holds is merged into it). The blocks' `maybes` and the second look's `checked` lines are not moments: they are leads for a short's setup or a long video's context. The second look's `threads` connect setups and payoffs across blocks: read them with the `needs_context` of the moments you keep.
- `RUN/20-analysis/richness.json`: one score per chunk (the scan's), smoothed by you where a block agent was harsher or kinder than its neighbours, with the reason.
- `RUN/20-analysis/acts.json`: the mining agents' `acts` of every block, in rec time (each act: `rec_from`, `rec_to`, `kind`, `what`, `starts`, `ends`): an act that is open at the end of one block (`open_end`) and open at the start of the next (`open_start`) is joined into ONE act with one `what`.
  Every ad batch and every hole in the recording (`gap_before_s`) is marked in it as a border. Section 2 sets the borders between the long videos from these acts.
- `RUN/20-analysis/heat.md` (made by `T heat`: the chat's bursts and each chunk's hot share) and the moments' `heat` objects and the blocks' `heat_review` lines: how the audience reacted, a small clue that corroborates and never decides (`CC/reference/chat-heat.md`); `moments.json` keeps each moment's `heat` as the miner wrote it.
- `RUN/20-analysis/lexicon.json`: the merged Whisper-spelling fixes, `[["Whisper wrote","right"], ...]`, which every EDL's `captions.replace` takes; an object entry keeps its fields (`scope`, the context guards, `review`) as written.
- `RUN/20-analysis/sensitive.json` and `avoid.json`: every span to blur (gore, distress, nudity only) and every span to stay away from, merged, as the builders will need them.
- **The ad batches** (the user, 2026-11-14: the recorder skips nothing, and the agents leave the ad batches out when they analyse): the mining agents' `ad_batches` (each with a verdict `ad|stream|mixed`) merged by rec time into `RUN/20-analysis/ad-batches.json`
  (`T blocks` also lists the recorder's marker as `ad_batches`; compare: a marked batch no agent judged is yours to judge now, from its report and a sheet). Every span with verdict `ad` goes into `avoid.json` as `{"rec_from", "rec_to", "kind": "ad", "what": "..."}`, so
  every brief carries it as a span to stay away from. A `stream` verdict is not left out; a `mixed` one is left out only for the seconds that are the ad.

## 2. Long-form: the night as whole watches (streamer mode)
A long video is **one whole watch** (the user, 2026-11-14): whatever a viewer starts in it, a story, a bit, a game, a challenge, a conversation, ends inside the same video, so nobody has to open the next one to see how it finished.
When the content is there, a whole watch usually runs **about 25 minutes to an hour, set by the content and its context** (what is being said, who is on, what the stream is doing, where it changes), never by the clock and never "an hour, then whatever is left". The range says how a night that has plenty is split into watches; it is not a length to reach.
From a 7-hour stream the content may give three videos of 36, 43 and 27 minutes. It must not give one of an hour and a second of 46 minutes that opens with the end of a story the first one left half told (the viewer would have to watch the first 7 minutes of the second to finish what they were watching) when the stream could have been split into watches that are easier to finish.
A video shorter than 25 minutes or longer than an hour is right when that is how long its content is whole (all a night gave may be one great 20-minute stretch; a 70-minute challenge may not be cuttable without losing it). Length means the finished video, the dull parts already out: the plan estimates it from the spans.
**How much of the stream becomes videos is the agents' judgement and never a target** (the user, 2026-11-14): from an 8-hour stream they may make three hours of long videos if the analysis says so, or only 32 minutes in all, or nothing. There is no minimum in total and none per video, and no number of minutes to reach:
a stretch goes in because it is worth watching, never to fill a video or to get to a length, and a streamer who is not doing anything worth including (idle, away, repeating the same thing, talking to no one) is left out however long it lasts.
The agents weigh the factors themselves (what is said and done, how it lands with this audience, whether it stands on its own, how the chat and the internet react, the picture and the sound), with no formula (the chat's heat is a small nudge on a moment the rest already rates and a tie-break, `CC/reference/chat-heat.md`).

**What a long video is for, for this audience** (the user, 2026-11-20: an editor who knows the streamer and their audience, never a generic funny-moment detector or a summariser of the stream). Read the brief's long-video line first (`brief.md` part 3: how this streamer's long-form viewers watch: what their own channel and the clip channels post as long videos, how long, how much of a stream they keep, whether the full VOD is already up). It is evidence or a guess (its tag says which) and the plan treats it as what it is. A long video gives a viewer the stream without what gives them nothing: a plan that keeps nearly all of a stream says why this audience would watch nearly all of it, from that line, the moments and the acts; a guess is not a reason, and nothing is kept by default because it is there.
**The three kinds of footage** (`clip-economy.md`, long-form; the user, 2026-11-20) run through every act and span: what is worth watching, what a kept part needs to land, and what gives the viewer nothing. The plan keeps the first two and leaves out the third, judged for this streamer and this audience and never because a stretch is quiet; something new or odd that does not fit the streamer's usual kinds can still be the first kind. The block files show where each lies (the moments and their `needs_context`, the summary lines, the acts and their kinds, the richness line); the builder confirms it in the words.

Read the richness line across the whole stream (a score per chunk), not just the peaks, and the acts.
- **The acts** (`acts.json`): the stretches of the stream that are each one watchable thing from its start to its end (a game or a match from the lobby to the result, a guest segment, a story from its setup to its end, a challenge or a bet to its verdict, a topic the stream stayed on, a break or a dull stretch).
  A block file with no `acts` (a run made before the field existed, or a thin file) is read from its summary lines and its transcripts.
- Find **runs**: stretches of chunks that are good to watch as a whole (the stream on fire, a long guest segment, a challenge from start to end). Runs can be short or 20 chunks long.
- Find **pickups**: good moments between the runs (2 minutes from one chunk, 30 s from another) that belong in a video but do not make a run.
- **Set the borders between the videos from the acts, then see what lengths come out.** A border falls between acts (a pause, a scene change, a guest leaving, a game or a topic ending, a break, a hole in the recording, an ad batch, a dull stretch that is left out), never inside a story, a bit, a game, a challenge or a conversation.
  Acts that belong together (one guest, one game session, one topic, one mood) go into one video, so that each video is a whole watch (usually somewhere between 25 minutes and an hour when the content is there). Where the sums fall badly (an hour and a remainder of 46 minutes that begins in the middle of something), move the borders or group the acts again until every video is a good whole.
  What is left over and too thin for a video of its own is left out, goes with an act it truly belongs to (never added just to lengthen a video) or becomes material for shorts; it is never a tail that ends in the middle of a story.
- **An act that alone runs past an hour** (a marathon game session, an all-night challenge) stays one video when it holds as a whole. If it has to be divided, only where one part is finished (a game won, a level done, a topic closed, a break the stream takes itself):
  each part ends on a resolved point and the next opens with its own setup (a bit of what it needs may be kept as a context span, `context-keeping.md`). Never at a place where the answer, the reveal, the result or the payoff lies on the other side.
- **Check every border by reading the words** (`T window` about two minutes on each side of it, and a frame): nothing is in progress across it: no story whose end is on the other side, no question not yet answered, no bet or game not yet decided, no "wait for it".
  Where something is, it goes whole into one video: the border moves to its start or to its end, whichever makes the better watches. The first and the last scene of every long video are read the same way: it does not open in the middle of something, and it does not end before its last story lands.
  A stretch of the stream is in ONE long video only: a short may reuse a moment, and a bit kept as a context span may come from a stretch another video holds (`context-keeping.md`); otherwise two long videos never overlap.
- Decide **how many videos and how much of the stream in all**: as many whole watches as the content earns, and no more. If it is consistently hot for this creator's audience (they would watch it all) there can be many, hours of content in total; if it is warm, fewer and shorter ones that are all good; if only a few pockets are good, one video of half an hour, or none. The number is not the stream's length divided by an hour, and the total is not a share of the stream. Padding is a failure.
- **Read the set as a viewer would** (the user, 2026-11-20): every video gives a reason to watch it that the others do not; a beat that comes back in several (three robberies that go the same way, the same joke told again) is kept where it lands best and condensed or left out elsewhere; the strongest material gets the place it deserves (an opening, a climax), not a place buried in a middle.
- Decide **each video's form** from what the stream gave and how this audience watches (the user, 2026-11-20: "the recording itself should determine the most appropriate output"); no form is the default:
  - a **story episode**: a story, a mission line, a challenge, a match or a conversation that holds as one continuous watch, kept whole from its setup to its end with the third kind cut out of it;
  - a **condensed run**: one long event worth following whose middle is mostly routine: its turns, its climax and its end, with what the viewer needs between them, the routine cut;
  - a **best-of**: the strongest moments of a stretch or of the night that do not make one story, each with its own setup, joined into one watch (in time order unless another order is the better watch; a running bit can be its spine);
  - another form when it is the better watch (a theme video: "every guest moment").
  A night can give several forms or one. Whatever the form, every story in it is whole. A cold open of the best 20 to 40 s may start it.
  Where the recording has a gap (a chunk with `gap_before_s` or `ads_skipped_s` in the table), the join is part of the plan. An ad batch (verdict `ad`) is never part of a run, a span or a pickup: the stream on either side of it is a cut, not a continuation.
- **Dull parts and the context a bit of one may carry** (`CC/reference/context-keeping.md`): the parts with nothing to show are left out, as before. Where something that is kept leans on a bit of one, the planner may keep that bit as a **context span**; a moment's `needs_context`, the people and summary lines of the block files and the agents' `notes` are leads; where the stream says it, even in another block, is found with a plain text search of the transcripts (the file says how); it is checked in the words and a frame, and the data decides.
- List for each: `L01`, working title, its form and why, the ordered source spans (`rec_from`, `rec_to`, a line on why each is in), the expected length (an estimate of what plays: the spans without the third kind, never a goal) and why (the acts in it), its editorial notes (section 4), how it opens (the scene, and what a viewer needs to follow it) and how it ends (where its last story lands),
  the video before it and the one after it (id, and the rec time where each ends and starts), the chapter list, the sensitive and avoid spans inside, the guests it features.

## 3. Shorts: which moments, how many
**The user's level for shorts** (`T state --run RUN get shorts_level`, asked at the start whenever shorts are made; the user, 2026-11-14):
- **`concentrated`**: only the one, two or three best shorts: the most highlighted pieces of the content, the ones with the most potential. Read all the candidates, then keep only those; each gets the most care. Never more than three.
- **`normal`**: about 5 to 10 shorts, the best moments spread across the night. Never more than ten.
The level is the range the user chose, never a quota: when the stream has fewer moments worth a short than the level would make (a quiet night under `normal`; only one real highlight under `concentrated`), the planner makes fewer, and none when none is worth it.
Nothing is made to reach a number: the best are chosen first, and a moment that is not good enough is not added to fill the range. A run made before the question existed has no level: the planner judges the count as before.
- Start from the moments with the highest shareability and standalone scores that fit this streamer's criteria, then read them again as a clip editor: does the first 2 seconds hook? is the payoff clean?
- **Chat heat** (the user, 2026-11-15): among moments of about the same worth, the one the audience reacted to harder goes first (the miners' `heat`, already worth at most a point inside their `shareability`); it never puts a lesser moment ahead of a clearly better one, a burst a miner looked at and rejected (`heat_review`) is not rescued by it, and a moment with no burst loses nothing.
- One moment can make one short; two or three small beats about one thing can make one short together (a "3 times chat was right" cut); a strong moment can also be cut twice with different hooks when both are good.
- Do not make five shorts that are the same joke. Do not make a short of a moment that needs 40 s of setup to deserve 15 s of payoff.
- Spread: under `normal`, variety across the night and across the kinds of moment that travel for this streamer; under `concentrated` the best come first and variety matters less; the best 3 get the most care.
- Count: set by the level (`concentrated`: up to 3; `normal`: about 5 to 10) and never more than are good: a hot night fills its range with the best moments, a quiet one gives fewer than the range, and there is no minimum. A run with no level (made before the question existed): as many as are good.
- A moment can be in a short and in a long video; that is normal.
- List for each: `S01`, working title, the source spans, the expected length (up to 3 min; the length follows the content), the hook idea (what the first 2 s are), the payoff, what to blur (spans from `sensitive.json`),
  what to stay away from, the SFX or zoom ideas if any (optional), the posting title and hashtags idea.

## 4. Write the briefs (one file per video) and the plan
`RUN/40-briefs/<id>-brief.md` (about a page): the goal in a sentence; who it is for; the sources (spans with rec times and a line each; a context span is marked as one, with what it gives and what needs it); constraints (length, the sensitive spans with what to blur, the avoid spans, the lexicon,
the people and their spelling); what the editor should try (a hook, an order, a cold open, a callback); for a streamer-mode long video also how it opens and how it ends and the videos before and after it (where each stops and starts), so its builder can check its own borders, and its **editorial notes** (the user, 2026-11-20), a few lines a builder can act on and never a cut list: what the video is (its form, and why it suits this stream and this audience); why it is worth watching (the streamer's kinds in it, from the brief and the criteria); what each span gives (its moments, its story, what it needs); where the third kind is likely (from the block files; the builder confirms it in the words); how it differs from the other videos of the set (a game-mode brief says which match and game it is: `cc-plan-game`); the title and hashtags for `posting.md`.
`RUN/20-analysis/portfolio.md`: the table of videos (id, kind, working title, expected length, spans, why it exists; for a streamer-mode long video also the acts in it and its two borders; game mode: also its match and game), the count of each kind, and the plan for the order of building (best first). Record the ids in
`state.json -> videos` (`cc.py state --run RUN set videos.S01={...}`).
The planner does not cut. A production agent gets a brief and decides the exact cuts, zooms, sounds and captions itself.

## 5. Agents for the build
One production agent per video, at most 4 at the same time (`config.json -> agents.produce_max`), the best videos first. Each is a different CapCut project, a different folder under `60-outputs`, and shares nothing but the read-only chunks.
