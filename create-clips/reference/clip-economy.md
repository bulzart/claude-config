# Clip economy: what makes a streamer moment travel (the craft sheet every agent reads)

Think like someone who has farmed clips for years: the job is to find the 30 seconds a stranger would send to a friend, and to package it so a thumb stops for it.
Everything below is a starting point; the streamer's own context brief (`20-analysis/brief.md`) overrides it where they differ.

## What travels (moment types; adapt the list to this streamer)
1. **Reaction explosion**: shock, scream, disbelief, a sudden silence, "NO WAY" (a reveal, a jump scare, a big win or loss, a surprise guest).
2. **A bit that lands**: banter, a roast, a running joke paying off, wordplay, an absurd answer, an awkward pause that gets worse.
3. **Chaos and fails**: something breaks, goes wrong, goes right by accident; a streamer caught off guard.
4. **Guest chemistry**: first meeting, a guest saying the unexpected thing, two personalities clashing or clicking, someone getting out-talked.
5. **Rage and meltdown**: used with care, the point is the human moment, not the shouting.
6. **Wholesome**: a fan moment, a donation, an emotional line, a streamer being kind.
7. **Skill and clutch**: the insane play, a world first, a trick, a comeback.
8. **Challenge or bet payoff**: the dare, the punishment, the verdict.
9. **Storytime**: a story with a turn (needs its setup; the setup is part of the clip).
10. **Moment-in-time**: the release, the leak, the trailer reaction, the record, the news everyone is talking about this week (these expire: speed matters).
11. **Chat as a character**: chat spamming one word, chat being right, a streamer reading chat out.
Chat reaction is a signal: a spike in messages per second right after something happened means the audience felt it (see `chat_per_s` in the sheets and `stream.md`).
Nothing is a moment because a script says so; it is a moment because you read the words, saw the frames and felt the turn.

## A short (Shorts, TikTok, Reels) is a promise kept fast
- **Length follows the moment**: 15 to 45 s is the sweet spot, 45 to 90 s for a story with a turn, up to 3 min (the hard cap) only when several beats keep paying off. Shorter is not better; "no dead second" is better.
- **The first 2 seconds are the clip**: open on the most intriguing line or reaction, even if it comes from the middle (a cold open can be a 1 to 3 s teaser of the payoff, then the setup, then the payoff in full). A greeting, a "so", a pause or a title card never opens a clip.
- **Setup only as long as the payoff needs**: a viewer who has never seen the stream must understand it. If it needs 12 s of setup, cut the setup tight (drop filler words, keep the question and the reaction), or choose another moment.
- **Payoff, then stop**: end 0.3 to 1 s after the laugh, the word or the reaction lands. Do not trail off. A line that loops back to the start is a plus.
- **Cuts**: on the breath between words, never inside a word; cut dead air, "um", the streamer reading a long chat message nobody needs, AFK. Jump cuts every 2 to 5 s keep energy but never cut away from a reaction that is the joke.
- **Captions on every second of speech** (when the run's captions setting is on, the default; the user can pick no captions at the start, then none), bold, 3 words at a time, inside the safe area, in the default look the word being spoken on a rounded colour box that moves word to word (the Loupe look, `edit-craft.md`), the size small to normal (about 4.6 percent of the picture's smaller side, `size: "auto"`) because most viewers are on a phone or a tablet.
- **9:16, safe area**: keep the action and captions between roughly 12% from the top and 22% from the bottom (the apps draw their buttons there). Captions sit around 60 to 70% of the height, not over the face.
- **Sound effects are seasoning**: one per big beat at most (an impact on the reveal, a record scratch on the wrong answer, a whoosh on a zoom). Never over speech; never so many that the clip reads as spam.
- **Zoom is emphasis**: a quick punch-in (110 to 125%, 0.15 to 0.25 s in) on the line or the face that carries the joke. Not every cut.
- **Hook, context, payoff** is the shape; the posting title and the first caption word should not spoil the payoff.

## A long-form video (YouTube, streamer mode) is one whole watch of about 25 minutes to an hour
- The viewer wants the streamer's best parts without watching the whole stream, in watches they can finish: a video holds the acts that belong together, each from its start to its end (the planner's rule: `portfolio.md` section 2). Chronological order is fine and honest; a cold open of the best 20 to 40 s sets the tone.
  (Game mode's long videos are condensed matches, one per map or game, and their length follows the kept rounds or fights: `game-clipping.md`.)
- Cut what the live audience needed and the replay viewer does not: long silences, technical trouble, repeated reading of the same chat, a long AFK. Keep the rhythm of the stream.
- **Never cut a story, a bit or a game moment in half, inside a video or between two**: what a viewer starts in a video ends in it, and the next video never opens with the end of something this one began.
  The length (about 25 minutes to an hour) is a ballpark that follows the content, not a wall: finish the scene even if the video runs a little over an hour, stop earlier when what follows is weak, never pad.
- Straight stretches are normal (20 chunks in a row when the stream is on fire); a mix of short pickups and long runs is normal too. The segment list is the agent's call.
- Chapters: write timestamps for the places a viewer would jump to. Transitions are plain cuts or very short ones; the content does the work.
- A long video earns its length from richness: if the stream was only warm, a 30 to 40 minute video that is all good beats a padded hour. If it was hot all night, several whole watches, split where the stream's own acts end (36, 43 and 27 minutes from a 7-hour stream, not an hour and a leftover that starts in the middle of a story).

## Posting notes (each video gets them in `posting.md`)
- Title: curiosity or shock without spoiling the payoff; the streamer's name and the topic where it helps search; no clickbait that the clip does not deliver.
- Description: one line of what happens, credit to the streamer and the channel (`twitch.tv/<channel>`), 3 to 5 hashtags (the streamer, the topic, one format tag).
- Several shorts from one moment (different hooks) are fine; the same clip twice is not.
- Each platform wants its own upload; one 9:16 file serves Shorts, TikTok and Reels.

## What to avoid
- Religion and superstition (never in what a clip says or shows: choose another moment or cut around it), see `safety.md`.
- A moment that needs knowledge the viewer does not have and cannot get in 5 s.
- Spoiling your own punchline in the first caption or the title.
- Music the streamer is playing that is commercial (it can get a clip muted or claimed): cut around it or keep it out of the clip.
- (Another person's private information on screen is NOT blurred and not avoided: only gore, distress and nudity are ever blurred, `safety.md`.)
- Taking one line out of context so that it means the opposite of what was said (a cut must keep the meaning).
