### 2.16 Posture, support and place

**Rule (derive, then check).** A placed or carried product takes the posture its own shape and weight allow, on a support the scene visibly offers, at a place where a person would really leave it. Reason in that order: the product's details (a thin flat sachet or pouch lies flat, label up; a small flat carton also lies flat on a bare surface and stands on its base only for a reason the plan states; a can stands on its base; a stand-up pouch stands), the support the source frame shows (a fixed natural surface such as a counter, a desk or a grill's side shelf), and the people nearby (clear of a working hand or arm). What cannot hold in a real scene is a defect for any product: a lean against something the model has to invent, a stance on its edge against a person, a rest on a thin sheet that hands move or write on, a prop that is carried away, and a placed product taking a beat of the source that belongs to another object. These are defaults for a placed product with no stated reason otherwise; the source and the plan's reason decide (`generation-system.md` §6), so a pack the source shows held upright, or set on a lid by the story, is not a break.

**Breaks.** The model has nothing real to rest the pack on and invents a lean or a stance against a person; two surfaces fight for one space (a carton on an exam sheet's corner doubles and warps the sheet while the hand writes); the pack lands in a working hand's lane and the hand covers it or goes through it; a free strip is narrower than the pack; the pack is planned flat and renders upright or propped. Cases: CE-006, CE-007, CE-012, CE-013, CE-021, CE-037, CE-041, CE-059, CE-060, CE-061, CE-072, CE-076, CE-084, CE-087, CE-092.

**Ledger.** The anchor row carries the posture (flat / on its base / in hand), the support (bare surface or
a named solid object that is in frame), the clearance to the nearest working hand, arm or sheet, and the
story reason in one clause ("set down at the near edge, out of the writing hand's way").

**Prompt.** Posture, support and reason as one chronological sentence, positive first: the posture on the face it rests on, the label direction, the visible surface it rests on and where, the clearance to the nearest working hand, and the story reason in a clause; then at most the one exclusion for this product's own wrong posture, in the model's syntax (verified, CE-012). A support is bare surface or a large solid object the source frame shows. Name the object that is picked up and say the product stays where it was. Write this video's own nouns.

**QC.** At 30 fps across every rest and every pass of a hand or sheet: the posture (flat or on its base, no
tilt, no float), the support (the whole underside on a visible surface or solid, with its contact shadow),
the clearance to a working hand or arm (touching is a regenerate reason; a gap under about half a
hand-width is a note, CE-012's accepted take had about 0.2), the sheet region for the whole shot when paper
is near, and the story reason written as its own line of the verdict.
- **Regenerate** on a lean against nothing, a posture the shape cannot take, a pack touching a working hand
  or arm, a doubled or warped sheet, or a source beat taken over. No post-fix.
- **Hand lanes:** each hand's path over the whole clip (resting, writing, page-turning, pick-up sweeps) stays
  clear of the pack; a hand that covers, wraps or passes through it, or a teleport, is a regenerate reason
  (CE-072, CE-076).
- **Retry:** re-plan the posture and the place (flat on bare near-side surface, on the fixed natural surface
  the source shows, or in hand; a spot that passes `generation-system.md` §4b.1c) and drop any prop the model
  has to invent or that moves; a size-and-place guide that shows the pack in its posture and place
  (`generation-system.md` §4b.6) is the lever for posture and size; the sizing words are not (§2.7).
