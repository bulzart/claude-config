## 5. Niche-bias control (the references' product niche must not decide the answer)

References usually come from one corner of the market. Left alone, three biases push the answer back into it: the **pool** (candidates are brainstormed from what the videos show), the **scoring** (a familiar category feels safer and more proven) and the **selection** (a bigger pool of one category fills the top three by sheer count). The controls below are **duties of the agents** (the main session and the fresh niche-audit, blind scoring and red-team agents). Each is applied from a rule of thumb in `config.json → viral_browser.rules_of_thumb`, carried out, and written in the bias log (item 11). No script enforces, refuses, caps, ranks or computes any of them: the toolkit only records and tabulates what the agents decide (`niches`, `candidates`, `evidence`, `viral-stats`). A number may be departed from when the evidence says so, with the departure and its reason written beside it.

1. **Layer separation (§3).** The mechanism is collected; the product is not. Candidates come from the mechanism's *transplant needs*, never from "more of what the videos show".
2. **Mechanism-first generation (§6).** The long list is built from mechanisms, problem scans and market feeds across many categories. The references' own products enter as **one source among five**, tagged `origin: reference`.
3. **Niches are data, decided by agents and recorded by the toolkit.** The controls count niches, so one niche must not hide behind several names (a plural, a synonym, a broader or a narrower phrase).
   - *Who decides.* The agents. The main session starts from `T niches propose` (every label in use, with its count, where it is used and the niche it is recorded under) and `T niches list` (each niche, its member labels, how many cards and candidates sit in it, the labels still unmapped, the audits on record) and records its decisions with `T niches add` / `map` / `merge`, each with `--by` and `--reason`. A label that another niche held moves to the new one and the record says so; `merge --from … --to …` folds the members into the target and renames the niche on the candidates. The toolkit files a label under the niche recorded for it (labels are matched after case-folding and Unicode normalisation; combining marks are kept, so two different words of an Indic script stay two), files it as written when none is recorded, and says so. It warns of no look-alike name, shared word or recurring word, and blocks nothing.
   - *The safeguard.* A **fresh niche-audit agent** (§13.5) reads the vocabulary, the labels in use and the candidates (names, types, niches) and judges, pair by pair, whether a shopper, a supplier search or a marketplace category would treat two niches as the same place to look for the product. Two names that share no word can be one niche, and a niche can be so broad that it hides different buyers and uses. It writes `30-research/niche-audit.md` (one line per niche; the merges it proposes; the pairs that look alike but are distinct, with a reason; the niches that are too broad) and changes nothing.
   - *Afterwards.* The main session reads the report, applies the merges it accepts (`T niches merge --from … --to … --reason "…" --by main`), runs `T viral-stats` again when a merge touched a card label, and **records the audit** with `T niches audit --by "niche-audit agent <id>" --result "<merges applied, pairs kept distinct and why, or none needed>" --report 30-research/niche-audit.md` (the record keeps who audited, which niches the audit reviewed, what it found and what was applied; it is not a gate and nothing checks the report). It does not start research (item 4) before a fresh agent has audited the vocabulary, has another audit done when niches were added or merged afterwards, and writes in the bias log what the audit found and what was applied or kept apart. Every merge, audit and niche change after research began sits, with its `--by` and `--reason`, in the history of the vocabulary file or of the candidate, so a split is a visible, recorded decision and never an accident.
4. **Pool balance, by the main session.** Rules of thumb (`min_categories_in_pool`, `max_pool_share_per_category`): the long list spans at least 6 niches, no niche is more than 40 % of it, and at most half of it comes from the references' own products (`origin: reference`); the shortlist that is actually researched stays broad too (6 candidates span at least 3 niches, 9 at least 5, 12 at least 6).
   - The main session reads `T candidates summary --run RUN [--exclude-eliminated] [--ids …]` (the candidates counted by niche, type, origin and status) on the long list, and again on the exact shortlist before research starts. It applies the numbers with judgement and repairs a thin or lopsided set by adding candidates from other niches, never by dropping the dominant niche's candidates. It writes in the bias log what it checked, what it repaired and any deliberate exception with its reason.
   - Research starts with `T candidates set --run RUN --ids C01,C02,… --status researching --by main --reason "<why these; what the balance check showed>"`. Statuses are a vocabulary the agent uses, not a gate: `long`, `shortlisted`, `researching`, `researched`, `eliminated`, `picked`, `runner-up`, `watch`; `set` changes any field and any status from any status and keeps `--by` and `--reason` in the candidate's `history` (an eliminated candidate keeps its reason as `eliminated_by`).
   - Nothing in the toolkit blocks research on a thin set, an unaudited vocabulary or missing statistics, and nothing blocks a change to a candidate after research began (`candidates set --name | --type | --definition | --category`): the main session does not start without them, keeps such changes rare and mentions them in the bias log. If the data alone should decide, it may relax the numbers and says so.
5. **Origin-blind evidence and scoring, by a fresh agent.** The ledger knows each candidate's origin; **dossiers and scoring never mention it**, and research agents are not told where a candidate came from. The main session built the pool and read the cards, so **it does not score**: a **fresh scoring agent** (§13.4) receives only `T candidates show --blind`, the evidence, the dossiers, the brief and the essence and fit profile (for `fit` only), and records its ratings with `T candidates rate`. Reference-derived rows show to it as "not independent", and it does not lean on them for the independent criteria (item 6).
6. **Independent, directional evidence, applied by the scoring agent.** The references prove nothing about the market. Evidence rows carry a **signal** (positive, negative, mixed, neutral: which way the fact points for selling the product in that lane) and an independence flag, as the researching agent stated them; the ledger checks the form (the required fields, and a URL behind an independent grade A or B row) and tabulates (`T evidence list --counts`: rows by lane, signal, grade, independence and distinct sites) but weighs nothing. Rules of thumb for the **blind scoring agent** (`independent_criteria`, `cap_without_independent_evidence`, `contradicted_cap`):
   - a rating on demand, commercial, competition, economics or compliance with **no independent grade A or B row** bearing on it (of any direction) should not be above 2;
   - a 4 or a 5 needs a **positive** independent A or B row, and so does a 3 on `compliance` (route and cost known, §7.4); a 3 on the other criteria can rest on a flat or mixed A or B reading or on a single source (anchor 3 in §8.1);
   - for `economics` the calculation that `T econ --cand` registers is grade C and gives the contribution, but the rating also needs a grade A or B row for a sourced input behind it (a supplier price, a fee schedule, a freight or duty source, for the same variant as the price);
   - a criterion whose negative rows match or outnumber the positive ones should not be above 3 when the agent would otherwise give a 4 or 5.
   The agent applies these with judgement, may depart from one when the evidence says so, and writes in its note which rule it applied or set aside and why. No script caps a rating: `T candidates rate` records the integer 1-5 as given and prints the criteria not yet rated.
7. **Gates and floors (§8).** The main session applies the six gates of thumb of §8.3 when it chooses the picks, reading `T candidates table` and the evidence: positive independent grade A or B evidence from at least two distinct sources across the demand, commercial and competition lanes (one of them commercial), a supported compliance status, a calculated economics row with a sourced grade A or B input row behind it, and a rating of at least 3 on demand, commercial, economics, compliance and fit **after the evidence rules of item 6**. It writes the failed gate and the reason for every candidate it sets aside. A product whose rows say demand fell, nothing sells and the price war has begun cannot be carried by high ratings elsewhere.
8. **Per-niche and per-type limits.** At most `max_picks_per_category` (2) picks share one niche, and no two picks share a product type (the `type` an agent assigned; whether two types are the same kind of product is the main session's judgement, not a word match). A third pick from the same niche goes to the next-best candidate of another and appears among the runner-ups as "category cap". These limits are a deliberate diversification safeguard on the three-place table, not an exclusion of any niche; the main session may relax them with the reason written in the bias log, and the user can edit the number in the config.
9. **The defence note and the red team come before the picks are final.** Every pick from a dominant niche (rule of thumb: a niche that is at least 40 % of the references' cards; there can be two), and every pick that the main session judges to be on the same shelf as a dominant niche although it is filed under another name, carries a written defence: its independent evidence side by side with the best outside-niche alternative (independent ratings, evidence rows, contribution margin, saturation) and why it still wins (`T candidates set --id C## --defence "…" --by main --reason "…"`). Every pick carries a red-team reading (`--redteam survives|weakened|fails`) from the fresh red-team agent (§7.6). The main session writes both before it records a pick as final, so a defence can still demote a pick; no command checks that they exist, and the bias log says which pick has which defence and reading. Before it records the picks it also reads for itself that the corpus statistics are current (`viral-stats` run after the last vocabulary change, every reference card with a category) and that no niche hides under two names (item 3).
10. **Reference-gravity tests (what-ifs).** The main session reads the table twice more and writes the results in the bias log: (a) with every evidence row that is not independent set aside (`T evidence list --md` shows the flag, `--counts` tabulates the independent rows): does the order of the candidates change? (the independence rules, not the references, should decide); and (b) with the `fit` criterion, which is derived from the references, left out of the weighted sum (`T candidates table --exclude-criteria fit`). If either would change the picks, say so in the bias log and the report. No script reports whether the picks changed.
11. **Transparency.** The main session writes the bias log into `30-research/ranking.md`: the niche-concentration line with the figures it read, the pool and shortlist checks and repairs, each rule of thumb applied and each set aside with its reason, the niche vocabulary with its merges and audit (who audited, what was found, what was applied), the limits applied, any tie at the pick boundary and how it was broken, the what-if results, the defence notes and the red-team readings, every niche change after research and every other exception. The source for it is the toolkit's records: the `history` of each candidate in `candidates.json`, the history and the audits in `niche-vocabulary.json`, the retractions in the evidence ledger. The report prints it (§10.2 item 6).

**What bias control does not do:** it does not exclude the dominant niche. A product from the references' own niche that the market data backs stays eligible, may win, and may take two of the three places. The evidence rules demand *independent proof*, not a quota. The pool and pick limits (40 % of a pool, two picks per niche) are the only quota-like parts: they keep the long list broad and the three-place answer diversified, they never stop a data-backed product from competing, and the main session, or the user in the config (`max_pool_share_per_category`, `max_picks_per_category`), can relax them if the data alone should decide, with the reason written in the bias log.

**How the layers read (structure only; every label comes from the material, never from this file).** When most references show products of one niche, the product layer says "this niche sells". The mechanism layer says what each video does for the viewer: for each mechanism M#, what is seen, how fast, with or without sound, at what price perception, in how many of the videos (k of n) and how independent of the product it is. The transplant needs follow from them (a visible change within a few seconds, silent-friendly, an impulse price, a single-location demonstration). Candidates are then drawn by those needs across the market, in at least six unrelated categories. The references' own products compete with them on *independent* demand, commercial, competition and economics evidence only: one wins only if that evidence, not its presence in the references, beats the others; if it wins it stays, with its defence note; if a candidate from another niche wins, the answer was found by the mechanism.
