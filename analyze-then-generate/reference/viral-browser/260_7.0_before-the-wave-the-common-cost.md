### 7.0 Before the wave: the common cost basis and the shared inputs

Parallel research agents answer the same economic question for different products. If each chooses its own fulfilment, return-rate and fee inputs, the results cannot be compared, and a candidate can look viable only because its agent assumed a cheaper parcel (one agent's cheaper fulfilment figure can turn a failing candidate into the only one that passes). So, before the screen (§6) and before the wave, the main session (or one short agent) writes **`30-research/cost-basis.md`**: the inputs that do not depend on the product, each with its source row and a note on which ones are the agent's assumption:

- the **postage per order by weight class**, from a carrier's own price list (US and the lead country), plus pick, pack and materials = the **fulfilment per order**, and the **shipping policy** of the store (§2): free (the seller pays the postage: the default) or charged (`T econ --shipping-charge`, the amount the customer pays, the same for every candidate);
- the **return rate** and the **cost of a returned order** (postage back plus handling) per market, and whether returned units are resold (default: no);
- the **card and platform fees** of the channel's real fee schedule (US and EU), the **exchange rate** with its source and date, and the **tariff regime** of the market for the origin (the ordinary rate and every special duty that can apply to that origin and product class, such as special-origin lists, metal-derivative headings, forced-labour measures and anti-dumping duties, their stacking and the date each applies from; the US has several) with the guess it rests on stated (one rule for every candidate when a product's list membership cannot be verified, for example the highest rate that could apply, with the lower case shown as a what-if: agents that each guess a different list make the margins incomparable);
- the **freight tables** for the origin lane, the **brokerage, port and inland cost per shipment**, the **parcel weight classes** beyond the basis parcel (a heavier product costs more postage and a larger return: say which class a product falls in and adjust only with a sourced rate), the **fixed yearly regulatory costs** per market (a device registration, a representative's retainer: a yearly amount and the volume it is spread over, as `T econ --fixed-annual … --units-per-year …`) and the **calendar** (a factory holiday, a selling window).

Every `T econ` of the run uses these (`T landed` builds the landed cost from the supplier price tier, freight and duty on the same basis). Only the retail price the market supports, the landed cost, the MOQ and a per-unit allowance (`--extra-per-unit`) are the candidate's own. The sourced rows are registered **once**, under one candidate, and the research agents **copy** them to theirs (`T evidence copy --ids EV…,EV… --to C…,C… --by "<agent>"`: each copy says it is a copy and keeps the original's grade and source), so that the web budget goes to what is product-specific. The scripts hold none of these numbers; they are agents' decisions with sources. A run on another basis is a scenario and says so (`T econ --note`). If the results already exist when a difference is found, the main session reads the stored inputs of every candidate side by side, decides the common basis, re-runs `T econ` for every candidate and market (the stored result of a market is replaced) and withdraws the superseded calculation rows with a reason.
