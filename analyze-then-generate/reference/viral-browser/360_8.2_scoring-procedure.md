### 8.2 Scoring procedure

1. **A fresh scoring agent scores, not the main session** (§5 item 5; prompt §13.4). The main session built the pool, saw the origins and wrote the mechanism map, so it is the one reader that cannot be blind. Before the agent starts, the main session marks each candidate whose dossier and evidence are in as `researched` (`T candidates set --ids … --status researched --by main --reason "dossier and evidence in"`). The scoring agent receives `T candidates show --run RUN --id C## --blind`, the candidate's evidence (`T evidence list`), its dossier, `20-analysis/brief.md` (market, price band, exclusions) and `viral-essence.md` (for `fit` only). It never opens the cards, the mechanism map or the origin. The dossiers keep the numbers as the research agent wrote them: after a re-run of `T econ` (the common basis, the browser pass) the **stored `economics_by_market`** shown by `candidates show` is the authority, and the scoring brief says so (the main session never edits a dossier). When the candidates are rated by more than one scoring agent (a backfill wave has its own), the sums of the batches sit on two raters' scales: the revisit after the red team goes to **one** fresh agent across the near-misses of both batches, so that the criteria that decide are on one scale.
2. The agent records its scores with `T candidates rate --run RUN --id C## --ratings demand=4,commercial=3,fit=5,… --refs "demand=EV004+EV005 commercial=EV009" --confidence high|medium|low --note "<one line>" --by "scoring agent"`. The command records the integers as given and checks the form only (a known criterion, an integer from 1 to 5, evidence ids that exist for this candidate); it prints the criteria not yet rated and the plain weighted sum of the ones rated, and caps nothing. **The agent applies the rules of thumb for evidence itself** (§5 item 6): no higher than 2 on demand, commercial, competition, economics or compliance when no independent grade A or B row bears on the criterion; a 4 or 5 needs a positive independent A or B row, and so does a 3 on compliance (route and cost known, §7.4), while a 3 on the others can rest on a flat or mixed A or B reading or a single source (for economics the calculated estimate needs a sourced input row for the same variant too), and no higher than 3 when negative rows match the positive ones; it says in its note which rule it applied or set aside and why.
3. Set the gate fields from the evidence, with the reason: `T candidates set --id C## --price-fit yes|variant|no|unknown --compliance clear|resolved|open|unknown --excluded yes|no --by "scoring agent" --reason "…"` (`--compliance` also takes the agent's own words; the command echoes the fields it stored). The contribution is not typed: it comes from `T econ … --cand C##` (§7.3), one result per market. The rule of thumb is that **`clear` or `resolved` is backed by a positive independent grade A or B compliance row**, and that a `clear` that sits beside negative compliance rows is wrong: use `resolved` with the route, or `open`. The main session checks this when it applies G2 (§8.3); the toolkit does not. **The words:** `price-fit` `yes` = the market shows the product at a price inside the band at which the economics hold; `variant` = only a variant, a bundle or a higher scenario price would fit; `no` = no price inside the band works; `unknown` = no price was read in a named market (the reason says which). `compliance` `clear` = no regime beyond general product safety, backed by a positive row; `resolved` = a regime applies and its route and cost are established in every named market, backed by positive rows (§7.4); `open` = a regime or an obligation is identified but a route or a cost is missing, or negative rows are unresolved; `unknown` = the lane was not researched. The ledger stores the word and checks nothing else.
4. **Confidence:** high = two or more independent grade A rows in each of demand and commercial and a region match; medium = grade A or B rows in most lanes, a gap noted; low = thin or conflicting evidence or unreachable key sources (a market gap alone caps the confidence at medium, §2; with thin evidence it is low).
