### 7.2 Depth

| | quick | standard | deep |
|---|---|---|---|
| candidates researched | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| web calls per candidate (a `WebSearch` or a `WebFetch` each) | 8 | 16 | 30 |
| trend checks on the references' formats (§4.8) | 3 | 6 | 12 |
| browser pages per candidate (§7.5, only when allowed; EU and global evidence mostly needs them) | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| reference pages (§3.6, only with a links file and Chrome) | 0 | 10 | 30 |
| backfill candidates (§7.1, one wave) | 3 | 5 | 6 |
| red-team calls per candidate (red-team targets) | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| trend checks per pick, for the blueprint (§9 item 7) | 2 | 4 | 8 |
| verification re-fetches (§10.4) | 12 | 12 | 12 |

A lane that settles a gate early (a prohibited product, a margin that cannot work) ends the candidate's research: the agent reports it, the main session marks the candidate eliminated (`T candidates set --id C## --status eliminated --by main --reason "<gate>: <what the lane found>"`), and the rest of the budget is spent nowhere. `WebSearch` modes: `standard` for lookups; `extended` only when a standard result is thin or stale, or for fresh prices and availability and for niche facts. A failed, blocked or redirected call counts against the budget, and one address is retried at most once. `WebFetch` returns a small model's answer to the prompt, not the page: ask it for exact quotes, treat the digits it gives as read-through, and confirm in the browser pass the numbers that carry a decision.
